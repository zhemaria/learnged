2. What to look for

  • Reading level. Can a student who reads with difficulty follow every sentence? Mark any word or sentence that is too hard.
  • Tone. Our students are adults. Does anything sound childish, or too academic?
  • Math accuracy. Numbers, words for numbers, rounding, and symbols.
  • US conventions. Anything that sounds translated or "not how we say it here."
  • Hints. Do the hints help students find their mistake without giving the answer away?
  • Use in class. Would you use this with your students? What is missing?
  • Accessibility. Try the pages on a phone and with the keyboard only.

3. How feedback works

All LearnGED activities follow the same idea: a mistake is information, not a failure.

  1. First wrong answer: the student gets a hint that points to the specific mistake (for example, "That is just the digit. Its place makes it worth more."). The wrong choice is marked and the student tries again.
  2. Second wrong answer: the page shows the solution, explained step by step.
  3. Praise is about the strategy, not about being smart: "You worked step by step," "You used the hint well."
  4. The score shows both: answers solved on the first try and answers solved after a hint.

The practice quiz works in two rounds: students check all their answers, read the hints, fix what they can, and check again. Then they see the solutions for anything still wrong.

4. Style rules

RuleExample
Short sentences: one idea per sentence. Most sentences under 15 words."You already use numbers every day. You read prices at the store."
Talk to the student as "you.""When you finish this lesson, you will be able to…"
Everyday words first; explain a math word the first time it appears."To compare means to decide which number is greater (bigger)…"
Real adult situations: pay, prices, miles, rent, populations."A city has 483,716 people."
Instructions start with a verb."Round 8,651 to the nearest thousand."
No idioms that do not travel well.Avoid: "a piece of cake," "ballpark figure."
Each lesson ends with a short "Key words" list.digit, place value, round…

7. Questions for you

  1. Is the reading level right for your lowest-level students? If not, which parts are hard?
  2. "About 4 hours" of work for this lesson: is that realistic?
  3. Should numbers in words use commas between the groups ("two hundred eight thousand, forty")? Some textbooks leave them out.
  4. The homework points to ThatQuiz. Is there another free site your students already use?
  5. Is "Self-check checklist" with "Got it · Getting there · Needs more practice" clear?
  6. Would a "read aloud" button help your students?
