1

Compelling, supporting, or neither?

›

Use the five-second test. If a quick search answers it and everyone agrees, it is supporting. If informed people could defend different answers with evidence, it is compelling. If only taste decides, it is neither.

2

Which dimension of the arc?

›

Ask: is the person deciding what to ask (1), choosing how to look at the problem (2), judging sources (3), or presenting and acting (4)?

3

Which source answers it?

›

A good supporting question lets you name its source before you look. Choose the source that answers it best.

Build an inquiry

›

Read the compelling question. Then decide, for each question under it, whether it helps answer it, assumes the answer, or is off topic. If something is not right, the questions marked in yellow get a hint.
