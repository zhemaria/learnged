1

False or biased?

›

A false source states facts that did not happen. A biased source tells true facts, but not all of them, with words that push. Some sources are neither.

↑ Back to contents
2

The inventory of what is missing

›

Read the two reports. Then decide where each fact appears: only in A, only in B, or in both. If something is not right, the facts marked in yellow get a hint.

↑ Back to contents
3

Name the signal

›

The four signals: a judging word in place of a fact, who is named and who is not, a claim with no source, and the headline as a frame.

↑ Back to contents
4

Say it neutrally

›

Each sentence uses loaded language. Choose the version that tells the same event without judging it.

↑ Back to contents
5

Fact, opinion, or reasoned judgment?

›

A fact can be checked. An opinion is a preference. A reasoned judgment is a conclusion with evidence behind it.
