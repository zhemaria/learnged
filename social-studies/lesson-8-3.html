What the aligned codes mean:

  • R2 CCR Reading 2: find the central ideas and summarize them.
  • R3 CCR Reading 3: analyze how people, events, and ideas develop and interact.
  • R8 CCR Reading 8: evaluate the claims in a text and the evidence behind them.
  • R4 CCR Reading 4: interpret words and phrases, including technical meanings.
  • L4 CCR Language 4: work out the meaning of unknown words (context, roots).
  • R7 CCR Reading 7: combine information from text, visuals, and numbers.
  • R9 CCR Reading 9: compare how two texts treat the same topic.
  • 7.RP Math, ratios and proportions (grade 7): rates, percents, and proportional relationships.
  • 3.MD Math, measurement and data (grade 3): picture and bar graphs.
  • 8.SP Math, statistics (grade 8): patterns in two-variable data.
  • S-ID Statistics: interpret categorical and quantitative data.
  • R1 CCR Reading 1: read closely and cite specific evidence from the text.

DOK (depth of knowledge): 1 = recall, 2 = use a skill or concept, 3 = reason with evidence. Before you start

Lesson 8.1 trained scenarios under a clock and 8.2 asked you to investigate on your own. This last lesson is about the test itself: its five item types, how to spread seventy minutes, a full timed practice test, what to do with its report, and the Illinois Constitution requirement for the diploma.

1The five item types

This lesson teaches no new content. It teaches the test: how it is built, how to answer it, and what to do with the result. Twenty-three lessons of content and skills and a project are behind you. One thing is left: checking that all of it works with a clock running and nobody to help.

The test is on a computer and not every question is multiple choice. There are five ways to answer, and it pays to have used them before so you do not lose time working out how they work.

The five item types and what to do with each one
Item typeHow you answerWhat to know
Multiple choiceChoose one of four options.The most common. Read all four before deciding: the second-best is usually there on purpose, and it usually differs by an absolute.
Drop-down menuA list opens inside a sentence; choose the term that completes it.Read the whole sentence with each option inside it, not the list alone. Many options are real terms that do not fit that sentence.
Drag and dropMove items into boxes: sort, put in time order, match.Place the ones you are sure of first and leave the doubtful ones for last. The boxes already filled narrow down the rest.
Fill in the blankType the answer in a box, usually a word or a number.Watch the format: if a percent is asked for, type the number without the symbol unless told otherwise. Check the spelling of technical terms.
Hot spotClick a place on a map, a graph, or a picture.The strangest for those who have never seen it. Find the axes or the legend first, and then click.

What you are training

The two that surprise people most are the hot spot and drag and drop, because they look like nothing on a paper test. The computer skill is simple; what you train is not losing thirty seconds per question working out what to touch. Thirty seconds times ten questions is five minutes of the real test.

Look at the first row and draw the conclusion

Half the test is civics and government. If you could review only one block before test day, it would be Topics 3, 4, and 5: how the Constitution was made, what each branch does, and how rights are exercised. All of history is worth about seven questions; the three branches are worth a good deal more.

The three time rules, once more

If a question passes two minutes, mark it and move on. In a scenario, the second and third questions should take half the time of the first; if not, you are rereading. And at the end no box may be empty: with no penalty for a wrong answer, a guess has some chance and a blank has none.

3The practice test

This is the full test: 35 questions in 70 minutes, with the official share of each domain. It does not correct as you go. You answer it all, you can go back and change anything while time lasts, and the correction comes at the end, together with the report.

Before you press “Start,” three conditions

Seventy minutes in a row without getting up. No notes, not this page, not any lesson. And one attempt per session: if you interrupt it, the result no longer tells you what you need to know. It is uncomfortable on purpose, because test day will be too.

Open the timed practice test

At the end you get a report by domain and by practice, with an explanation of each mistake and the lesson where that content is studied. The calculator is on the page, as on the real test, where a TI-30XS emulator appears on the screen.

A warning about the result

A practice test measures how you did that day, not how much you know. A low score with three interruptions means nothing; a high one done with notes open means nothing either. The number is only worth something if the conditions were real. Before you look at the score, write down how you think you did and which domain you think you missed most. Then compare.

4What to do with the report

This is the part almost everyone skips, and it is what makes the practice test useful. A test that only gives a percent is a thermometer; one that says what to review is a plan. Mistakes are not all alike, and mixing them up leads you to study the wrong thing. There are four kinds.

The four kinds of mistakes and how to fix each one
KindWhat happenedHow it is fixed
ContentYou did not know the fact or the concept.Go back to the lesson the report points to.
SkillYou knew the content and misread the source, the graph, or the argument.In Topics 1 and 2 and Lesson 8.1, not in the topic of the domain.
Question wordingYou knew the answer and answered something else: you did not read what was asked.By reading the question twice, not by studying more.
TimeYou did not finish, or you answered fast and badly at the end.With the rules of section 2.

The distinction that changes your study plan

If you missed three questions from three different domains and all three were scenarios with several sources, your problem is not history, economics, or geography: it is Lesson 8.1. Studying more content would not fix it. That is why you read the report twice: once by domain, and once by what you were asked to do.

What to do depending on your practice test result
ResultWhat it meansWhat to do
Under 50%Content foundations are missing, not just technique.Review the whole topics the report points to, not just single lessons. Start with civics, which is half the test.
50% to 65%The content is there; applying it fails.Go back to the linked lessons, above all 8.1 and Topics 1 and 2. Repeat the practice test in a week.
65% to 75%Close. What is left is usually time and reading the question.Sort each mistake into the four kinds above. If wording and time dominate, do not study more: practice with a clock.
Over 75%Ready, with a margin.Review only what the report marks below that level, and take the official GED Ready® test, which predicts the real result best.

The most comfortable label

If almost all your mistakes came out “content,” reread the wording of two or three of them before you accept it. It is the most comfortable label (you just study more), and that is why people overuse it. Wording and time mistakes are fixed faster and raise the score more.

5The Illinois Constitution

Besides passing the four GED® tests, in Illinois you must meet the Constitution requirement to receive the State of Illinois High School Diploma. It is separate from the test, and you meet it by completing a free online lesson on the official portal. Read the four points below before you open the portal, which is at the end of the section. Two of them can save you hours.

First: you may not have to do it

Anyone who passed an approved high school equivalency test (GED® or HiSET®) before June 30, 2022 receives the diploma without this requirement, because it was suspended then. And anyone who already passed the Constitution test in high school, in an approved adult education program, or at an Illinois Regional Office of Education has met it: find that proof before you start the module. If you are unsure, ask your program.

Second: choose your language before you read a line

The portal opens in English, but it has a language selector at the top right, next to the globe icon. Choosing ESPAÑOL switches the whole lesson, questions included, into Spanish. Many people do the whole module in English without knowing they did not have to.

Third: the four steps
StepWhat you do
1 · RegisterYou need an account on the portal: “Log in” only works if you already have one.
2 · Complete the lessonAbout two hours. At the end it asks for information that connects your completion with your equivalency test record (GED® or HiSET®).
3 · Keep the certificatePrint or save the certificate you get at the end. It is your proof, so keep a digital copy too.
4 · Present itGive that certificate to the Regional Office of Education of the county where you live. In Cook County, including Chicago, that is the Cook County HSE Records Office. This step closes the requirement; without it, the other three do not count.

Fourth, the good news: you have already studied it

The structure of the Constitution, the three branches, federalism, the Bill of Rights, and the amendments are Topics 3, 4, and 5 of this course. What is specific to Illinois (the structure of the state government and the rights its constitution adds) is the only thing to look at separately, and the module itself teaches it.

Open the official portal: ilconstitution.org (opens in a new tab)

The recommended order
StepWhat
1Take the practice test of section 3 and work through its report.
2Review what the report points to and repeat the practice test.
3Take the official GED Ready® when you score over 75% on the practice test.
4Leave the Constitution module for after the test if time is tight: it is a requirement for the diploma, not for passing the test.

7Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises come in a new order every time. Keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Before the clock (25 minutes). Do the five activities of the practice page, one per item type, without watching the clock. Note which format cost you the most to handle. Check: this is a rehearsal of the machine. If the hot spot or drag and drop made you hesitate about how to answer and not what to answer, you have already gained something.
  • The full practice test (exactly 70 minutes). Find seventy clean minutes: phone silent, nothing else open, nobody to interrupt. Take the practice test in one sitting without looking anything up. Check: before you see the score, write down how you think you did and which domain you missed most. Then compare with the report.
  • Sort your mistakes (30 minutes). With the report in front of you, give each mistake one of the four labels of section 4: content, skill, question wording, or time. Count how many of each and write, in one sentence, what you will do next week. Check: if almost all came out “content,” reread two or three of the questions before you accept it.

➡️Coming next

Three things from this lesson. The five item types are rehearsed beforehand, because half a minute of hesitation per question is five minutes of the test. Half the test is civics and government: if you must review one block, it is Topics 3, 4, and 5. And a skill mistake is not fixed by studying more content, so read the report twice: once by domain, once by what you were asked to do.

And here the course ends. Twenty-four lessons, eight topics. If you keep only one thing from all of it, it would not be a date or an amendment. It would be a question, the one that has appeared in every topic in different disguises: how do you know, and how far does what you have let you go in saying it? You practiced it with two accounts of a Chicago City Council meeting in 1905, with a runaway notice and the autobiography of the same woman, with a voter registration graph from Louisiana, with two opinions from the same court, and with an average density that describes nobody. The test measures that. So does, every day, anything you are asked to believe.

Good luck. And when you pass, come back and tell us.

📘Key words

Item type
The way a question is answered: multiple choice, drop-down, drag and drop, fill in the blank, or hot spot.
Hot spot
An item answered by clicking a place on a map, graph, or picture.
Domain
A content area of the test: civics, history, economics, or geography.
Practice test
A full test under real conditions, to measure where you stand.
GED Ready®
The official practice test of GED Testing Service, the best predictor of the real result.
Regional Office of Education
The county office in Illinois that keeps high school equivalency records.
