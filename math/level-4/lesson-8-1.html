Lesson 8.1 · Quadratic functions: the parabola, the vertex, and the intercepts

Read the shape of a parabola, find its vertex, its axis, and its intercepts, and use the vertex to solve maximum and minimum problems.

⏱️ About 4 hours with practice and homework · take breaks 🧮 Calculator allowed

🎯What you will learn

When you finish this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Tell which way a parabola opens from the sign of a.
  • Find the vertex with x = −b/(2a) and the axis of symmetry.
  • Find the intercepts with the x-axis and the y-axis.
  • Use the vertex to solve maximum and minimum problems in context.

1The parabola and its shape

A link with Topic 7. In Topic 7 you classified quadratic functions by their pattern. Now you study them in depth: their graph (the parabola), their most important point (the vertex), and how they model maximum and minimum situations, such as the height of a ball or the profit of a business.

The graph of a quadratic function f(x) = ax² + bx + c is a parabola. The sign of a decides which way it opens:

How the sign of a decides the way the parabola opens
If…The parabola…The vertex is…
a > 0opens upa minimum
a < 0opens downa maximum

2The vertex and the axis of symmetry

The vertex is the highest or lowest point of the parabola. You find its x-coordinate with the formula, and its y-coordinate by evaluating the function. The axis of symmetry is the vertical line through the vertex.

x of the vertex = −b / (2a) · axis of symmetry: x = −b / (2a)

Example 1: find the vertex and the axis of f(x) = x² − 4x + 3.

1Here a = 1 and b = −4. x of the vertex: −(−4) / (2 · 1) = 4/2 = 2.

2The y of the vertex: f(2) = (2)² − 4(2) + 3 = 4 − 8 + 3 = −1.

✓Answer. Vertex (2, −1); axis of symmetry x = 2; since a > 0, it is a minimum.

3The intercepts with the axes

The intercepts are where the parabola crosses the axes:

How to find the intercepts of a parabola
InterceptHow to find it
With the y-axisEvaluate f(0) = c. It gives the point (0, c).
With the x-axisSolve ax² + bx + c = 0 (by factoring or with the quadratic formula of Topic 4).

↔ Slide the table sideways to see all the columns.

Example 2: the intercepts of f(x) = x² − 4x + 3.

1With y: f(0) = 3, so (0, 3).

2With x: x² − 4x + 3 = 0 → (x − 1)(x − 3) = 0 → x = 1 and x = 3, so (1, 0) and (3, 0).

A link with Topic 4

Finding the x-intercepts of a parabola is exactly solving a quadratic equation. The discriminant b² − 4ac tells you how many there are: positive, two; zero, one (the vertex touches the axis); negative, none.

4Maximum and minimum in context

The vertex solves real problems: the greatest height of a ball, the greatest profit of a business, the greatest area of a yard. If the parabola opens down, the vertex is the maximum; if it opens up, it is the minimum.

Example 3 (a ball): its height is h(t) = −16t² + 64t + 5 feet (t in seconds). When does it reach its greatest height, and what is it?

1Since a = −16 < 0, it opens down: the vertex is a maximum.

2t of the vertex: −64 / (2 · −16) = −64 / −32 = 2 seconds.

✓Greatest height: h(2) = −16(4) + 64(2) + 5 = −64 + 128 + 5 = 69 feet.

Example 4 (profit): the profit is P(x) = −2x² + 120x − 800 dollars. How many units give the greatest profit?

1x of the vertex: −120 / (2 · −2) = −120 / −4 = 30 units.

✓Greatest profit: P(30) = −2(900) + 120(30) − 800 = −1,800 + 3,600 − 800 = $1,000.

5Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. For your own work, with your notebook, graph paper, the calculator, and the GED formula sheet: (1) The ball and the profit: the height of a ball is h(t) = −16t² + 64t + 5 (in feet, with t in seconds). Graph it, find the greatest height with the vertex, and find when it hits the ground. Then find how many units x give the greatest profit P(x) = −2x² + 120x − 800 and what that profit is. (2) The four features: make a table with the direction of opening, the vertex, the axis of symmetry, and the intercepts of y = x² − 4x + 3, y = −x² + 6x − 5, y = 2x² + 8x, and y = x² + 2x + 5. One of them has no x-intercepts; explain how you know. (3) From the vertex to the answer: you have 40 feet of fence for a rectangular garden. Write the area as a function of the width, find the vertex, and say which measurements give the greatest area. Do it again if one side of the garden is against a wall and needs no fence. Then do several exercises of each activity on the practice page. About 70 minutes; check your work with the checklist of this lesson.
  • In your daily life. Throw a ball straight up and count the seconds until it comes back down. Use h(t) = −16t² + vt with that time to estimate how fast you threw it and how high it went.

📘Key words

parabola
The U-shaped graph of a quadratic function.
vertex
The highest or lowest point of a parabola; its x is −b/(2a).
axis of symmetry
The vertical line through the vertex: x = −b/(2a).
intercept
A point where the graph crosses an axis.
