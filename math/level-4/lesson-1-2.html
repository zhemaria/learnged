Lesson 1.2 · Slope: idea, formula, and meaning

Find the slope of a line from its graph and from two points, tell the four types apart, and read the slope as a rate of change.

  • Read the slope as a rate of change and compare two lines by how steep they are.

1What is slope?

A link with Lesson 1.1. In Lesson 1.1 you found the horizontal change (Δx) and the vertical change (Δy) between two points to get the distance. This lesson uses exactly those two changes, but now as a quotient: the slope measures how much a line goes up or down (Δy) for each unit it moves to the right (Δx). It is the idea that connects the geometry of the plane with the algebra of the line in Lesson 1.3.

The slope of a line, written with the letter m, measures how steep it is. It is the rise (how much it goes up or down, the vertical change) divided by the run (how far it moves to the right, the horizontal change):

m = rise ÷ run = Δy ÷ Δx

A key idea

The slope is the same all along a line. Whatever two points of the same line you choose, rise ÷ run always gives the same number.

2The slope from two points

When you know two points (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂), find the slope with the formula:

m = (y₂ − y₁) ÷ (x₂ − x₁)

Example 1: a positive slope. Find the slope of the line through (1, 2) and (4, 11).

1Rise (top). y₂ − y₁ = 11 − 2 = 9.

2Run (bottom). x₂ − x₁ = 4 − 1 = 3.

3Divide. m = 9 ÷ 3 = 3.

✓Answer. The slope is 3 (positive: the line goes up).

Example 2: a negative slope. Find the slope of the line through (0, 0) and (5, −10).

1Rise. −10 − 0 = −10.

2Run. 5 − 0 = 5.

3Divide. m = −10 ÷ 5 = −2.

✓Answer. The slope is −2 (negative: the line goes down).

Keep the same order

The point you subtract first on top must be the point you subtract first on the bottom. If you switch only one of the two, the sign of the slope comes out wrong. Rise goes on top: Δx ÷ Δy is the most common mistake.

3The four types of slope

The sign and the value of the slope describe the direction of the line. Always read lines from left to right.

Types of slope: what the line looks like and the sign or value of m
TypeWhat does the line look like?Value of m
PositiveIt goes up from left to right.m > 0
NegativeIt goes down from left to right.m < 0
ZeroHorizontal (flat).m = 0
UndefinedVertical.m does not exist (run = 0)

↔ Slide the table sideways to see all the columns.

Why is a vertical line undefined?

On a vertical line every point has the same x, so the run (x₂ − x₁) is 0. You cannot divide by 0, so the slope does not exist. On a horizontal line, the rise is 0, so m = 0 ÷ run = 0.

To compare two lines, the steeper one has the larger absolute value of the slope. For example, a line with m = −5 is steeper than one with m = 3, even though one goes down and the other goes up.

4The slope as a rate of change

Outside the plane, the slope takes on a very useful meaning: it is a rate of change, that is, how much one quantity changes for each unit of the other. The units of a slope are always “units of y per unit of x.”

Examples of the slope read as a rate of change in different situations
SituationThe slope is…
Distance traveled vs. timethe speed (miles per hour).
Pay vs. hours workedthe pay per hour (dollars per hour).
Total cost vs. items boughtthe price per item (dollars per item).
Water in a tank vs. timehow fast it fills or empties (gallons per minute).

Example 3: reading the slope. The graph of the distance a car travels (in miles) against time (in hours) is a line with slope 60. What does that slope mean?

1The units. Miles per hour.

2The meaning. A slope of 60 means that for every hour that goes by, the distance grows by 60 miles.

✓Answer. The car travels at a steady speed of 60 miles per hour.

The sign also has a meaning

A positive rate means the quantity grows; a negative one, that it shrinks. A tank that empties at −10 gallons per minute loses 10 gallons every minute: say it in words, not only with the sign.

5Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. For your own work, with a notebook and graph paper: (1) Six slopes from two points, two of them with negative coordinates. Write the rise and the run before you divide, and simplify the fraction. (2) Four lines drawn, one of each type (positive, negative, zero, and undefined). Explain in one sentence why each one has that slope. (3) Three different lines with slope 3/5 that go through different points. Write what they have in common and why. (4) Three situations from your daily life (a fee, a speed, a savings plan) written as a rate of change with units, and one sentence that explains each one. About 60 minutes; check your work with the checklist of this lesson.
  • In your daily life. Look at an electric, gas, or phone bill. Find the price per unit (per kWh, per therm, per GB) and write it as a slope: “dollars per ___.” If you used 100 more units, how much more would you pay? Then look for a road sign that warns about a steep grade (for example, “7% grade”): that percent is a slope too, 7 feet of rise for every 100 feet of run.

📘Key words

slope (m)
How steep a line is: rise ÷ run, Δy ÷ Δx.
rise
The vertical change between two points, y₂ − y₁.
run
The horizontal change between two points, x₂ − x₁.
undefined slope
The slope of a vertical line: the run is 0.
rate of change
How much one quantity changes for each unit of another, like miles per hour.
