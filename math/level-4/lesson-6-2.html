1The elimination method

A link with Lesson 6.1. In Lesson 6.1 you solved systems by substitution. Substitution is ideal when a variable is already alone; but when both equations are in the form ax + by = c, the elimination method is usually faster: add the equations so that one variable disappears.

The idea is to make the coefficients of one variable opposites (for example, +y and −y), so that when you add the equations that variable is eliminated. Sometimes they are already opposites; other times you multiply one or both equations first.

Example 1: coefficients already opposite. Solve x + y = 10 and x − y = 4.

1Add the two equations: (x + y) + (x − y) = 10 + 4.

2The y disappears: 2x = 14, so x = 7.

3Find y: 7 + y = 10, y = 3.

✓Solution. (7, 3).

Example 2: multiply one equation. Solve 2x + 3y = 13 and x − y = −1.

1Multiply the second by 3: 3x − 3y = −3.

2Add it to the first: (2x + 3y) + (3x − 3y) = 13 + (−3).

3The y disappears: 5x = 10, x = 2.

4Find y: 2 − y = −1, y = 3.

✓Solution. (2, 3).

Example 3: multiply both. Solve 2x + 3y = 7 and 3x + 2y = 8.

1To eliminate x: the first times 3 and the second times −2: 6x + 9y = 21; −6x − 4y = −16.

2Add: 5y = 5, y = 1.

3Find x: 2x + 3(1) = 7, x = 2.

✓Solution. (2, 1).

2Classifying systems

Not every system has exactly one solution. Depending on the two lines, there are three kinds:

How systems are classified by the number of solutions
KindLinesSolutionsWhen you solve, you get…
IndependentCross at one pointOne solutionx = … , y = …
InconsistentParallelNoneSomething false (like 0 = 5)
DependentThe same lineInfinitely manySomething always true (0 = 0)

The result tells you the kind

If both variables disappear and a false statement is left (like 0 = 5), the system is inconsistent; if a statement that is always true is left (0 = 0), it is dependent.

3Substitution or elimination?

Both methods lead to the same solution; what matters is choosing the faster one for each system.

When substitution is best and when elimination is best
Best methodWhen
SubstitutionWhen a variable is already alone or has a coefficient of 1 or −1.
EliminationWhen both equations are in the form ax + by = c and the coefficients line up easily.

4Application: mixtures, coins, and speeds

Example 4: coins. There are 12 coins, some nickels (5¢) and some dimes (10¢), worth 95¢ in all. How many of each are there?

1Let n be the number of nickels and d the number of dimes: n + d = 12 and 5n + 10d = 95.

2Divide the second by 5: n + 2d = 19.

3Subtract the first: d = 7. Then n = 5.

✓Answer. 5 nickels and 7 dimes.

Two kinds of equations

In coin, ticket, and mixture problems there is almost always an equation of amount (how many in all) and one of value (how much money in all).

5Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. For your own work, with your notebook open: (1) How many solutions does it have? Solve by elimination and classify each system as independent (one point), inconsistent (parallel), or dependent (the same line), using the result as your reason: x + y = 6 and x − y = 2; 2x + 3y = 12 and 2x − y = 4; 3x + 2y = 16 and x − 2y = 0; x + y = 4 and x + y = 9; 2x − y = 3 and 4x − 2y = 6. (2) Do I eliminate or substitute? Before solving, explain in two sentences which method is best: y = 3x − 1 and 2x + y = 9; 3x + 4y = 10 and 3x − 2y = 4; x = 2y and 5x − 3y = 14; 5x + 2y = 1 and 3x − 2y = 7. (3) Coins and mixtures: (a) you have 20 dimes and quarters worth $3.50; how many of each are there? (b) A store mixes coffee at $6 a pound with coffee at $10 a pound to get 20 pounds at $7 a pound; how many pounds of each does it use? Set up the system, solve it, and check. Then do several exercises of each activity on the practice page. About 60 minutes; check your work with the checklist of this lesson.
  • In your daily life. Empty a jar or a pocket of coins of two kinds. Count how many there are and how much they are worth, and ask someone at home to find how many of each there are with a system.

📘Key words

elimination
Adding the equations, after multiplying if needed, so that one variable disappears.
independent system
One solution: the lines cross at one point.
inconsistent system
No solution: the lines are parallel.
dependent system
Infinitely many solutions: both equations are the same line.
