Clearing the denominators with the LCD

›

Four moves: write down the excluded values, multiply both sides by the LCD of all the denominators, solve the equation that is left, and check the solution against the excluded values.

Multiplying by the LCD is not the same as adding fractions: here there is an equals sign, so you can multiply everything by the LCD and the denominators disappear. In a sum with no equals sign, the denominators stay.

2

Extraneous solutions: the checker

›

Multiplying by the LCD can produce a value that solves the equation without fractions but makes a denominator of the original equation zero. That value is an extraneous solution and is thrown out. The only way to catch it is to check.

An extraneous solution is not an arithmetic mistake. The algebra is right: multiplying by the LCD multiplied by something that is zero for that value of x, and that adds a solution the original equation did not have. If every solution is extraneous, the equation has no solution.

Practice: valid or extraneous?

3

Working together

›
1/a + 1/b = 1/t

If a person takes a hours to do a whole job, in one hour they do 1/a of it. That is the whole idea: add the parts each one does in one hour.

Common-sense check: together they always take less than the faster one alone. If the result is greater, the times were added instead of the rates.

4

Distance, speed, and time

›
time = distance / speed

Since the speed is in the denominator, as soon as the speed has a variable a rational equation appears. The classic case is a boat on a river or a plane in the wind: with the current the real speed is v + c, and against it, v − c.

Try making the current equal to the speed of the boat. The denominator v − c becomes zero and the upstream time stops existing: it is the excluded value of the first activity, told as a real story.
