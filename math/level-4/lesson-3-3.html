Math · Level 4 · Topic 3 — Multiplying and factoring

Lesson 3.3 · Factoring trinomials (a = 1 and a ≠ 1)

Factor trinomials by finding two numbers whose product and sum fit, use the ac method when a ≠ 1, and always factor completely.

⏱️ About 4 hours with practice and homework · take breaks ✏️ No calculator

🎯What you will learn

When you finish this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Factor x² + bx + c by finding two numbers whose product is c and whose sum is b.
  • Factor ax² + bx + c (a ≠ 1) with the ac method (product and sum).
  • Combine the common factor with factoring trinomials when needed.
  • Check every factorization by expanding.

1Trinomials with a = 1: two numbers

A link with Lesson 3.2. In Lesson 3.2 you factored by taking out the common factor and by recognizing a difference of squares. Now you factor trinomials (three terms), which are exactly what you get when you multiply two binomials with FOIL. To factor a trinomial is to ask: which two binomials, multiplied, give this trinomial?

To factor x² + bx + c, look for two numbers that do two things at the same time:

  • multiplied, they give c (the constant term);
  • added, they give b (the coefficient of x).

Those two numbers go in the binomials: x² + bx + c = (x + number₁)(x + number₂). The signs of c and b guide the search:

How the signs of c and b tell you the signs of the two numbers
Sign of cSign of bThe two numbers are…
positive (+)positive (+)both positive
positive (+)negative (−)both negative
negative (−)eitherone positive and one negative

Example 1: factor x² + 7x + 12.

1Two numbers with product 12 and sum 7.

2They are 3 and 4. 3 · 4 = 12 and 3 + 4 = 7.

3Write the binomials. (x + 3)(x + 4).

✓Answer. (x + 3)(x + 4).

Example 2: with a negative constant. Factor x² − x − 12.

1Two numbers with product −12 and sum −1.

2They are −4 and 3. (−4) · 3 = −12 and −4 + 3 = −1.

✓Answer. (x − 4)(x + 3).

2Trinomials with a ≠ 1: the ac method

When the coefficient of x² is not 1, use the ac method:

  1. Multiply a · c.
  2. Find two numbers whose product is ac and whose sum is b.
  3. Rewrite the middle term (bx) with those two numbers.
  4. Group two and two and take out the common factor of each group.

Example 3: factor 2x² + 7x + 3.

1a · c = 2 · 3 = 6.

2Two numbers with product 6 and sum 7: 6 and 1.

3Rewrite. 2x² + 6x + x + 3.

4Group. 2x(x + 3) + 1(x + 3) = (2x + 1)(x + 3).

✓Answer. (2x + 1)(x + 3).

Example 4: with signs. Factor 3x² − 10x + 8.

1a · c = 3 · 8 = 24.

2Two numbers with product 24 and sum −10: −6 and −4.

3Rewrite. 3x² − 6x − 4x + 8.

4Group. 3x(x − 2) − 4(x − 2) = (3x − 4)(x − 2).

✓Answer. (3x − 4)(x − 2).

The trick is in the middle term

The two numbers of the ac method “split” the term bx in two. When you group, the same binomial must appear in both groups; if it does not, check the signs.

Another road: multiply and divide by a

You can also multiply and divide by a, factor as if a were 1, and simplify. For 2x² + 7x + 3: [(2x)² + 7(2x) + 6] ÷ 2 = (2x + 6)(2x + 1) ÷ 2 = (x + 3)(2x + 1). The two numbers are the same ones (6 and 1), and the divisor always disappears when you simplify. If it does not, one of the numbers is wrong.

3Special cases and complete factoring

Some trinomials are perfect squares: they come from squaring a binomial. For example, 4x² − 12x + 9 = (2x − 3)², because (2x)² = 4x², 3² = 9 and 2 · 2x · 3 = 12x.

And, as always, before you factor a trinomial, take out the common factor if there is one. Some trinomials, such as x² + x + 1, cannot be factored with whole numbers: no two whole numbers multiply to 1 and add to 1.

Example 5: common factor first. Factor 2x² + 10x + 12 completely.

1Common factor 2. 2(x² + 5x + 6).

2Factor the trinomial. x² + 5x + 6 = (x + 2)(x + 3).

3The complete result. 2(x + 2)(x + 3).

✓Answer. 2(x + 2)(x + 3).

Always check by expanding

When you multiply the factors, including the common factor, you must get the original trinomial back. If something does not match, check the two numbers and the signs.

4Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. For your own work, with your notebook open and no calculator: (1) The factoring ladder, at your own pace. Level 1: x² + 7x + 12 and x² − 5x + 6. Level 2: x² − x − 12 and x² + 3x − 10. Level 3: 2x² + 7x + 3 and 3x² − 10x + 8. Level 4: 4x² − 12x + 9 (a perfect square). (2) Product and sum: for x² + 9x + 20, x² − 2x − 15, 2x² + 11x + 5, and 6x² + x − 2, first complete the sentence “two numbers that multiply to ___ and add to ___,” and only then write the binomials. (3) All the way: factor 2x² + 10x + 12, 3x² − 12x + 12, and 5x² − 5 completely, taking out the common factor first. Check each one by expanding and explain why the factorization is complete. Then do a round of each activity on the practice page, solving each exercise on paper before you type the answer. About 70 minutes; check your work with the checklist of this lesson.
  • In your daily life. Make a set of cards: on one side a trinomial (x² + 7x + 12, x² − x − 6, 2x² + 7x + 3, …), on the other its factors. Shuffle them and play with someone at home: one reads the factors and the other multiplies to find the trinomial.

📘Key words

trinomial
A polynomial with three terms, such as x² + 7x + 12.
ac method
Find two numbers with product a · c and sum b, split the middle term with them, and group.
grouping
Taking out the common factor of each pair of terms so that the same binomial appears.
perfect square trinomial
a² ± 2ab + b² = (a ± b)²
factored completely
When no factor can be factored any further.
