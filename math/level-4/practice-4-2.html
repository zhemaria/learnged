1

The visual idea: completing a square

›

x² + bx is drawn as a square with side x plus a rectangle of area bx. Cut the rectangle in half and put one half on each side of the square: only one corner is missing to form a big square. That corner measures b/2 by b/2, so its area is (b/2)².

If b is negative, the picture is the same with b/2 negative: x² − 6x + 9 = (x − 3)². The number you add, (b/2)², is always positive.

Practice: which binomial squared is it?

2

The five steps (when a = 1)

›

1. Leave the x terms on one side. 2. Add (b/2)² to both sides. 3. Write the left side as (x + b/2)². 4. Take the square root with ±. 5. Solve for x.

Do not forget the ±. The square root gives two results, the positive one and the negative one. Without the ± you lose a solution.

When a ≠ 1: divide first

›

The five steps work when the coefficient of x² is 1. If it is not, first divide the whole equation by a, including the constant.

5

The origin of the quadratic formula

›
x = (−b ± √(b² − 4ac)) / 2a

Completing the square in the general equation ax² + bx + c = 0, with letters, gives exactly the quadratic formula. It is not an isolated trick: it is the proof of where the formula of Lesson 4.3 comes from.

Watch: the derivation, step by step

Watch: the same steps with numbers

Look at step 4: the right side, (b² − 4ac)/4a², is exactly what goes inside the square root in the formula. And √(4a²) = 2a: that is where the denominator 2a comes from.
