Lesson 3.1 · Multiplying binomials: FOIL and special products

Multiply two binomials with the area model and FOIL, and use the shortcuts for the square of a binomial and for sum times difference.

⏱️ About 4 hours with practice and homework · take breaks ✏️ No calculator

🎯What you will learn

When you finish this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Multiply two binomials with the area model and with FOIL.
  • Combine the middle terms and give the result in standard form.
  • Use the identity (a ± b)² = a² ± 2ab + b².
  • Use the identity (a + b)(a − b) = a² − b².

1The area model and FOIL

A link with Lesson 2.3. In Lesson 2.3 you multiplied a monomial by a polynomial with the distributive property. Now each factor has two terms: you multiply a binomial by a binomial. The idea is the same (each term of one factor multiplies each term of the other), organized with a method called FOIL.

Multiplying (x + 3)(x + 5) is like finding the area of a rectangle with sides (x + 3) and (x + 5). Split the rectangle into four parts: the total area is the sum of the four areas.

The FOIL method puts those four multiplications in order. The letters tell you which pairs to multiply:

What each letter of FOIL means when you multiply two binomials
LetterStands forMultiply
FFirstthe first terms of each binomial
OOuterthe two terms on the outside
IInnerthe two terms in the middle
LLastthe last terms of each binomial

2FOIL step by step

Example 1: multiply (x + 3)(x + 5).

FFirst: x · x = x².

OOuter: x · 5 = 5x.

IInner: 3 · x = 3x.

LLast: 3 · 5 = 15.

✓Combine the like terms (5x + 3x = 8x). x² + 8x + 15.

Example 2: with numbers and signs. Multiply (2x − 1)(x + 4).

F 2x · x = 2x².

O 2x · 4 = 8x.

I −1 · x = −x.

L −1 · 4 = −4.

✓Combine: 8x − x = 7x. 2x² + 7x − 4.

Watch the signs

Each term carries its sign when you multiply. A negative times a positive is negative, and a negative times a negative is positive. The last step is always to combine the two middle terms (Outer + Inner).

3Special product: the square of a binomial

When a binomial is squared, FOIL always gives the same pattern. The shortcut is worth knowing by heart:

(a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b²  ·  (a − b)² = a² − 2ab + b²

In words: the square of the first, plus (or minus) twice the product, plus the square of the second.

Example 3: expand (x + 5)².

1Square of the first. x².

2Twice the product. 2 · x · 5 = 10x.

3Square of the second. 5² = 25.

✓Answer. x² + 10x + 25.

Example 4: with a minus sign. Expand (x − 3)².

1Square of the first. x².

2Twice the product, with a minus sign. −2 · x · 3 = −6x.

3Square of the second. 3² = 9.

✓Answer. x² − 6x + 9.

(x + 5)² is not x² + 25

Squaring a binomial is not squaring each term separately: the middle term, 2ab, is missing. Forgetting it is the most common mistake. And the last term is always positive: (−3)² = 9.

4Special product: sum times difference

When you multiply a sum and a difference of the same two terms, the middle terms cancel and a difference of squares is left:

(a + b)(a − b) = a² − b²

Example 5: multiply (x + 7)(x − 7).

1Square of the first. x².

2The middle terms cancel. +7x − 7x = 0.

3Square of the second, with a minus sign. −49.

✓Answer. x² − 49.

The shortcut saves time

When you recognize (a + b)(a − b), you can write a² − b² right away, without the four products of FOIL. This identity will be the key to factoring in the next lesson.

5Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. For your own work, with your notebook open and no calculator: (1) Twelve products. Before you solve, sort each one as general FOIL, the square of a binomial, or sum times difference, then use the fastest method: (x + 3)(x + 5), (x − 4)(x + 2), (2x + 1)(x − 3), (x + 6)², (x − 7)², (3x + 2)², (x + 9)(x − 9), (2x − 5)(2x + 5), (x − 1)(x − 8), (4x + 3)(4x − 3), (x + 2y)², and (5x − 1)(x + 4). (2) Expand (x + 5)² two ways, with the whole FOIL and with the identity. Check that they match and point out where the 2ab term appears in FOIL. (3) Find the mistake: fix these three expansions and describe each mistake in one sentence: (x + 4)² = x² + 16; (x − 3)² = x² − 6x − 9; (x + 6)(x − 6) = x² − 12x − 36. About 60 minutes; check your work with the checklist of this lesson.
  • In your daily life. A square room measures x feet on each side. You add a strip 3 feet wide along two sides, so the new room is (x + 3) by (x + 3). Draw it as an area model and find the new area: where are the 2ab pieces? Measure a real room and check your formula.

📘Key words

binomial
A polynomial with two terms, like x + 3.
FOIL
First, Outer, Inner, Last: the four products of two binomials.
special product
A product with a pattern you can write right away.
perfect square trinomial
The result of squaring a binomial: a² ± 2ab + b².
difference of squares
a² − b², the result of (a + b)(a − b).
