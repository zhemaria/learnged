Multiplying binomials: the area model

›

The product of two binomials is the area of a rectangle. Find the area of each of the four parts, add them, and combine the like terms. The two middle parts always hold like terms (both have x), so the total has only three terms.

Multiplying binomials with FOIL

›

F, O, I, L: First, Outer, Inner, Last. Each term carries its sign: if the binomial is (x − 4), the last term is −4, not 4, and that sign travels with the number in the O, I, and L products.

Special product: the square of a binomial

›
(a + b)² = a² + 2ab + b² · (a − b)² = a² − 2ab + b²

Build the three pieces: the square of the first, twice the product (with its sign), and the square of the second.

The most common mistake: writing (x + 5)² as x² + 25. The middle term, 2ab, is missing. Squaring a binomial is never squaring each term separately.

Practice: expand the square

4

Special product: sum times difference

›
(a + b)(a − b) = a² − b²

First decide whether the product is a sum times a difference. It is only when the two binomials have the same terms and differ only in the sign in the middle. If not, use FOIL.

Step 1. Can you use the shortcut (a + b)(a − b) = a² − b²?

Why it works: in FOIL, the Outer and Inner products are opposites and cancel. Only the square of the first minus the square of the second is left.
