Lesson 1.3 · Equations of a line: slope-intercept, point-slope, and standard form

Write the equation of a line in slope-intercept, point-slope, and standard form, change between them, and tell parallel and perpendicular lines by their slopes.

⏱️ About 4 hours with practice and homework · take breaks 📐 Graph paper

🎯What you will learn

When you finish this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Write the equation of a line in slope-intercept form (y = mx + b) and read m and b.
  • Use the point-slope form to write the line through a point with a given slope, or through two points.
  • Change between slope-intercept, point-slope, and standard form (Ax + By = C).
  • Recognize parallel and perpendicular lines by their slopes.

1The slope-intercept form: y = mx + b

A link with Lesson 1.2. In Lesson 1.2 you found the slope (m) of a line. A line, though, is fixed by two facts: how steep it is (the slope) and where it goes through (a point or the intercept). This lesson puts the two together to write the equation of any line and change it between three equivalent forms. It is the tool that lets you model real linear situations (fees, pay, usage) with one algebraic expression.

The most direct way to write a line is the slope-intercept form:

y = mx + b

Here m is the slope (how much the line goes up or down for each unit to the right) and b is the y-intercept: the value of y where the line crosses the vertical axis, that is, the point (0, b).

Example 1: from the slope and the intercept. A line has slope 2 and crosses the y-axis at 3. What is its equation?

1Name the facts. m = 2 and b = 3.

2Substitute in y = mx + b.

✓Answer. y = 2x + 3.

Example 2: reading the slope and the intercept. For the line y = −3x + 5, find its slope and its intercept.

1The number in front of x is the slope. m = −3.

2The number by itself is the intercept. b = 5, so the line goes through (0, 5).

✓Answer. m = −3 (the line goes down) and b = 5.

Why y = mx + b is so handy

Just by looking at the equation you know what the line looks like: the sign of m tells you whether it goes up or down, and b tells you where it crosses the y-axis. That is why it is the favorite form for graphing. When m = 0, the line is horizontal and its equation is just y = b.

2The point-slope form: y − y₁ = m(x − x₁)

When you know the slope and any point of the line (not necessarily the intercept), use the point-slope form:

y − y₁ = m(x − x₁)

Here (x₁, y₁) is the known point. From this form you can always solve for y to get to y = mx + b.

Example 3: a point and the slope. Write the equation of the line with slope 3 through the point (2, 5).

1Substitute in the point-slope form. y − 5 = 3(x − 2).

2Distribute. y − 5 = 3x − 6.

3Solve for y. y = 3x − 6 + 5.

✓Answer. y = 3x − 1.

Example 4: two points. Write the equation of the line through (1, 2) and (3, 8).

1First, the slope. m = (8 − 2) ÷ (3 − 1) = 6 ÷ 2 = 3.

2Use one point, for example (1, 2). y − 2 = 3(x − 1).

3Distribute and solve for y. y − 2 = 3x − 3 → y = 3x − 1.

✓Answer. y = 3x − 1. (You get the same line with the other point.)

With two points, the slope comes first

The point-slope form needs a value of m. So when you are given only two points, the first step is always to find the slope with m = (y₂ − y₁) ÷ (x₂ − x₁). Watch the signs: with the point (−2, 4), the form is y − 4 = m(x + 2).

3The standard form and changing between forms

The standard form of the equation of a line is:

Ax + By = C

Here A, B, and C are whole numbers and, by custom, A is positive. The three forms describe the same line; you choose one or another depending on what you need.

The three forms of the equation of a line, with the general expression and when to use each
FormExpressionWhen to use it
Slope-intercepty = mx + bTo graph, and to see m and b at once.
Point-slopey − y₁ = m(x − x₁)To write the line from a point and the slope.
StandardAx + By = CA common format on the GED test; useful for systems of equations.

Example 5: from slope-intercept to standard. Change y = 2x + 3 to standard form.

1Move the x-term to the left side. −2x + y = 3.

2Multiply by −1 so that A is positive. 2x − y = −3.

✓Answer. 2x − y = −3.

Example 6: from standard to slope-intercept. Change 2x + 3y = 12 to slope-intercept form.

1Leave the y-term alone. 3y = −2x + 12.

2Divide everything by 3. y = −(2/3)x + 4.

✓Answer. y = −(2/3)x + 4, with slope −2/3 and intercept 4.

A check that never fails

Choose a value of x, for example x = 3, and evaluate both forms. If both give the same y, the conversion is right. In Example 6: 2(3) + 3y = 12 gives y = 2, and −(2/3)(3) + 4 = 2 too.

4Parallel and perpendicular lines

The slopes show how two lines are related:

  • Parallel lines have the same slope (m₁ = m₂). They never cross.
  • Perpendicular lines have negative reciprocal slopes; their product is −1 (m₁ × m₂ = −1). They cross at a right angle.

Example 7: classifying two lines. How are y = 2x + 1 and y = 2x − 4 related? And y = 2x + 1 and y = −(1/2)x + 3?

1The first two have the same slope (2). They are parallel.

2For y = 2x + 1 and y = −(1/2)x + 3, multiply the slopes. 2 × (−1/2) = −1.

✓Answer. y = 2x + 1 and y = −(1/2)x + 3 are perpendicular.

Example 8: writing a parallel line. Write the equation of the line parallel to y = 3x − 2 that goes through (0, 5).

1Parallel means the same slope. m = 3.

2It goes through (0, 5). b = 5.

✓Answer. y = 3x + 5.

The negative reciprocal: two moves, not one

To find the slope perpendicular to a given one, flip the fraction and change the sign. The perpendicular to m = 3 (that is, 3/1) is −1/3; the perpendicular to m = −2/5 is 5/2. Stopping halfway (flipping without changing the sign) is the most common mistake.

5Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. For your own work, with a notebook and graph paper: (1) Four lines in slope-intercept form: two from m and b, and two reading the slope and the intercept on a graph that you draw. (2) Four lines in point-slope form, two with a point and the slope and two with two points; then change each one to slope-intercept form. (3) Three equations changed between the three forms, one of them with a fraction for the slope. (4) Six pairs of lines sorted as parallel, perpendicular, or neither, with the slopes as your reason. (5) A real problem: a taxi charges $2.50 to start plus $1.80 per mile. Write the equation of the cost in the three forms and find how much a 12-mile ride costs. About 70 minutes; check your work with the checklist of this lesson.
  • In your daily life. Find a price with a fixed part and a part per unit: a cell phone plan, a gym membership with a sign-up fee, a delivery fee plus a price per pound. Write it as y = mx + b, say what m and b mean in that situation, and graph it for the first 10 units. Compare two plans: are their lines parallel? What would that mean?
