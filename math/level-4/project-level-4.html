Project instructions

›

This project brings together what you learned in Level 4, and it is the last piece of the level. You will model a real situation with a quadratic or exponential function and present it completely. It must include five parts:

  1. Real situation: describe what happens and explain why it is quadratic or exponential.
  2. The function: write the function that models the situation.
  3. The graph: with axes, scale, units, and the name of the function.
  4. Algebraic analysis: the vertex (quadratic) or the growth rate (exponential), and the value at a specific point.
  5. Written interpretation: what the result means in the real situation.

Ideas: the path of a ball (quadratic), savings with compound interest (exponential), the greatest area of a garden with a fixed fence (quadratic), or the growth of followers on social media (exponential).

The template draws the graph and checks your analysis, but you must show your work in writing: the automatic check is only there to check yours.

Your work is saved only in this browser. To keep it, press “Export as PDF” and choose “Save as PDF” as the destination.

4 · Algebraic analysis

›

Quadratic: find the vertex with x = −b/(2a), the axis of symmetry, and the intercepts. Exponential: the starting value and the percent of change per period. In both: the value at a specific point that matters in your situation.

5 · Written interpretation

›

Write at least five complete sentences. The numbers do not speak by themselves: explain what they mean.

Writing guide
  • What does the vertex (or the growth rate) mean in your situation, with its units?
  • What does the value at your specific point tell you?
  • For which values of x does the model make sense (its domain in real life)?
  • End with a conclusion of your own: what did you learn about the situation?

Project rubric

›

Five criteria at four levels (20 points in all). Read them before you start, and come back to them when you finish to grade your own work honestly.

Rubric for the final project, with five criteria and four levels
CriterionBeginning (1)Developing (2)Proficient (3)Outstanding (4)
1. Real situation and reasonsNo situation, or one that cannot be modeled.Describes the situation, but does not explain the kind of function.Describes the situation and explains why it is quadratic or exponential.A rich situation, with clear reasons for the chosen model.
2. Graph with labelsNo graph, or a wrong one.A graph with missing labels (axes, scale, or units).A correct graph with axes, scale, units, and the name of the function.A precise, complete graph, easy to read.
3. Algebraic analysisNo analysis, or serious mistakes.A partial analysis, or with calculation mistakes.Finds the vertex or the rate and the value at a specific point.A complete, correct analysis with all the key elements.
4. Written interpretationDoes not explain the results.An incomplete explanation, or one with no context.Explains the results clearly in the real situation.A complete, clear explanation, connected with the situation.
5. Oral presentationNo presentation, or it cannot be understood.Presents, but with little clarity or vague vocabulary.Presents clearly with suitable mathematical vocabulary.A confident, precise, convincing presentation.

The project grade is combined with the grade on the Level 4 final check (30 questions).
