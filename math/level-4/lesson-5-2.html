Math · Level 4 · Topic 5 — Rational expressions

Lesson 5.2 · Adding, subtracting, multiplying, and dividing rational expressions

Operate with rational expressions just as with fractions of numbers: factor and cancel to multiply, flip to divide, and use a common denominator to add and subtract.

⏱️ About 4 hours with practice and homework · take breaks ✏️ No calculator

🎯What you will learn

When you finish this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Multiply rational expressions by factoring and cancelling first.
  • Divide by multiplying by the reciprocal.
  • Add and subtract with the same denominator, giving the minus sign to the whole numerator.
  • Add and subtract with different denominators, using the LCD.

1Multiplying

A link with Lesson 5.1. In Lesson 5.1 you simplified rational expressions by factoring and cancelling. Now you operate with them. The good news: each operation is the same as with fractions of numbers. If you can operate with 2/3 and 1/4, you can operate with fractions of polynomials.

To multiply fractions, factor, cancel any common factor (a top one with a bottom one), and multiply the numerators and the denominators. Cancelling before multiplying saves a lot of work.

Example 1: multiply (x + 2)/(x − 3) · (x − 3)/(x + 5).

1Cancel the common factor (x − 3).

✓Answer. (x + 2)/(x + 5).

Example 2: multiply 6x/(x + 1) · (x + 1)/3.

1Cancel (x + 1).

26x/3 is left, and 6 ÷ 3 = 2.

✓Answer. 2x.

2Dividing

Dividing is multiplying by the reciprocal: flip the second fraction and multiply. The rule is “keep, change, flip.”

a/b ÷ c/d = a/b · d/c

Example 3: divide (x/2) ÷ (x²/6).

1Multiply by the reciprocal: (x/2) · (6/x²).

2Cancel: 6x / (2x²) = 3/x.

✓Answer. 3/x.

3Adding and subtracting with the same denominator

When the denominators are the same, combine the numerators and keep the denominator, just as 2/7 + 3/7 = 5/7.

Example 4: add 3x/(x + 2) + 4/(x + 2).

1Same denominator: add the numerators, 3x + 4.

✓Answer. (3x + 4)/(x + 2).

Example 5: subtraction. Subtract 5x/(x − 3) − 2x/(x − 3).

1Subtract the numerators: 5x − 2x = 3x.

✓Answer. 3x/(x − 3).

When you subtract, use parentheses

The minus sign goes to the whole second numerator: (2x + 1) − (x − 3) = 2x + 1 − x + 3 = x + 4. Forgetting the parentheses gives x − 2, which is wrong. And the denominator does not change: you never add the denominators.

4Adding and subtracting with different denominators

When the denominators are different, find the least common denominator (LCD), rewrite each fraction with it, and then combine the numerators. It is the same process as 1/2 + 1/3 = 3/6 + 2/6 = 5/6.

Example 6: add 1/x + 1/(2x).

1LCD of x and 2x: 2x.

2Rewrite: 2/(2x) + 1/(2x).

3Add the numerators: (2 + 1)/(2x).

✓Answer. 3/(2x).

Example 7: add 3/x + 2/x².

1LCD of x and x²: x².

2Rewrite: 3x/x² + 2/x².

✓Answer. (3x + 2)/x².

Example 8: two binomials. Add 2/x + 1/(x + 1).

1LCD: x(x + 1).

2Rewrite: 2(x + 1)/[x(x + 1)] + x/[x(x + 1)].

3Add the numerators: 2x + 2 + x = 3x + 2.

✓Answer. (3x + 2)/[x(x + 1)].

The same method as with numbers

Find the LCD, rewrite each fraction, and combine the numerators. The only new thing is that the denominators are now expressions with variables.

5Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. For your own work, with your notebook open. Work the four operations as if they were four stations: (1) Multiply (factor and cancel before you operate): (x² − 9)/(x + 2) · (x + 2)/(x − 3); 2x/(x² − 1) · (x + 1)/(4x²); (x² + 3x)/(x − 5) · (x² − 25)/(x² + 8x + 15). (2) Divide (multiply by the reciprocal): (x² − 4)/(3x) ÷ (x + 2)/(6x²); (x + 1)/(x² − x) ÷ (x² − 1)/x; (x² + 2x − 8)/(x² − 9) ÷ (x + 4)/(x + 3). (3) Add and subtract with the same denominator: 3/x + 5/x; (2x + 1)/(x − 4) − (x − 3)/(x − 4); x²/(x + 2) − 4/(x + 2). In the subtractions, give the minus sign to the whole numerator. (4) Add and subtract with different denominators (write the LCD first): 2/x + 3/(x + 1); 5/(x − 2) − 1/(x + 3); x/(x² − 9) + 2/(x + 3). Solve 1/4 + 1/6 next to them to compare the steps. (5) Checking: in one problem of each station, substitute a value of x that is not excluded in the original expression and in your result. If they do not match, find the mistake. Then do several exercises of each activity on the practice page. About 70 minutes; check your work with the checklist of this lesson.
  • In your daily life. Write 1/2 + 1/3 and 1/x + 1/(x + 1) side by side on a card. Explain to someone at home that the steps are the same, and check the second one with x = 1: 1/1 + 1/2 = 3/2, and (2x + 1)/[x(x + 1)] = 3/2.

📘Key words

reciprocal
A fraction flipped: the reciprocal of a/b is b/a.
keep, change, flip
To divide fractions: keep the first, change ÷ to ·, flip the second.
least common denominator (LCD)
The smallest expression that every denominator divides.
like denominators
Fractions with the same denominator: combine only the numerators.
