Lesson 7.3 · Average rate of change and kinds of functions

Find the average rate of change on an interval, read it with its units, and tell linear, quadratic, and exponential functions apart by their pattern.

1The average rate of change

A link with Lesson 7.2. In Lesson 7.2 you learned what a function is and how to evaluate f(x). Now you study how a function changes: how fast it goes up or down between two points. That measure is the average rate of change, and its pattern reveals the kind of function.

The average rate of change of a function on the interval from x = a to x = b measures how much the output changes for each unit of input. It is the slope between the points (a, f(a)) and (b, f(b)).

rate = (f(b) − f(a)) / (b − a)

In the graph, Δx (read “delta x”) means the change in x and Δy the change in y. From (1, 1) to (3, 9), Δy / Δx = 8 / 2 = 4.

Example 1: if f(2) = 10 and f(5) = 25, what is the average rate of change on [2, 5]?

1Use the formula: (25 − 10) / (5 − 2).

2Compute: 15 / 3 = 5.

✓Answer. On average, the function goes up 5 units for each unit of x.

Rate of change = slope

Finding the average rate of change between two points is exactly the same as finding the slope of the line that joins them (the secant line). In a linear function, that rate is the same on every interval.

2Reading the rate in context

The average rate of change makes sense with units: dollars per year, feet per second, people per month. The unit is always “unit of output per unit of input.”

Example 2: a savings account goes from $100 to $160 in 3 years. What is the average rate of change?

1Change in the money: 160 − 100 = 60 dollars.

2In 3 years: 60 / 3 = 20.

✓Answer. On average, the account grows $20 per year.

A negative rate

Always take the final value minus the starting value. If the quantity goes down, the rate is negative: a tank that goes from 400 to 100 gallons in 6 hours changes at −50 gallons per hour. The minus sign is not a mistake: it says the quantity decreases.

3Identifying the kind of function

The pattern of the outputs reveals the kind of function. Look at the differences or the ratios between consecutive values (with equally spaced inputs):

The pattern of the outputs that identifies each kind of function
KindPattern in the tableExample outputs
LinearConstant difference (adds the same)2, 5, 8, 11 (+3)
QuadraticConstant second difference0, 1, 4, 9 (differences 1, 3, 5)
ExponentialConstant ratio (multiplies by the same)1, 2, 4, 8 (×2)

↔ Slide the table sideways to see all the columns.

Adds the same or multiplies by the same

If to go from one value to the next you always add the same amount, the function is linear; if you always multiply by the same number, it is exponential; if the second difference is constant, it is quadratic.

📘Key words

average rate of change
(f(b) − f(a)) / (b − a): how much the output changes per unit of input.
secant line
The line that joins two points of a graph; its slope is the average rate of change.
second difference
The difference of the differences; it is constant in a quadratic function.
ratio
The quotient of two consecutive values; it is constant in an exponential function.
