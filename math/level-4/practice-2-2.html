Breaking a composite figure into parts

›

Choose a figure. Check the simple solids it breaks into and decide whether their volumes are added or subtracted. Then check your answer: when it is right, the parts are drawn apart, each in its color.

1. Which simple solids make it up?
2. Are the volumes added or subtracted?
Add or subtract? If the parts come together to make a bigger body, add. If a part is taken away (a hole, a hollow), subtract.

Surface area: the solid and its net

›

Each solid is shown closed and opened flat (its net). Choose lateral or total area, or press a piece to light it up in the net. The area is built piece by piece.

Lateral and total surface area of the solids on the formula sheet
SolidLateral areaTotal area
Rectangular prismperimeter of the base × height2ab + 2ac + 2bc
Cylinder2πrh2πrh + 2πr²
Coneπrℓ (ℓ = slant height)πrℓ + πr²
Pyramid1/2 × perimeter × ℓ1/2 × perimeter × ℓ + B
Sphere—4πr²
Lateral means “without the bases.” The lateral area counts only the surfaces around the body; the total area adds the bottom and, if there is one, the top. Read the problem carefully: an open tank has no top.

4

An idea of optimization

›
Area of a box = 2(length·width + length·height + width·height)

Choose a volume and move the slider through all the boxes with whole-number sides that hold exactly that volume. The table marks in green the one that uses the least cardboard.

The slider goes from the box that uses the most cardboard to the one that uses the least.

The same volume does not mean the same material. The volume measures what fits inside; the surface area measures the material that wraps it. Shapes closer to a cube tend to have less surface area.

Practice: which design uses less material?
