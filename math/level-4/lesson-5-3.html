Lesson 5.3 · Solving rational equations

Solve a rational equation by multiplying by the LCD, check for extraneous solutions, and model work-together and distance problems.

⏱️ About 4 hours with practice and homework · take breaks 🧮 Calculator allowed

🎯What you will learn

When you finish this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Check every solution and throw out the extraneous ones.
  • Model work-together problems with 1/a + 1/b = 1/t.
  • Solve distance, speed, and time problems with time = distance/speed.

1Clearing the denominators with the LCD

A link with Lesson 5.2. In Lesson 5.2 you added, subtracted, multiplied, and divided algebraic fractions. Now they appear inside equations. The strategy is direct: multiply by the LCD of all the denominators to clear them, and the rational equation becomes an equation you already know how to solve.

Four moves: write down the excluded values, multiply both sides by the LCD of all the denominators, solve the equation that is left (with no fractions), and check the solution against the excluded values.

Example 1: solve 6/x + 1 = 4.

1LCD of the denominators: x. Excluded value: x = 0.

2Multiply everything by x: 6 + x = 4x.

3Solve: 6 = 3x, so x = 2.

4x = 2 does not make the denominator zero. It is valid.

✓Solution. x = 2.

Example 2: solve 1/(x − 2) = 3/x.

1LCD: x(x − 2). Multiplying: x = 3(x − 2).

2Solve: x = 3x − 6, then −2x = −6, x = 3.

3x = 3 does not make any denominator zero. It is valid.

✓Solution. x = 3.

An equation is not a sum

Multiplying by the LCD is not the same as adding fractions: here there is an equals sign, so you can multiply everything by the LCD and make the denominators disappear. In a sum with no equals sign, like the ones in Lesson 5.2, the denominators stay.

2Extraneous solutions

When you multiply by the LCD, a “solution” may appear that actually makes a denominator of the original equation zero. That solution is extraneous and must be thrown out. That is why you must check every solution against the excluded values.

Example 3: solve x/(x − 3) = 3/(x − 3) + 2.

1LCD: (x − 3). Multiplying: x = 3 + 2(x − 3).

2Solve: x = 3 + 2x − 6, then x = 2x − 3, x = 3.

3But x = 3 makes the denominator (x − 3) zero. It is an extraneous solution.

✓Result. The equation has no solution.

Always check

A solution found with algebra that equals an excluded value is not valid. An extraneous solution is not an arithmetic mistake: multiplying by the LCD multiplied by something that is zero for that value of x. If every solution is extraneous, the equation has no solution.

3Application: working together

If one person takes a hours to do a job and another takes b hours, working together they take t hours, where:

1/a + 1/b = 1/t

Each fraction is the part of the job each one does in one hour.

Example 4: Ana paints a room in 3 hours; Luis, in 6 hours. How long do they take together?

1Set it up: 1/3 + 1/6 = 1/t.

2Add: 2/6 + 1/6 = 3/6 = 1/2.

3So 1/2 = 1/t, and t = 2.

✓Answer. Together they take 2 hours.

A common-sense check

Together they always take less time than the faster one alone. If your answer is greater than the shorter time, the setup is wrong: probably the times were added instead of the rates.

4More applications: distance, speed, and time

Many GED problems use the relation time = distance / speed, which produces rational equations. A typical case is a boat or a plane helped or slowed by a current or the wind:

The real speed of a boat depending on its direction compared with the current
SituationReal speed
With the current (downstream)v + c (the current helps)
Against the current (upstream)v − c (the current slows it)

↔ Slide the table sideways to see all the columns.

Here v is the speed in still water and c is the speed of the current. Writing each time as distance/speed gives a rational equation, which you solve with the LCD method.

Example 5: a boat goes 15 mph in still water. It takes the same time to go 20 miles downstream as 10 miles upstream. How fast is the current?

1Same time: 20/(15 + c) = 10/(15 − c).

2Multiply by the LCD (15 + c)(15 − c): 20(15 − c) = 10(15 + c).

3Solve: 300 − 20c = 150 + 10c, so 150 = 30c, c = 5.

✓Answer. The current goes 5 mph. Check: 20/20 = 1 hour and 10/10 = 1 hour.

5Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. For your own work, with your notebook and the calculator: (1) Do we work together? Solve with 1/a + 1/b = 1/t and check that the time together is less than each time alone: Ana paints a room in 6 h and Luis in 3 h; one faucet fills a tank in 4 h and another in 12 h; one machine finishes an order in 5 h and another in 20 h. (2) With and against: a boat goes 15 mph in still water and takes the same time to go 20 miles downstream as 10 miles upstream; how fast is the current? A plane flies 500 mph with no wind and covers 1,200 miles with the wind in the same time as 800 miles against it; how fast is the wind? (3) Hunting extraneous solutions: solve, compare with the excluded values, and throw out the solution that makes a denominator zero: x/(x − 3) = 3/(x − 3) + 2; x²/(x − 1) = 1/(x − 1); 3/x + 1 = 4/x. Explain which one has no extraneous solution. Then do several exercises of each activity on the practice page. About 70 minutes; check your work with the checklist of this lesson.
  • In your daily life. Think of a chore you share at home, such as washing the dishes. Time how long each person takes alone, use 1/a + 1/b = 1/t to predict how long you take together, and then try it.

📘Key words

rational equation
An equation with x in a denominator.
least common denominator (LCD)
The smallest expression that every denominator divides; multiplying by it clears the fractions.
extraneous solution
A value found with algebra that makes a denominator of the original equation zero; it is thrown out.
rate of work
The part of a job done in one unit of time: 1/a if the whole job takes a hours.
