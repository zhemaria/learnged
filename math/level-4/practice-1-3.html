The slope-intercept form: y = mx + b

›

In y = mx + b the two numbers tell you everything about the line: m is the slope (how much it goes up or down for each unit to the right) and b is the y-intercept, the point (0, b) where it crosses the vertical axis. Move both and watch.

Slopes from −3 to 3: −3, −2, −3/2, −1, −2/3, −1/2, 0, 1/2, 2/3, 1, 3/2, 2, 3

2

The point-slope form

›

When the known point is not the intercept, start with the point-slope form, which works with any point of the line:

y − y₁ = m(x − x₁)

Step by step

With two points, the slope comes first: the point-slope form needs a value of m, so step zero is always (y₂ − y₁) ÷ (x₂ − x₁). It does not matter which of the two points you use next: you get the same line.

3

Changing between the three forms

›

The three forms describe the same line; only the outfit changes. It pays to know how to go from one to another, because each one is good for something different.

The three forms and what each one is for
FormExpressionWhen to use it
Slope-intercepty = mx + bTo graph: m and b can be read at a glance.
Point-slopey − y₁ = m(x − x₁)To write the line from any point.
StandardAx + By = CA common GED format, and the form for systems of equations.

Step by step

A check that never fails: choose a value of x, for example x = 2, and evaluate both forms. If both give the same y, the conversion is right.

4

Parallel and perpendicular lines

›

You decide how two lines are related by looking only at the slopes: equal means parallel; negative reciprocals (their product is −1) mean perpendicular.

Negative-reciprocal calculator

Type a slope as a fraction and get the perpendicular slope. For a whole number, leave the bottom as 1.

Two moves, not one. The perpendicular slope comes from flipping the fraction and changing the sign. Stopping halfway (flipping without changing the sign) is the most common mistake here.
