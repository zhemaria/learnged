Lesson 6.1 · Solving systems of equations by substitution

1What is a system and what is its solution?

A link with Topic 5. Until now you solved one equation with one unknown. In Topic 6 you work with two equations at once, each with two variables (x and y). Solving the system means finding the pair (x, y) that makes both equations true at the same time. On a graph, it is the point where the two lines cross.

A system of linear equations is a set of two equations with the same two variables. Its solution is the ordered pair (x, y) that makes both equations true.

The key idea

For the system x + y = 5 and x − y = 1, the pair (3, 2) is the solution because it works in both: 3 + 2 = 5 and 3 − 2 = 1. No other pair works in both at once.

Both equations, always

A solution must work in both equations: it is not enough for a pair to work in one of them. That is why, at the end, you always check in both.

2The five steps of substitution

The five steps to solve a system by substitution
StepWhat to do
1Solve one of the equations for one variable.
2Substitute that expression into the other equation.
3Solve the equation you get (it has only one variable).
4Substitute that value to find the other variable.
5Check the solution in both original equations.

↔ Slide the table sideways to see all the columns.

Example 1: solve the system y = 2x + 1 and 3x + y = 11.

1The first equation already has y alone: y = 2x + 1.

2Substitute into the other: 3x + (2x + 1) = 11.

3Solve: 5x + 1 = 11, so x = 2.

4Find y: y = 2(2) + 1 = 5.

5Check: 3(2) + 5 = 11. Right.

✓Solution. (2, 5).

Example 2: solve first. Solve the system x + y = 7 and 2x − y = 2.

1Solve the first for y: y = 7 − x.

2Substitute: 2x − (7 − x) = 2.

3Solve: 3x − 7 = 2, so x = 3.

4Find y: y = 7 − 3 = 4.

✓Solution. (3, 4).

Choose the easiest variable

Solve for the variable whose coefficient is 1 or −1: that way you avoid fractions. And use parentheses when you substitute, especially after a minus sign: 2x − (7 − x) = 2x − 7 + x.

3Checking an ordered pair

To check whether a pair (x, y) is a solution of a system, substitute it in both equations. If it works in both, it is a solution; if it fails in one, it is not.

Example 3: is (4, 1) a solution of x + y = 5 and x − y = 3?

1First: 4 + 1 = 5. Right.

2Second: 4 − 1 = 3. Right.

✓Answer. Yes, (4, 1) is a solution.

4Application: prices

Many real problems have two unknowns and two facts. Give each unknown a variable, write two equations, and solve the system.

Example 4: at a coffee shop, 2 coffees and 1 tea cost $7; 1 coffee and 1 tea cost $5. How much does each one cost?

1Let c be the price of a coffee and t the price of a tea: 2c + t = 7 and c + t = 5.

2Solve the second for t: t = 5 − c.

3Substitute: 2c + (5 − c) = 7, so c + 5 = 7, c = 2.

4Find t: t = 5 − 2 = 3.

✓Answer. The coffee costs $2 and the tea, $3.

5Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. For your own work, with your notebook open: (1) Six systems by substitution, writing the five steps: y = 2x and x + y = 9; y = x + 1 and 2x + y = 10; x + y = 8 and x − y = 2; x = 3y and 2x − y = 10; 3x + y = 7 and 2x − 3y = 1. The sixth is a price problem: at another coffee shop, 3 coffees and 2 teas cost $13, and 1 coffee and 1 tea cost $5. (2) Which variable do I solve for? In each system, decide which variable is best to solve for (the one with coefficient 1 or −1), explain why that avoids fractions, and solve it: 2x + y = 11 and 3x − 2y = 6; x − 4y = 2 and 3x + 5y = 23; 5x − y = 3 and 2x + 3y = 25; 4x + 3y = 10 and −x + 2y = 3. (3) I always check in both: check each pair in both equations. If it is not a solution, say which equation it fails and find the right solution: (2, 5) in y = x + 3 and x + y = 7; (4, 1) in x − y = 3 and 2x + y = 12; (3, −2) in x + y = 1 and 2x − y = 5. Then do several exercises of each activity on the practice page. About 60 minutes; check your work with the checklist of this lesson.
  • In your daily life. Look at a receipt from a store or restaurant where you bought two kinds of things. Write a system with the prices as unknowns and the receipt totals as the facts, and ask someone at home to solve it.

📘Key words

system of equations
Two equations with the same two variables.
solution of a system
The ordered pair (x, y) that makes both equations true; on the graph, the crossing point.
substitution
Solving one equation for a variable and putting that expression into the other equation.
ordered pair
(x, y): the x value always first.
