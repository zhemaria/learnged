1The exponential function f(x) = a · bˣ

A link with Lesson 8.1. Quadratic functions go up and then down (or down and then up), making a parabola. Exponential functions are different: they grow (or decay) faster and faster, because they multiply by a fixed base. They model money that earns interest, a population that doubles, or a substance that breaks down.

In f(x) = a · bˣ, the number a is the starting value (when x = 0), and the base b decides the behavior:

The behavior of an exponential function by its base b
If the base b…The function…Example
is greater than 1 (b > 1)grows (growth)y = 2ˣ
is between 0 and 1 (0 < b < 1)decays (decay)y = (1/2)ˣ

The two curves cross at (0, 1) because any base to the power 0 is 1. To the right, the growth curve goes up faster and faster; the decay curve gets closer and closer to zero without touching it.

2Growth and decay in context

Exponential growth appears when something is multiplied by a fixed factor every period: a population that doubles, an account that earns interest. Exponential decay appears when something is reduced by a fixed factor: the half-life of a radioactive substance, a car that loses value.

Half-life

The half-life is the time it takes for an amount to be cut in half. If there are 80 ounces and the half-life is 1 hour, after 1 hour there are 40 ounces, after 2 hours 20 ounces, and so on.

From a percent to the base

Grows 8% each year: it keeps all of it plus 8%, so the base is 1 + 0.08 = 1.08. Loses 15% each year: it keeps 85%, so the base is 1 − 0.15 = 0.85.

3Compound interest

Compound interest is exponential growth applied to money. It is on the GED formula sheet:

A = P(1 + r/n)nt

P is the starting amount (the principal), r the rate (as a decimal), n the number of times it is compounded each year, t the number of years, and A the final amount.

Example 1: $1,000 at 5% a year, compounded once a year (n = 1), for 10 years.

1Substitute: A = 1000(1 + 0.05/1)1·10 = 1000(1.05)10.

2With the calculator: (1.05)10 ≈ 1.6289.

✓Answer. A ≈ $1,628.89.

4A first look at the logarithm

The logarithm is the inverse of the exponential. It answers the question: “to what exponent must I raise the base to get this number?”

if by = x, then logb(x) = y

Example 2: log₁₀(1000).

1The question: to what power do I raise 10 to get 1000?

✓Since 10³ = 1000, log₁₀(1000) = 3.

The log key

On the TI-30XS, the log key finds log₁₀ directly. For example, log(100) gives 2, because 10² = 100.

5Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • Paper and pencil. For your own work, with your notebook, the calculator, and the GED formula sheet: (1) The bank and time: find how much $1,000 is worth at 5% interest compounded yearly after 1, 5, 10, and 20 years. Graph the points and describe the shape of the curve: why does the growth speed up? (2) When does it double? With the table above, estimate between which two years the money reaches $2,000, and narrow it down by trying the years in between. Then find log₁₀(1000), log₁₀(100), and log₂(8), and check each result by writing it as a power. (3) Growth or decay? Classify each model and justify it with the base (greater or less than 1): a population P = 2000(1.03)ᵗ; a medicine in the blood M = 500(0.5)ᵗ; savings A = 800(1.04)ᵗ; the temperature difference of a cooling cup of coffee D = 60(0.9)ᵗ; the value of a car V = 25000(0.85)ᵗ. Then do several exercises of each activity on the practice page. About 60 minutes; check your work with the checklist of this lesson.

📘Key words

starting value
The a in f(x) = a · bˣ: the value when x = 0.
half-life
The time it takes for an amount to be cut in half.
compound interest
Interest earned on the interest too: A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt).
logarithm
