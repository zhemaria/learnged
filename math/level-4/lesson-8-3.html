Math · Level 4 · Topic 8 — Modeling with functions and a practice GED test

Lesson 8.3 · Final project and practice GED math test

Bring Level 4 together in your own mathematical model, present it, learn strategies for the GED math test, and take the final check.

⏱️ About 4 hours with practice and homework · take breaks 🎓 The close of Level 4

🎯What you will learn

When you finish this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Model a real situation with a quadratic or exponential function.
  • Present the project “My mathematical model” with clear mathematical vocabulary.
  • Use test strategies, the formula sheet, and the calculator for the GED Mathematical Reasoning test.
  • Answer GED-style questions that combine the eight topics of the level.
🎓 Standards for this lesson (GED® and CCR)

This lesson works on these GED® Assessment Targets:

  • A.2.d Solve a system of two simultaneous linear equations by graphing, substitution, or linear combination. Solve real-world problems leading to a system of linear equations.
  • A.4.a Solve quadratic equations in one variable with rational coefficients and real solutions, using appropriate methods (e.g., quadratic formula, completing the square, factoring, inspection).
  • A.5.e For a function that models a linear or nonlinear relationship between two quantities, interpret key features of graphs and tables in terms of quantities, and sketch graphs showing key features given a verbal description of the relationship.
  • A.7.c Evaluate linear and quadratic functions for values in their domain when represented using function notation.

This lesson builds the foundation for these GED® Assessment Targets:

  • A.1.d Add, subtract, multiply polynomials, including multiplying two binomials, or divide factorable polynomials.
  • A.1.h Add, subtract, multiply and divide rational expressions.
  • A.6.a Write the equation of a line with a given slope through a given point.

Mathematical Practices you use:

  • MP.1.b Plan a solution pathway or outline a line of reasoning.
  • MP.1.c Select the best solution pathway, according to given criteria.
  • MP.3.a Build steps of a line of reasoning or solution pathway, based on previous step or givens.
  • MP.4.c Display data or algebraic expressions graphically.

College and Career Readiness (CCR) Standards for Adult Education:

  • A-CED.2 Level E Create equations in two or more variables to represent relationships between quantities; graph equations on coordinate axes with labels and scales.
  • F-IF.4 Level E For a function that models a relationship between two quantities, interpret key features of graphs and tables in terms of the quantities, and sketch graphs showing key features given a verbal description of the relationship. Key features include: intercepts; intervals where the function is increasing, decreasing, positive, or negative; relative maximums and minimums; symmetries; end behavior; and periodicity.
  • F-LE.5 Level E Interpret the parameters in a linear or exponential function in terms of a context.

1The final project “My mathematical model”

The close of the level. This is the last lesson of Level 4. It brings everything together: analytic geometry, polynomials, quadratic equations, rational expressions, systems, and functions. The goal is twofold: present your project and show, on a final check, that you are ready for the GED test.

The project, “My mathematical model”, is to model a real situation with a quadratic or exponential function. It must include five parts:

The five parts of the project “My mathematical model”
PartWhat to include
1. Real situationDescribe what happens and explain why it is quadratic or exponential.
2. The functionWrite the function that models the situation.
3. The graphGraph it with axes, scale, units, and the name of the function.
4. Algebraic analysisFind the vertex or the growth rate, and the value at a specific point.
5. Written interpretationExplain in words what the result means in the real situation.

Good situations

The path of a ball (quadratic), savings with compound interest (exponential), the greatest area of a garden with a fixed fence (quadratic), or the growth of followers on social media (exponential). Use the Final project template: My mathematical model: you type your function and your work, and it draws the graph, checks your vertex or your values, and saves everything in this browser.

2The oral presentation

Present your model in 3 to 4 minutes: explain the situation, show the graph, and talk about the analysis, to someone you trust (a relative, a friend, or a study partner) or recording yourself on your phone. Ask them to ask you questions, or, if you record yourself, ask them yourself as you watch the video. What counts is the clear and precise use of mathematical vocabulary (vertex, growth rate, intercept, domain, and so on).

A tip for the presentation

Practice out loud once before. Start with the real situation (to catch interest), then show the function and the graph, and end with the interpretation: “this means that…”.

3Strategies for the GED test

The final check works like the real test. These strategies help you do your best:

Strategies for the GED Mathematical Reasoning test
StrategyHow to use it
Manage your timeDo not spend too long on one question; flag it and come back at the end.
Rule out choicesCross out the impossible answers before you calculate.
The formula sheetUse it with confidence; you already know where each formula is.
The TI-30XS calculatorClear it before each calculation; check what you type.
Read what is askedUnderline the question and the units. Many wrong answers are right answers to a different question.

🧮 About the calculator

The GED test uses the TI-30XS MultiView calculator. The calculator on this page does the same operations, although some keys have other names or are in other places. If you have a TI-30XS, practice with it too so you learn where its keys are.

Real test conditions

Take the Level 4 final check in one sitting: time yourself, do not look at the lessons, and use only the calculator and the formula sheet. Breathe, read calmly, and trust what you learned.

The final check

When you are ready, take the Level 4 final check: 30 questions from the eight topics, answered all at once, with a report by topic that tells you what to review. You pass with 21 right answers (70%).

5Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. This is the last homework of Level 4. Do it in this order, over several days: (1) Complete the project “My mathematical model” in the Final project template: My mathematical model, with its five parts (situation, function, graph, analysis, and interpretation), grade it with the rubric, and fix what does not reach “Proficient.” Save it as a PDF. (2) Present your model in 3 to 4 minutes to someone you trust, or record yourself on your phone, and check that you used the mathematical vocabulary precisely. (3) Take the Level 4 final check in real conditions: in one sitting, timing yourself, and only with the calculator and the formula sheet. (4) My final study plan: with the report of the final check, write which topics to review, with which practices (the index on the practice page of this lesson gathers them all), and in what order. End with a concrete goal for the week before your test. About 3 hours, spread over several days; the final check needs a block with no interruptions.
  • In your daily life. Choose the situation for your model from your own life: the savings you want to build, the price of something you plan to buy, a ball you throw with your children, or the garden you would like to fence.

Rubric for the final project “My mathematical model” and what Got it, Getting there, and Needs more practice look like
SkillGot itGetting thereNeeds more practice
