1What a ratio is

Welcome to Topic 4. Until now, all of Level 2 has been about operating with numbers: integers, fractions, powers, roots, and PEMDAS. In Topic 4 the question changes. It is no longer “What does this operation give?” but “How do these two quantities compare?” This lesson opens the topic with the most basic idea of all: comparing two quantities.

A ratio compares two quantities of the same kind. You read it “this to that” or “this for every that.” For example: in a classroom there are 12 students and 3 teachers. The ratio of students to teachers is 12 to 3, which simplifies to 4 to 1: there are 4 students for every teacher.

Three ways to write the same ratio

With the word “to”
4 to 1
read “4 to 1”
With a colon
4 : 1
read “4 to 1”
As a fraction
414/1
read “4 to 1”

The fraction form is the most useful one for calculating: you simplify the ratio the same way you simplify a fraction, dividing both terms by their greatest common factor.

Example: simplify a ratio

1Write it. A bakery has 15 loaves of wheat bread and 10 loaves of rye. The ratio of wheat to rye is 15 to 10, or 151015/10.

2Simplify. The greatest common factor is 5: 15 ÷ 5 = 3 and 10 ÷ 5 = 2.

✓Answer. 3 to 2 (or 3 : 2, or 3/2). For every 3 loaves of wheat there are 2 of rye.

Order matters

“Wheat to rye” is 3 to 2, but “rye to wheat” is 2 to 3. The quantity named first goes first in the ratio. Always.

2A rate: a ratio with different units

When the two quantities have different units, the ratio has a special name: a rate. Rates are everywhere: price per pound, miles per hour, words per minute, dollars per gallon.

A unit rate is a rate whose second term is 1. To find it, divide the first term by the second. It is the most useful form, because it lets you compare options at a glance.

Example: a unit rate

1The rate. A car travels 240 miles in 4 hours.

2Divide. First term by second term: 240 ÷ 4 = 60.

✓Answer. 60 miles per hour. “Per hour” tells you the second term is now 1.

Word clue

The words per, each, and for one point to a unit rate. “$3 per pound” means 3 dollars for each pound; “60 miles per hour” means 60 miles in one hour.

🧮 Unit rates on the calculator

For 240 miles in 4 hours, type 240 ÷ 4 enter. The screen shows 60. Always divide in the order of the words: first term ÷ second term.

3Comparing options with unit rates

The unit rate is the most practical tool for choosing between two sizes of the same product. Find the price per unit of each one, and the comparison becomes a matter of reading two numbers.

Example: which bag of rice is the better buy?A 5-pound bag of rice costs $7.50, and a 10-pound bag costs $13.00.

1Bag A. $7.50 ÷ 5 pounds = $1.50 per pound.

2Bag B. $13.00 ÷ 10 pounds = $1.30 per pound.

✓Answer. Bag B is cheaper: each pound costs 20 cents less.

The lowest total price can fool you

The 5-pound bag costs less in total, but more per pound. Only the unit rate lets you compare fairly.

4Real-life applications

Ratios and unit rates show up in shopping, travel, recipes, sports, and work. Once you know the unit rate, you can multiply to find a total, or divide to find how many.

Example: mixing paintA paint mix uses 2 parts blue for every 5 parts white. If you use 8 parts blue, how much white do you need to keep the same ratio?

1The ratio. Blue to white is 2 to 5, and it stays the same.

2Multiply both terms. Blue went from 2 to 8: it was multiplied by 4. White too: 5 × 4 = 20.

✓Answer. 20 parts of white paint.

Example: miles per gallonCar A goes 320 miles on 16 gallons. Car B goes 270 miles on 9 gallons. Which car gets more miles per gallon?

1Car A. 320 ÷ 16 = 20.

2Car B. 270 ÷ 9 = 30.

✓Answer. Car B, with 30 miles per gallon.

5Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. Write and simplify five ratios in their three forms. Find five unit rates in real situations (shopping, travel, reading). Compare four pairs of options by their unit rate. Solve two mixing problems that keep a ratio the same.
  • In your daily life. Next time you shop, choose one product sold in two sizes. Find the price per unit of each one (it is often printed on the shelf label as “unit price”) and decide which one is the better buy.

📘Key words

ratio
A comparison of two quantities of the same kind, like 3 to 2.
rate
A ratio of two quantities with different units, like 240 miles in 4 hours.
unit rate
A rate whose second term is 1, like 60 miles per hour.
unit price
The price of one unit (one pound, one ounce, one item).
