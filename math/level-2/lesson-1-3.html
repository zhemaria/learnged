1The sign rule for multiplying

In Lesson 1.2 you learned to add and subtract integers. This lesson finishes Topic 1 with the other two operations: multiplying and dividing. The good news: both use one sign rule, and it is shorter than the rule for adding.

To multiply two integers, multiply the absolute values as you always do. What is new is the sign, and it depends only on the signs of the two factors.

The sign of the product for each pair of signs of the factors
Factor 1Factor 2Product
positivepositivepositive (+)
negativenegativepositive (+)
positivenegativenegative (−)
negativepositivenegative (−)

↔ Slide the table sideways to see all the columns.

The short rule

Same signs give a positive product. Different signs give a negative product. One sentence covers the four rows of the table.

Example: (−7) × (−6)

1Step 1. Multiply the absolute values: 7 × 6 = 42.

2Step 2. Both factors are negative: same signs.

✓Result. Same signs give a positive product: (−7) × (−6) = 42

Example: 9 × (−4)

1Step 1. Multiply the absolute values: 9 × 4 = 36.

2Step 2. One factor is positive and one is negative: different signs.

✓Result. Different signs give a negative product: 9 × (−4) = −36

Watch out!

This rule is for multiplying and dividing. For adding, the rule is different: −7 + (−6) = −13, but (−7) × (−6) = 42. Before you choose a rule, look at the operation.

2Why is a negative times a negative positive?

"A negative times a negative is positive" is not a random rule: you can discover it in a pattern. Multiply −2 by a list of numbers that goes down by one. Each product goes up by 2:

Products of a list that goes down by one, times −2, and how much each product changes
MultiplicationProductChange
3 × (−2)−6—
2 × (−2)−4went up 2
1 × (−2)−2went up 2
0 × (−2)0went up 2
(−1) × (−2)2went up 2 (the pattern says it must be positive)
(−2) × (−2)4went up 2

↔ Slide the table sideways to see all the columns.

For the pattern to keep going, the product of two negatives has to be positive. There is no other way to continue it without breaking it.

In everyday language

Taking away a debt again and again makes you richer. If a debt of $5 (−5) is taken away 3 times (−3), you gain $15: (−3) × (−5) = 15.

You do not need to repeat the pattern every time. It only shows that the rule makes sense. In practice, use the short rule: same signs, positive; different signs, negative.

3Dividing integers

Dividing integers uses exactly the same sign rule as multiplying. There is nothing new to memorize: same signs give a positive quotient; different signs give a negative quotient.

Example: (−45) ÷ (−9)

1Step 1. Divide the absolute values: 45 ÷ 9 = 5.

2Step 2. Both numbers are negative: same signs, so the quotient is positive.

✓Result. (−45) ÷ (−9) = 5

Example: (−56) ÷ 8

1Step 1. Divide the absolute values: 56 ÷ 8 = 7.

2Step 2. One is negative and one is positive: different signs, so the quotient is negative.

✓Result. (−56) ÷ 8 = −7

Check by multiplying

You can always check a division with a multiplication. If (−45) ÷ (−9) = 5, then 5 × (−9) must be −45. It is, so both the value and the sign are right.

Zero in a division

Zero divided by any other number is 0: 0 ÷ 5 = 0, because 0 × 5 = 0. But you cannot divide by zero: 5 ÷ 0 has no answer, because no number times 0 gives 5. We say that a division by zero is undefined.

On the GED

A question may ask when an expression is undefined. Look for a division where the number you divide by is (or becomes) 0. On the calculator, a division by zero gives an error.

4Expressions with the four operations

Many expressions mix adding, subtracting, multiplying, and dividing integers. For now, with no parentheses and no powers, follow one simple rule: first multiply and divide, from left to right; then add and subtract, from left to right. You will study the full order of operations in Topic 3.

Example: (−4) × 3 + (−10) ÷ 2

1Step 1. The multiplication: (−4) × 3 = −12.

2Step 2. The division: (−10) ÷ 2 = −5.

3Step 3. Now there is only an addition: −12 + (−5) = −17.

✓Result. (−4) × 3 + (−10) ÷ 2 = −17

The most common mistake

Working from left to right without looking at the operations. In the example, adding −4 and 3 first would give a wrong answer. Multiplying and dividing come first.

🧮 The calculator knows the order

The calculator follows the same order. For (−4) × 3 + (−10) ÷ 2, press (−) 4 × 3 + (−) 10 ÷ 2 enter. The screen shows −17. Solve it on paper first, then check.

5Real-life problems

You multiply when an amount with a sign repeats, and you divide when it is shared into equal parts. A loss, a drop, or a debt has a negative sign.

Example: a dropping temperature

1The problem. The temperature in a freezer room drops 3 °F every hour. How much has it changed after 5 hours?

2The signs. "Drops 3 °F" is −3 per hour, and it repeats 5 times: (−3) × 5.

✓Result. Different signs, negative: −15. The temperature dropped 15 °F, a change of −15 °F.

Example: a shared loss

1The problem. A small business lost a total of $84 in 7 days, the same amount each day. What was the change each day?

2The signs. The total loss is −84, shared among 7 days: (−84) ÷ 7.

✓Result. Different signs, negative: −$12 a day, a loss of $12 each day.

6Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. Write and solve ten products and quotients of integers: predict the sign before you calculate, and check each division with a multiplication. Then write and solve five expressions that use all four operations, step by step.
  • In your daily life. Write three problems from your life where an amount with a sign repeats or is shared: a temperature that drops every hour, a debt paid in equal payments, a price that goes down each week. Solve each one and explain the sign of the answer.

📘Key words

product
The answer to a multiplication.
quotient
The answer to a division.
factor
Each of the numbers you multiply.
sign rule
To multiply or divide: same signs give a positive answer; different signs give a negative answer.
undefined
An expression with no answer, like a division by zero.
order of operations
The order to solve an expression. For now: multiply and divide first, then add and subtract.
