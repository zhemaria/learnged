Lesson 7.2 · Circumference of a circle

A new figure: the circle. Its parts, the discovery of π, the formulas C = πd and C = 2πr, and how to find the radius from the circumference.

⏱️ About 4 hours with practice and homework · take breaks 🧮 Calculator to check

🎯What you will learn

When you finish this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Name the parts of a circle: center, radius, diameter, and circumference.
  • Use the relationship d = 2r between diameter and radius.
  • Discover π as the constant ratio of the circumference to the diameter.
  • Find the circumference with C = πd or C = 2πr.
  • Solve reverse problems: find the radius or the diameter from the circumference.

1The parts of a circle

In Lesson 7.1 you worked with rectangles, triangles, and trapezoids: figures with straight sides. This lesson brings in a completely different figure: the circle, a closed curve whose points are all the same distance from a center point. The old formulas are not enough to measure it; a famous constant appears: π (pi).

The parts of a circle
PartWhat it is
Center (O)the point that is the same distance from every point on the circle
Radius (r)the distance from the center to any point on the circle
Diameter (d)the segment across the circle through the center; it is twice the radius
Circumference (C)the length of the edge of the circle: its “perimeter”

d = 2 × r     r = d ÷ 2

Careful with the words

The circle is the whole region, including the inside; the circumference is only the edge. People sometimes use the words loosely, but when you measure, be clear about which one you are measuring.

2The discovery of π

Measure the edge (the circumference, C) of several round objects with a string, and measure their diameter (d) with a ruler. Something amazing happens: the ratio C ÷ d always gives the same number, whatever the size of the object.

The ratio C ÷ d in real objects
ObjectDiameter d (in)Circumference C (in)C ÷ d
Quarter (coin)0.9553.00≈ 3.14
Dinner plate1031.42≈ 3.14
Soda can2.68.17≈ 3.14
Car tire2681.68≈ 3.14

↔ Slide the table sideways to see all the columns.

That constant number has its own name: π, the Greek letter “pi”. Its exact value has infinitely many decimal places with no repeating pattern, but for calculations an approximation is enough:

π ≈ 3.14159

The usual approximation is π ≈ 3.14. Another useful one, as a fraction, is π ≈ 22/7. The calculator also has a π key, which gives a slightly more exact answer.

A key idea

Nobody invented π. It is a constant of nature: every circle, of any size, has a circumference about 3.14159 times its diameter. That is why π shows up so often in mathematics.

3The two circumference formulas

The definition of π gives the circumference formula right away. If C ÷ d = π, then:

C = π × d

when you know the diameter

C = 2 × π × r

when you know the radius (because d = 2r)

The two formulas are the same; choose based on the data you have. Both are on the GED formula sheet.

Example: with the diameterA pizza has a diameter of 14 inches. What is its circumference?

1Data. d = 14 in. Use C = π × d.

2Substitute. C ≈ 3.14 × 14 = 43.96 in.

✓Answer. The circumference of the pizza is about 44 inches.

Example: with the radiusA circular walking path has a radius of 40 feet. How long is one full lap?

1Data. r = 40 ft. Use C = 2 × π × r.

2Substitute. C ≈ 2 × 3.14 × 40 = 251.2 ft.

✓Answer. One lap is about 251.2 feet.

Watch out

Any result found with π ≈ 3.14 is approximate, not exact. That is why we write “≈” (approximately equal). If an exact answer is asked for, leave it in terms of π: C = 14π inches.

🧮 π on the calculator

For the walking path: 2 × π × 40 enter gives 251.327… With the π key the answer is a little more exact than with 3.14 (251.2). On the GED, both are usually close enough; read the answer choices.

4The reverse problem: finding the radius

Sometimes you know the circumference and need the radius or the diameter. Just solve the formula:

d = C ÷ π     r = C ÷ (2π)

Example: find the radiusThe edge of a round table is 188.4 in long. What is its radius?

1Data. C = 188.4 in. You want r.

2Solve. r = C ÷ (2π) ≈ 188.4 ÷ (2 × 3.14) = 188.4 ÷ 6.28.

3Divide. 188.4 ÷ 6.28 = 30.

✓Answer. The radius of the table is 30 in.

Check

Check by working backward. If r = 30 in, then C = 2 × 3.14 × 30 = 188.4 in. ✓ It matches. This quick check catches arithmetic mistakes.

5Real uses

The circumference shows up in very concrete situations: how much fencing goes around a round garden, how much trim covers the edge of a table, how far a tire rolls in one turn.

Example: fencing a gardenA round garden has a radius of 7 ft. How many feet of fencing go around it? Use π ≈ 22/7.

1Formula. C = 2 × π × r ≈ 2 × (22/7) × 7.

2Simplify. The 7 in the denominator cancels with the radius: 2 × 22 = 44.

✓Answer. About 44 ft of fencing. (22/7 is handy when the radius is a multiple of 7.)

Example: a rolling tireA car tire has a diameter of 24 in. How far does the car move in one full turn of the tire?

1One turn = the circumference. C ≈ 3.14 × 24 = 75.36 in.

2In feet. 75.36 ÷ 12 = 6.28 ft.

✓Answer. With each turn the car moves about 75.36 in, a little more than 6 feet.

6Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. Find the circumference in four problems given the radius and four given the diameter. Find the radius or the diameter in three problems given the circumference. Solve three real problems (a round garden, a tire, a plate).
  • In your daily life. Find three round objects at home (a plate, a can, a lid). Measure the edge with a string and the diameter with a ruler, in inches. Divide C ÷ d for each one. How close do you get to 3.14?

📘Key words

radius (r)
The distance from the center to the edge of a circle.
diameter (d)
The segment across the circle through the center: d = 2r.
circumference (C)
The length of the edge of a circle: C = πd.
π (pi)
The ratio C ÷ d of any circle, about 3.14.
