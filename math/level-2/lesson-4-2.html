1What a proportion is

In Lesson 4.1 you learned to write ratios and find unit rates to compare options. This lesson takes the natural next step: when two ratios show the same relationship, they form a proportion. A proportion lets you do more than compare: it lets you predict an unknown quantity. This lesson is about direct proportions; inverse proportions come in Lesson 4.3.

A proportion is an equation between two equivalent ratios. If two ratios simplify to the same result, they are equal, and together they form a proportion.

aba/b = cdc/d

It is read “a is to b as c is to d.”

Example: is it a proportion?Do the ratios 232/3 and 8128/12 form a proportion?

1Simplify. 8128/12 = 232/3 (divide by 4).

2Or compare cross products. 2 × 12 = 24 and 3 × 8 = 24.

✓Answer. Yes: 232/3 = 8128/12 is a proportion.

Key idea

A proportion keeps the ratio between two quantities the same, even when their sizes change. That is why you can scale: a recipe for 2 people can become a recipe for 10 without changing the taste.

2Is it proportional? The constant-ratio test

Two quantities are in direct proportion when, as one grows, the other grows in the same ratio: if one doubles, the other doubles; if one triples, the other triples. The test is simple: divide each pair of values. If the quotient is always the same, the relationship is proportional. That fixed quotient is the constant of proportionality.

Table A: hours worked, pay, and pay divided by hours
Hours1234
Pay ($)15304560
Pay ÷ hours15151515

The quotient is always 15: Table A is proportional.

Table B: miles, cost, and cost divided by miles
Miles1234
Cost ($)5.257.509.7512.00
Cost ÷ miles5.253.753.253.00

↔ Slide the table sideways to see all the columns.

The cost grows with the miles, but the quotient changes. Table B is not proportional: 2 miles do not cost twice as much as 1 mile.

Growing together is not enough

Any fixed charge (a sign-up fee, the base fare of a taxi, the monthly fee of a phone plan) breaks the proportion, because the cost of zero units is not zero.

3The cross-product rule

The power of proportions is in one simple rule: if two ratios are equal, their cross products are equal too.

If aba/b = cdc/d, then a × d = b × c

Example: find the missing termSolve 383/8 = x24x/24.

1Cross products. 3 × 24 = 8 × x.

2Work out the known side. 72 = 8x.

3Solve for x. Divide by 8: x = 72 ÷ 8 = 9.

✓Answer. x = 9

Only with two equal ratios

Cross products work only when there is an equation between two ratios. They do not apply when you add or subtract fractions, or to a single fraction.

4The rule of three and direct-proportion problems

The rule of three is the traditional way to set up a proportion when you know three values and want the fourth. Write the data in two rows, with the same units in the same column.

Example: at the marketIf 3 pounds of tomatoes cost $4.50, how much do 8 pounds cost?3 lb→goes with$4.508 lb→goes withx

1Is it direct? More pounds, more money, in the same ratio: yes.

2Cross-multiply and divide. x = (8 × 4.50) ÷ 3 = 36 ÷ 3 = 12.

✓Answer. $12.00

Shortcut with the unit rate

You can also find the price of 1 pound first ($4.50 ÷ 3 = $1.50) and multiply by 8 ($12.00). It is the unit rate from Lesson 4.1 put to work.

GED problems often show direct proportions in recipes, maps, currency exchange, and mixtures. The method is always the same: set up the ratios with the units lined up, solve, and check.

Example: a recipeA recipe uses 4 cups of flour for 12 cookies. How much flour do you need for 30 cookies?

1Set up. Flour on top, cookies on the bottom in both ratios: 4124/12 = x30x/30.

2Cross products. 4 × 30 = 12x → 120 = 12x.

3Solve. x = 120 ÷ 12 = 10.

✓Answer. 10 cups of flour. Check: 4 × 30 = 120 and 12 × 10 = 120. ✓

Example: a map scaleOn a map, 1 inch stands for 25 miles. What real distance do 3.5 inches stand for?

1Set up. Inches on top, miles on the bottom: 1251/25 = 3.5x3.5/x.

2Cross products. 1 × x = 25 × 3.5 = 87.5.

✓Answer. 87.5 miles

Example: changing moneySuppose the bank gives 18.50 Mexican pesos for 1 dollar. How many pesos do you get for $40? (The real exchange rate changes every day; this one is made up.)

1Set up. Dollars on top, pesos on the bottom: 118.501/18.50 = 40x40/x.

✓Solve. x = 40 × 18.50 = 740 pesos.

Common mistake: mixed-up units

If flour is on top in the first ratio, it must be on top in the second ratio too. Flipping only one ratio gives a wrong answer.

📘Key words

proportion
An equation between two equal ratios, like 2/3 = 8/12.
cross products
In a/b = c/d, the products a × d and b × c. In a proportion they are equal.
constant of proportionality
The fixed quotient y ÷ x in a direct proportion.
rule of three
A way to set up a proportion in two rows when you know three values and want the fourth.
