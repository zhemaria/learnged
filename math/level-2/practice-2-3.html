The root as the inverse operation

›

The square root answers the question: "What number times itself gives this number?" Squaring and taking the square root are inverse operations: one undoes the other. So the table of squares, read backward, is the table of roots.

72 = 49  ⟷  √49 = 7

The root as the side of a square

›

The area of a square is its side squared. So, if you know the area, the square root of the area is the side. A square of 36 small squares has 6 on each side.

Estimating roots that are not exact

›

If the number is not a perfect square, its root is not a whole number, but you can trap it between two perfect squares next to each other: since 49 < 50 < 64, √50 is between 7 and 8, and closer to 7.

4

Checking by squaring

›

Every root can be checked with the inverse operation: if √144 = 12, then 12 × 12 must be 144. That way you catch a mistake without a calculator. The TI-30XS is a backup, but knowing the squares by heart is faster on the test.
