At the doctor's office and the pharmacy

›

The United States uses the U.S. customary system (pounds, inches, gallons); almost all the rest of the world uses the metric system (kilograms, centimeters, liters). At the doctor's office weight is usually written in pounds, but many medicine doses are figured per kilogram.

From pounds to kilograms

To check: a kilogram is a little more than 2 pounds, so the weight in kilograms is a little less than half the weight in pounds (pounds ÷ 2.2).

Practice: at the doctor's office

Round to one decimal place, for example 74.9.

3

The conversion-fraction method

›

Since 1 in and 2.54 cm are the same length, the fraction 2.54 cm / 1 in is worth 1: multiplying by it changes the unit, not the amount. Golden rule: the unit you want to get rid of goes on the bottom, so it cancels. If there is no direct factor, chain several fractions.

Build the chain

Common mistake: using the fraction upside down. 10 km × 1.609 gives 16.09 “miles”, more than the kilometers, even though miles are longer. If the new unit is bigger, the number must come out smaller.

Practice: convert

Round to two decimal places, for example 30.48.

4

Temperature: a special case

›

Temperature is not converted with a single factor, because the two scales do not start at the same zero: water freezes at 0 °C, which is 32 °F. So you subtract or add 32, following the order of operations.

°C = (°F − 32) × 5 ÷ 9     °F = °C × 9 ÷ 5 + 32

The two thermometers

Practice: temperature

Round to one decimal place if needed; use the minus sign for temperatures below zero.
