The parts of a circle

›

The center is the same distance from every point on the edge. That distance is the radius (r). The diameter (d) goes across the circle through the center: it is twice the radius. The circumference (C) is the length of the edge, the “perimeter” of the circle.

d = 2 × r     r = d ÷ 2

The discovery of π

›

If you measure the edge of any round object and divide it by its diameter, you always get the same number, a little more than 3. That number is π (pi): π ≈ 3.14159. In calculations we use π ≈ 3.14.

3

The two circumference formulas

›
C = π × d     C = 2 × π × r

They are the same formula; choose based on the data you have. The result with π ≈ 3.14 is approximate (≈). If an exact answer is asked for, leave it “in terms of π”: C = 14π.

4

Finding the radius or the diameter

›

If you know the circumference, solve the formula: the diameter is C ÷ π, and the radius is C ÷ (2π).

d = C ÷ π     r = C ÷ (2π)
Example: the edge of a round table is 188.4 in. r = 188.4 ÷ (2 × 3.14) = 188.4 ÷ 6.28 = 30 in.

5

Real uses

›

A wheel rolls its circumference in each turn. A fence around a round garden measures its circumference. One lap around a circular track is its circumference.
