Lesson 6.2 · Depreciation and other money applications

The mirror image of simple interest: things that lose the same amount of value every year. The book-value table, the formula D = (V₀ − Vr) ÷ n, and problems that mix interest, tax, and depreciation.

⏱️ About 4 hours with practice and homework · take breaks 🧮 Calculator to check

🎯What you will learn

When you finish this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Explain starting value, salvage value, useful life, and book value.
  • Build the book-value table year by year before using the formula.
  • Find the yearly straight-line depreciation, (V₀ − Vr) ÷ n, and its yearly rate.
  • Find the book value of an item in a given year.
  • Solve money problems that combine percent, simple interest, and depreciation.

1Depreciation vocabulary

In Lesson 6.1 money in savings grew by the same amount every year because of simple interest. Many things you buy go the opposite way: a car, a computer, or a work tool lose value with use and with time. When that loss is the same every year, it is called straight-line depreciation.

2First the table, then the formula

Before you use a formula, it helps to see the pattern in a table.

Example: the office computerAn office buys a computer for $1,800. It will be used for 4 years, and at the end it can be sold for $200. What is it worth at the end of each year?

1Total loss. 1,800 − 200 = 1,600.

2Split it into equal parts. 1,600 ÷ 4 = 400 per year.

3Subtract year by year. Each year, take $400 away from the year before.

3The straight-line depreciation formulas

The steps of the computer example add up to two formulas.

D = (V₀ − Vr) ÷ n

D = yearly depreciation · V₀ = starting value · Vr = salvage value · n = useful life in years

Book value in year t = V₀ − D × t

D × t is the accumulated depreciation up to that year.

The yearly rate

If an item lasts 4 years, each year it loses 1 of 4 equal parts of the value being depreciated: 1 ÷ 4 = 0.25 = 25% a year. In general, the yearly rate of straight-line depreciation is 1 ÷ n. For the computer: 25% of $1,600 = $400, the same result.

Example: work equipmentA sewing shop buys an industrial machine for $3,000. Its useful life is 5 years and its salvage value is $500. Find the yearly depreciation, the yearly rate, and the book value at the end of year 3.

1Yearly depreciation. D = (3,000 − 500) ÷ 5 = 2,500 ÷ 5 = 500 per year.

2Yearly rate. 1 ÷ 5 = 0.20 = 20% a year.

3Book value in year 3. 3,000 − 500 × 3 = 3,000 − 1,500 = 1,500.

✓Answer. It loses $500 a year (20%), and at the end of year 3 it is worth $1,500.

Example: in what year does it reach a value?With the same machine: at the end of what year will its book value be $1,000?

1Value it must lose. 3,000 − 1,000 = 2,000.

2Years needed. 2,000 ÷ 500 = 4.

✓Answer. At the end of year 4.

Example: when the problem gives a percent of the cost“A $30,000 delivery van depreciates 10% of its original cost each year.” What is it worth after 4 years?

1Yearly depreciation. 30,000 × 0.10 = 3,000.

2Book value in year 4. 30,000 − 3,000 × 4 = 30,000 − 12,000 = 18,000.

✓Answer. $18,000. It is still straight-line depreciation: the loss is the same every year; only the way the data is given changes.

Common mistake

Dividing the starting value by the useful life without first subtracting the salvage value. For the machine, 3,000 ÷ 5 = 600 is wrong: with that number the machine would end up worth $0, not the $500 the problem says.

🧮 Book value on the calculator

For the machine in year 3: ( 3000 − 500 ) ÷ 5 enter gives 500. Then 3000 − 500 × 3 enter gives 1,500. The calculator multiplies before it subtracts.

4Combined money problems

In real life, money decisions mix several ideas: the percent of a tax, the interest on a loan, and the loss of value of what you buy. The strategy: solve each part on its own, then put the results together.

Example: the business vanRosa borrows $18,000 at 6% simple interest a year for 3 years to buy a van for her business. The van has a useful life of 6 years and a salvage value of $3,000. When she finishes paying the loan, how much has she paid in all, and what is the van worth?

1The loan. I = 18,000 × 0.06 × 3 = 3,240; total paid A = 18,000 + 3,240 = 21,240.

2The depreciation. D = (18,000 − 3,000) ÷ 6 = 2,500 per year.

3Book value in year 3. 18,000 − 2,500 × 3 = 10,500.

4What it means. 21,240 − 10,500 = 10,740.

✓Answer. Rosa paid $21,240 and the van is worth $10,500. Using it for three years really cost her $10,740.

Example: tax and depreciationA laptop costs $1,200 plus a 10% sales tax. It will be used for 3 years and at the end will be worth $120. What is it worth after 2 years?

1Starting value with tax (Lesson 5.3). 1,200 × 1.10 = 1,320.

2Yearly depreciation. D = (1,320 − 120) ÷ 3 = 1,200 ÷ 3 = 400.

3Book value in year 2. 1,320 − 400 × 2 = 520.

✓Answer. After 2 years it is worth $520.

5Homework

➡️Coming next

Straight-line depreciation is the mirror image of simple interest: instead of adding a fixed amount every year, you subtract it. With the year-by-year table and the formula D = (V₀ − Vr) ÷ n you can tell what an item is worth at any time and what it really costs to use it.

📘Key words

straight-line depreciation
A loss of value that is the same every year.
salvage value (Vr)
What an item is still worth at the end of its useful life.
useful life (n)
The number of years an item is expected to be used.
book value
What an item is worth, by the calculations, in a given year: V₀ − D × t.
