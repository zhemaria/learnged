1PEMDAS with fractions

In Lesson 3.2 you added powers, roots, and negative numbers to PEMDAS, and saw that the fraction bar groups. This lesson closes Topic 3 with two more steps: working with fractions inside PEMDAS, and going from numbers to letters, that is, substituting values into algebraic expressions. That is exactly what the GED asks every time it gives you a formula.

Fractions do not change the order of operations. What changes is how you do each operation, which you studied in Topic 2: to add or subtract, find a common denominator; to multiply, multiply the numerators and the denominators; to divide, multiply by the reciprocal.

Example: 121/2 + 343/4 × 232/3

1MD. Multiplication before addition: 343/4 × 232/3 = 6126/12 = 121/2.

2AS. 121/2 + 121/2 = 222/2 = 1.

✓Answer. 1. If you had added first, you would get 545/4 × 232/3 = 565/6, which is wrong.

Example: (232/3)2 − 191/9 ÷ 131/3

1E. (232/3)2 = 2 × 23 × 32 × 2/3 × 3 = 494/9.

2MD. Divide by multiplying by the reciprocal: 191/9 × 313/1 = 393/9.

3AS. 494/9 − 393/9 = 191/9.

✓Answer. 191/9

Watch the exponent

(232/3)2 = 494/9, because the parentheses raise the whole fraction. But 22322/3 = 434/3, because the exponent applies only to the 2. It is the same idea as (−5)2 and −52: the exponent reaches only what is right before it.

Example with signs: 12 − 231 − 3(12 − 23) ÷ (1 − 3)

1Numerator. E before S: 23 = 8, so 12 − 8 = 4.

2Denominator. 1 − 3 = −2.

✓Divide. 4 ÷ (−2) = −2 (different signs, negative).

2From numbers to letters: substitute and evaluate

A variable is a letter that takes the place of a number. To evaluate an algebraic expression, you replace each letter with its given value and calculate with PEMDAS. There are two golden rules.

Two golden rules

Rule 1 · Always substitute inside parentheses. If x = −2, write (−2) every place x appears. The parentheses protect the sign of the number.

Rule 2 · A letter next to a number means multiplication. 3x means 3 × x; 2ab means 2 × a × b.

Example: evaluate 4x − 7 for x = 3

1Substitute. 4(3) − 7

2MD. 4 × 3 = 12 → 12 − 7.

✓AS. 12 − 7 = 5

Example: evaluate x2 − 3x + 2 for x = −2

1Substitute. (−2)2 − 3(−2) + 2

2E. (−2)2 = 4. Thanks to the parentheses, the sign is squared too → 4 − 3(−2) + 2.

3MD. 3 × (−2) = −6, and subtracting −6 is the same as adding 6 → 4 + 6 + 2.

✓AS. 4 + 6 + 2 = 12

The most common GED mistake

Evaluate −x2 for x = −3. The exponent applies only to the x, so you calculate −(−3)2 = −(9) = −9, not 9. First raise the value you substituted; then apply the sign that was in front.

Example: evaluate a + ba − b(a + b) ÷ (a − b) for a = 5 and b = −3

1Substitute. (5) + (−3)(5) − (−3)((5) + (−3)) ÷ ((5) − (−3))

2Numerator and denominator. 5 + (−3) = 2. 5 − (−3) = 5 + 3 = 8.

✓Divide and simplify. 282/8 = 141/4

Example with a fraction: evaluate 2x + 121/2 for x = 343/4

1Substitute. 2(343/4) + 121/2

2MD. 2 × 343/4 = 646/4 = 323/2.

✓AS. 323/2 + 121/2 = 424/2 = 2

3Formulas in context: how the GED asks

The GED Math test gives you a formula sheet. You do not have to memorize anything: you have to know which value goes with each letter, substitute, and apply PEMDAS. It is the same skill as in the last section, with words around it.

Example · TemperatureThe formula F = 959/5C + 32 changes degrees Celsius to degrees Fahrenheit. How many degrees Fahrenheit is 25 °C?

1Substitute. F = 959/5(25) + 32

2MD. 959/5 × 25 = 2255225/5 = 45.

3AS. 45 + 32 = 77.

✓Answer. 77 °F

Example · Area of a trapezoidThe area of a trapezoid is A = 121/2(b1 + b2)h. Find the area if b1 = 6 feet, b2 = 10 feet, and h = 5 feet.

1Substitute. A = 121/2((6) + (10))(5)

2P. 6 + 10 = 16.

3MD. From left to right: 121/2 × 16 = 8; 8 × 5 = 40.

✓Answer. 40 square feet

Example · Simple interestThe simple interest formula is I = Prt. How much interest do $1,200 earn at a yearly rate of 5% for 3 years?

1The values. P = 1,200; r = 5% = 0.05; t = 3.

2MD. 1,200 × 0.05 = 60; 60 × 3 = 180.

✓Answer. $180. You will study this formula in depth in Lesson 6.1.

A strategy for formula problems
  1. Write the formula.
  2. Write the value of each letter under it.
  3. Substitute inside parentheses.
  4. Apply PEMDAS.
  5. Answer with the right unit, and ask yourself if the answer makes sense.

4Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. Solve eight PEMDAS expressions with fractions (fraction bars, operations with fractions, and powers of fractions), one line per step. Then evaluate four expressions from this lesson with new values: one positive, one negative, and one fraction. Last, choose three GED formulas (temperature, area of a trapezoid, and cost with a fixed charge), make up reasonable values, evaluate them, and check them on the calculator.
  • In your daily life. Find a formula in your daily life: a phone plan, a taxi or ride app, a gym, or a repair shop that charges a fixed fee plus a rate. Write it as C = fixed + rate × units, and find the cost for two different amounts.

📘Key words

variable
A letter that takes the place of a number, like x.
evaluate
To replace each letter with its value and calculate.
substitute
To put a number in place of a letter, always inside parentheses.
formula
A rule written with letters, like P = 2(l + w).
