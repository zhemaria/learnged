The full number line

›

Zero is in the center of the number line: the positive numbers are to the right and the negative numbers are to the left. The numbers grow to the right: any number is greater than every number to its left. That is why −2 is greater than −5.

Compare two integers

In real life: a thermometer is a number line standing up. −10 °F is lower, and colder, than −3 °F.

    Absolute value: the distance from zero

    ›

    The absolute value of a number is its distance from zero. A distance is never negative, so the absolute value is always positive or zero. You write it between two bars: |−5| = 5 and |5| = 5.

    Measure the distance

    The absolute value measures distance. A minus sign outside the bars stays: in −|−6|, first |−6| = 6, and then the minus sign outside makes it −6.

    Practice: absolute value

    Type an integer. If it is negative, start with the minus sign: −7 or -7.

    Opposites

    ›

    Two numbers are opposites when they are the same distance from zero, on opposite sides. A number and its opposite always add up to zero, so the opposite is also called the additive inverse. Zero is its own opposite.

    Zero is a mirror

    Two minus signs: the minus sign in front of a number means "the opposite of." So −(−8) is "the opposite of −8," which is 8.
