1What a power is: base and exponent

In Lesson 2.1 you finished your work with signed fractions. This lesson brings a new way of writing math: powers. A power is a short way to write a multiplication in which the same number repeats.

53 = 5 × 5 × 5 = 125

  • The big number, 5, is the base: the number that repeats.
  • The small raised number, 3, is the exponent: it tells how many times the base is a factor.
  • The answer, 125, is the value of the power.

You read 53 as "five to the third power" or "five cubed," and 72 as "seven to the second power" or "seven squared."

The most common mistake

The exponent does not multiply the base. 53 is not 5 × 3 = 15; it is 5 × 5 × 5 = 125. The exponent counts the factors; it is not one more factor.

Example: evaluate 25

1Step 1. The base is 2 and the exponent is 5: multiply 2 five times.

2Step 2. 2 × 2 = 4; 4 × 2 = 8; 8 × 2 = 16; 16 × 2 = 32.

✓Result. 25 = 32

2Special cases of the exponent

The exponent 1

Any number to the first power is the number itself: the factor appears only once. So 91 = 9 and 471 = 47.

The exponent 0

Any number other than zero to the power 0 is 1. So 80 = 1 and 2500 = 1.

Do not mix them up

a0 = 1 (always 1), but a1 = a (the number itself). The exponent 0 gives 1; the exponent 1 gives the base.

A negative base

When the base is negative and in parentheses, the sign is multiplied too. By the sign rule from Topic 1, the answer is positive if the exponent is even and negative if it is odd.

Example: (−3)2 and (−3)3

1Even exponent. (−3)2 = (−3) × (−3) = 9. Same signs: positive.

2Odd exponent. (−3)3 = (−3) × (−3) × (−3) = 9 × (−3) = −27.

✓Result. (−3)2 = 9 and (−3)3 = −27

Watch out! Parentheses matter

(−3)2 = 9, but −32 = −9. Without parentheses, the exponent applies only to the 3: −32 = −(3 × 3) = −9. The GED test checks this difference.

3Powers of 10

Powers of 10 have a very useful pattern: the exponent tells how many zeros follow the 1.

Multiplying a number by a power of 10 moves the decimal point to the right as many places as the exponent says. Add zeros if you need them.

Example: 5.3 × 103

1Step 1. The exponent is 3: move the decimal point 3 places to the right.

2Step 2. 5.3 → 53. → 530. → 5300. (add zeros when the digits run out)

✓Result. 5.3 × 103 = 5,300

Looking ahead

This pattern of the powers of 10 is the base of scientific notation, which you will study in Level 3. Learning it well now will make that topic much easier.

🧮 Powers on the calculator

For any power, use the ^ key: for 25, press 2 ^ 5 enter. The screen shows 32. To square a number, the x2 key is faster: 13 x2 enter shows 169.

For a negative base, use parentheses: ( (−) 3 ) x2 enter shows 9. Without the parentheses, the calculator gives −9.

4The perfect squares from 1 to 225

A perfect square is the result of squaring a whole number. Knowing the first fifteen by heart is a key skill: they are the tool for square roots in the next lesson.

  • 12 = 1
  • 22 = 4
  • 32 = 9
  • 42 = 16
  • 52 = 25
  • 62 = 36
  • 72 = 49
  • 82 = 64
  • 92 = 81
  • 102 = 100
  • 112 = 121
  • 122 = 144
  • 132 = 169
  • 142 = 196
  • 152 = 225
How to learn them

Learn them in groups of five: 1 to 25, 36 to 100, and 121 to 225. Flash cards help: the power on one side and the value on the other. The practice page has a set of cards you can flip.

5Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. Write and evaluate twelve powers, including some with the exponent 0 and some with a negative base (with and without parentheses). Then do four multiplications by powers of 10 in your head and check them on paper.
  • In your daily life. Make fifteen flash cards for the perfect squares: 72 on one side, 49 on the other. Review them for five minutes a day this week, on the bus or during a break.

📘Key words

power
A short way to write a repeated multiplication: 53 = 5 × 5 × 5.
base
The number that repeats as a factor.
exponent
The small raised number that tells how many times the base is a factor.
squared / cubed
To the power 2 / to the power 3.
perfect square
The square of a whole number, like 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, …
power of 10
10 to some exponent: 1 followed by that many zeros.
