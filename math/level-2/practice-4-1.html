What a ratio is

›

A ratio compares two quantities of the same kind: “so many for every so many.” It can be written three ways that mean the same: 4 to 1, 4 : 1, or 4/1. You simplify it like a fraction, dividing both terms by their greatest common factor.

Make the groups

Order matters: “wheat to rye” is 3 to 2, but “rye to wheat” is 2 to 3. The quantity named first goes first.

The unit rate

›

When the two quantities have different units (miles and hours, dollars and pounds), the ratio is called a rate. The unit rate has 1 as its second term: divide the first term by the second. The words “per” and “each” give it away.

Comparing options

›

To find the better buy, find the price per unit of each option. The lowest total price is not always the better buy: a small package costs less in all, but sometimes more per unit.

Speed and other rates in context

›

Once you know the unit rate, use it: at 60 miles per hour, in 3 hours a car goes 60 × 3 = 180 miles, and 300 miles take 300 ÷ 60 = 5 hours. The same works for pay, gas use, and mixes.
