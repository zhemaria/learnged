Lesson 6.3 · Converting between U.S. customary and metric units

From pounds to kilograms, from miles to kilometers, from Fahrenheit to Celsius: the conversion factor, the conversion-fraction method, and the special case of temperature.

⏱️ About 4 hours with practice and homework · take breaks 🧮 Calculator to check

🎯What you will learn

When you finish this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Convert lengths, weights, and capacities between the U.S. customary and metric systems with the most common factors.
  • Use the conversion-fraction method: multiply by 1 and cancel units.
  • Solve conversions with several steps, for example, from feet to meters or from miles to meters.
  • Convert temperatures between degrees Fahrenheit and Celsius.
  • Apply conversions in health, work, and travel situations, with a reasonable estimate.

1At the doctor's office and the pharmacy

In Topic 4 you worked with ratios and proportions; in Lessons 6.1 and 6.2, with amounts that change at a fixed rate. Converting units is one more kind of proportional reasoning: you multiply a measurement by a ratio of equal amounts, the conversion factor.

At the doctor's office, the nurse wrote down Mrs. Morales's weight: 165 pounds. But the pharmacy form asks for the weight in kilograms. How do you convert it?

The United States uses the U.S. customary system: inches, feet, miles, pounds, gallons. Almost all the rest of the world, including Mexico and the rest of Latin America, uses the metric system: centimeters, meters, kilometers, grams, liters. Medicine, science, and many products use metric units even in the U.S., so you need to go from one system to the other.

Example: the weight for the pharmacyConvert 165 lb to kilograms. Use 1 lb ≈ 454 g and 1 kg = 1,000 g.

1Pounds to grams. 165 × 454 = 74,910 g.

2Grams to kilograms. 74,910 ÷ 1,000 = 74.91 kg.

3Check with the rule of thumb. “1 kg is a little more than 2 lb”: 165 ÷ 2.2 = 75. ✓

✓Answer. Mrs. Morales weighs about 74.9 kg.

Rules of thumb for estimating

A meter is a little more than a yard · a kilogram is a little more than 2 pounds · a liter is a little more than a quart · a kilometer is a little more than half a mile (about 0.6 mi). Use them to check whether a result makes sense.

3The conversion-fraction method

Since 1 in and 2.54 cm are the same length, each of these fractions is exactly equal to 1:

2.54 cm1 in2.54 centimeters over 1 inch to go from inches to centimeters   ·   1 in2.54 cm1 inch over 2.54 centimeters to go from centimeters to inches

Multiplying by 1 does not change the amount, only the unit it is written in. The key is to choose the right fraction.

Golden rule

The unit you want to get rid of goes on the bottom of the fraction. That way it cancels with the unit on top, and the unit you want is left.

Example: inches to centimetersA school ruler is 12 in long. How many centimeters is that?

1Get rid of “in”: it goes on the bottom. 12 in × (2.54 cm / 1 in).

2The inches cancel. 12 × 2.54 = 30.48 cm.

✓Answer. 30.48 cm

Example: kilometers to milesHow many miles long is a 10 km race?

1Get rid of “km”: it goes on the bottom. 10 km × (1 mi / 1.609 km).

2Divide. 10 ÷ 1.609 ≈ 6.2 mi.

3Check. 1 km ≈ 0.6 mi, so 10 km ≈ 6 mi. ✓

✓Answer. About 6.2 miles.

Example: gallons to litersHow many liters does a 3-gallon water jug hold?

1Get rid of “gal”. 3 gal × (3.785 L / 1 gal) = 11.355 L.

✓Answer. About 11.4 L.

Conversions with several steps

When there is no direct factor, chain fractions together. Each one cancels one unit, until you reach the unit you want.

Example: feet to metersA person is 6 ft tall. How many meters is that?

1Path. feet → inches → centimeters → meters.

2Chain. 6 ft × (12 in / 1 ft) × (2.54 cm / 1 in) × (1 m / 100 cm).

3Step by step. 6 × 12 = 72 in; 72 × 2.54 = 182.88 cm; 182.88 ÷ 100 = 1.8288 m.

✓Answer. About 1.83 m.

Example: miles to metersHow many meters are in 2 miles?

1First step. 2 mi × (1.609 km / 1 mi) = 3.218 km.

2Second step. 3.218 km × (1,000 m / 1 km) = 3,218 m.

✓Answer. 3,218 m

Common mistake

Using the fraction upside down. Changing 10 km to miles by multiplying by 1.609 gives 16.09 “miles”: a bigger number, even though miles are longer than kilometers. If the new unit is bigger, the number must come out smaller.

4Temperature: a special case

Temperature is not converted with a single factor, because the two scales do not start at the same zero: water freezes at 0 °C, which is 32 °F. So first you subtract 32 (inside the parentheses, as in PEMDAS from Topic 3), and then you multiply.

°C = (°F − 32) × 5 ÷ 9

From Fahrenheit to Celsius

°F = °C × 9 ÷ 5 + 32

From Celsius to Fahrenheit

Example: body temperatureThe thermometer reads 98.6 °F. How many degrees Celsius is that?

1Parentheses. 98.6 − 32 = 66.6.

2Multiply and divide. 66.6 × 5 ÷ 9 = 333 ÷ 9 = 37.

✓Answer. 37 °C, normal body temperature.

Example: a pleasant dayThe forecast says 20 °C. How many degrees Fahrenheit is that?

1Multiply and divide first. 20 × 9 ÷ 5 = 36.

2Then add 32. 36 + 32 = 68.

✓Answer. 68 °F

🧮 Temperature on the calculator

For 98.6 °F: ( 98.6 − 32 ) × 5 ÷ 9 enter gives 37. Without the parentheses the calculator would multiply first and give a wrong result.

📘Key words

U.S. customary system
The system of inches, feet, miles, pounds, and gallons used in the United States.
metric system
The system of meters, grams, and liters, based on powers of 10.
conversion factor
A ratio of two equal measurements, like 2.54 cm / 1 in; it is worth 1.
conversion fraction
The conversion factor written so that the unit to get rid of is on the bottom.
