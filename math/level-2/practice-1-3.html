The sign rule

›

To multiply two integers, multiply the absolute values as always. What is new is the sign: same signs give a positive answer; different signs give a negative answer.

Why is a negative times a negative positive?

›

The rule comes from a pattern. Multiply a negative number by a list that goes down by one: each product goes up by the same amount. For the pattern to keep going past zero, the products of two negatives have to be positive.

Dividing integers

›

Dividing uses exactly the same sign rule as multiplying. And you can check it by multiplying: if (−45) ÷ (−9) = 5, then 5 × (−9) must be −45.

You cannot divide by zero: 0 ÷ 5 = 0, but 5 ÷ 0 has no answer, because no number times 0 gives 5. A division by zero is undefined.

Expressions with the four operations

›

When the four operations are mixed, multiply and divide first, from left to right, and then add and subtract, from left to right. You will study the full order in Topic 3.

Step by step

The most common mistake: working from left to right without looking at the operations. In (−4) × 3 + (−10) ÷ 2, the multiplication and the division come first: −12 + (−5) = −17.

Practice: solve the expression

Type an integer. If it is negative, start with the minus sign: −7 or -7.

Real-life problems

›
