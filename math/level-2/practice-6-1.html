Money vocabulary

›
The five quantities of simple interest
QuantityWhat it is
Principal (P)the starting money you deposit or borrow
Rate (r)the yearly percent applied to the principal
Time (t)how long, in years
Interest (I)the money the savings earn, or what the loan costs
Total amount (A)principal plus interest: A = P + I
Time goes in years: 18 months are 1.5 years, because 18 ÷ 12 = 1.5. And 6 months are half a year.

The simple-interest formula

›

3

The rate and the time in the same unit

›

The formula only works if the rate is a decimal and the rate and the time use the same unit. If the rate is yearly, the time goes in years: divide months by 12 and days by 365 (or by 360, if the problem says to use the banker's year). If the rate is monthly and the time is in months, they already match.

Common mistake: using 9 months as if they were 9 years. A $4,000 loan at 6% a year for 9 months costs 4,000 × 0.06 × 0.75 = $180, not $2,160.

4

Growth year by year

›

With simple interest, each year's interest is always the same, because it is figured on the original principal. The money grows in a regular way: a staircase with equal steps.

The savings staircase

Compound interest is different: there, each year's interest is figured on the amount from the year before, and money grows faster.

5

Solving for the principal, the rate, or the time

›

If you know three of the four quantities in I = P × r × t, you can find the missing one. One rule for all three: divide the interest by the product of the other two values.

The three ways to solve
You wantFormula
The principalP = I ÷ (r × t)
The rater = I ÷ (P × t), then × 100 for the percent
The timet = I ÷ (P × r)
If the problem gives the total amount A instead of the interest, first find I = A − P.
