Lesson 7.3 · Area of a circle and figures with semicircles

The inside of the circle: A = πr², when to use circumference and when to use area, the radius from the area, and figures that combine rectangles with semicircles.

1The area formula

In Lesson 7.2 you measured the edge of a circle: its circumference. This lesson finishes the study of the circle by measuring its inside surface: how much area fits inside it. You will also combine circles with simple figures to make real designs, like a running track or an arched window.

A = π × r²

The area is π times the square of the radius. The square applies only to the radius, not to π.

Where the formula comes from

Cut a circle into many thin slices and lay them side by side, alternately up and down. They form a shape that looks like a parallelogram. Its base is half the circumference (π × r) and its height is the radius (r). Base times height: π × r × r = π × r².

Order of operations

In A = πr², the square applies only to the radius. First find r², then multiply by π: exponents come before multiplication (PEMDAS). For example, if r = 5, then r² = 25 and A = π × 25.

Example: area from the radiusFind the area of a circle with a radius of 6 in. Use π ≈ 3.14.

1Formula. A = π × r² ≈ 3.14 × 6².

2Square first. 6² = 36.

3Multiply. 3.14 × 36 = 113.04.

✓Answer. The area is about 113.04 in².

Example: you only know the diameterFind the area of a circle with a diameter of 10 ft.

1First the radius. r = d ÷ 2 = 10 ÷ 2 = 5 ft.

2Square the radius. 5² = 25.

3Formula. A ≈ 3.14 × 25 = 78.5.

✓Answer. The area is about 78.5 ft².

🧮 Area on the calculator

For r = 6: π × 6 x² enter gives 113.097… The calculator squares the 6 before it multiplies, as PEMDAS says. With 3.14 you get 113.04; both are close enough.

2Circumference or area: which one?

The circle has two different “measures” in this topic, each with its own formula. Mixing them up is one of the most common mistakes. The rule is simple: it depends on what you want to measure, the edge or the surface.

Circumference and area of a circle, side by side
Circumference (edge) ⭕Area (surface) 🟣
Whenyou measure the outline: fencing around a garden, trim around a table, how far a wheel rolls in one turnyou measure the inside: grass that covers a garden, how much pizza there is, the bottom of a round pool
FormulaC = π × d  or  C = 2 × π × rA = π × r²
Unitslinear: in, ft, ydsquare: in², ft², yd²

Word clues

Words like “around”, “fence”, “edge”, “border”, “trim” ask for the circumference. Words like “cover”, “paint”, “plant”, “carpet”, “fill the surface” ask for the area. And the units never lie: if the answer is in feet, it is the circumference; in square feet, it is the area.

3The reverse problem: the radius from the area

When you know the area and want the radius, solve the formula. Since the radius is squared, you need a square root at the end.

r² = A ÷ π     r = √(A ÷ π)

Example: from area to radiusA round garden has an area of 78.5 ft². What is its radius?

1Divide by π. r² ≈ 78.5 ÷ 3.14 = 25.

2Square root. r = √25 = 5.

✓Answer. The radius of the garden is 5 ft.

Connection with Topic 2

Here you use what you learned in Lesson 2.3 about square roots of perfect squares. When A ÷ π is a perfect square (25, 36, 49, 100…), the radius comes out whole and the work is quick.

4Composite figures with semicircles

A semicircle is half of a circle. Its area is half the area of the whole circle:

Asemicircle = (π × r²) ÷ 2

Semicircles show up in many real figures together with rectangles: a running track, an arched window, a stage with a curved front.

Example: the trackA track is made of a 100 ft × 60 ft rectangle and two semicircles, one on each short end, each with a diameter of 60 ft. What is the total area?

1Rectangle. 100 × 60 = 6,000 ft².

2Two semicircles = one whole circle. Radius: 60 ÷ 2 = 30 ft.

3Area of that circle. 3.14 × 30² = 3.14 × 900 = 2,826 ft².

4Total. 6,000 + 2,826 = 8,826 ft².

✓Answer. The track has a total area of 8,826 ft².

Handy shortcut

When a figure has two semicircles of the same size, put them together in your mind to make one whole circle. That saves dividing by 2 twice and lowers the chance of a mistake.

5Real uses

The area of a circle comes up every day: planting grass, painting a round wall, covering the bottom of a pool, sharing a pizza.

Example: planting grassA round flower bed has a radius of 4 ft. Sod costs $2 per square foot. How much does it cost to cover it all?

1Area. A ≈ 3.14 × 4² = 3.14 × 16 = 50.24 ft².

2Cost. 50.24 × $2 = $100.48.

✓Answer. Covering the flower bed costs about $100.48.

Example: a table with round endsA dining table is a 6 ft × 3 ft rectangle with a semicircle on each short end (diameter 3 ft). What is its total area?

1Rectangle. 6 × 3 = 18 ft².

2Two semicircles = one circle. Radius: 3 ÷ 2 = 1.5 ft. Area ≈ 3.14 × 1.5² = 3.14 × 2.25 = 7.065 ft².

3Total. 18 + 7.065 = 25.065 ft².

✓Answer. The table has an area of about 25.07 ft².

📘Key words

area of a circle
The surface inside a circle: A = π × r².
semicircle
Half a circle; its area is (π × r²) ÷ 2.
square root
The number that, squared, gives the original: √25 = 5.
in terms of π
An exact answer that keeps the π symbol, like 36π.
