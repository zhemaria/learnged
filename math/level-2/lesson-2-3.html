1The square root as the inverse operation

In Lesson 2.2 you learned to square numbers and you learned the fifteen perfect squares from 1 to 225. This lesson brings the inverse operation: the square root. Just as subtracting undoes adding, the square root undoes squaring.

The square root of a number answers the question: "What number times itself gives this number?" You write it with the radical sign: √.

If 72 = 49, then √49 = 7

The number under the radical sign is the radicand. If the radicand is a perfect square, its square root is a whole number.

Each multiplication of a number by itself and the square root it gives
Because……we know that
5 × 5 = 25√25 = 5
9 × 9 = 81√81 = 9
12 × 12 = 144√144 = 12
15 × 15 = 225√225 = 15

The direct strategy

To find the square root of a perfect square, remember which number squared gives it. The table of perfect squares from Lesson 2.2, read backward, is a table of square roots.

Watch out!

The square root is not half of the number. √64 is 8, not 32: 8 × 8 = 64.

2The square root as the side of a square

The square root has a clear meaning in geometry. You find the area of a square by squaring its side. So, if you know the area, the square root of the area gives you the side.

A square with an area of 36 square units has exactly 6 small squares on each side, because 6 × 6 = 36. You can see that √36 = 6: it is the length of the side.

Example: a square garden has an area of 121 square feet. How long is each side?

1Step 1. The side is the square root of the area: √121.

2Step 2. Look for the number that squared gives 121: 11 × 11 = 121.

✓Result. Each side of the garden is 11 feet long.

Area or perimeter?

To get the side from the area, take the square root. To get it from the perimeter, divide by 4. For a garden of 121 square feet, the side is 11 feet, not 121 ÷ 4.

3Estimating square roots that are not exact

Not every number is a perfect square. The square root of a number like 50 is not a whole number, but you can estimate it by placing it between two perfect squares next to each other.

Example: estimate √50

1Step 1. Find the perfect squares closest to 50: 49 and 64.

2Step 2. Since 49 < 50 < 64, then √49 < √50 < √64.

3Step 3. That is: 7 < √50 < 8. And 50 is very close to 49, so √50 is close to 7.

✓Result. √50 is between 7 and 8, closer to 7 (about 7.1).

Which one is it closer to?

Look at which perfect square the radicand is closer to. √50 is almost at √49, so its value is just above 7. But √60 is closer to √64, so its value is close to 8 (about 7.7).

4Checking: by squaring and on the calculator

Every square root can be checked with the inverse operation: if √144 = 12, then 12 × 12 must be 144. That way you catch a mistake without a calculator.

🧮 Square roots on the calculator

On the TI-30XS, the square root is above the x2 key. Press 2nd x2, type the radicand, for example 144, and press enter. The screen shows 12.

The calculator is for checking. On the GED test time is short: knowing that √81 = 9 by heart is much faster than typing it.

5Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. Write and solve ten exact square roots up to the square root of 225, four estimates between two whole numbers (say which one each root is closer to), and two problems that find the side of a square from its area. Check every root by squaring.
  • In your daily life. Find something square around you: a floor tile, a window, a table. Measure one side in inches, find the area, and then check that the square root of the area gives the side back.

📘Key words

square root
The number that times itself gives the radicand: the square root of 49 is 7.
radical sign
The symbol √.
radicand
The number under the radical sign.
inverse operations
Two operations that undo each other, like squaring and taking the square root.
estimate
To find a value that is close, not exact: the square root of 50 is about 7.1.
