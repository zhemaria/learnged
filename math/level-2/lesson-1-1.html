Math · Level 2 · Topic 1 — Integers

Lesson 1.1 · The number line, absolute value, and opposites

Numbers to the left of zero: place them on the number line, compare them, and measure how far they are from zero.

⏱️ About 4 hours with practice and homework · take breaks 🎯 No calculator

🎯What you will learn

When you finish this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Place positive and negative integers on the number line.
  • Compare and order integers with the symbols >, <, and =.
  • Find the absolute value of a number, and explain it as a distance from zero.
  • Name the opposite (additive inverse) of any integer.
  • Use integers to describe real situations: temperature, money, and height.

1The full number line

Welcome to Level 2. In Level 1 you worked with numbers from zero up: you read them, compared them, and operated with them. Now the world of numbers grows to the left of zero. Negative numbers describe everything that is "below" a starting point: a temperature below zero, a debt, a depth below sea level.

The number line is a line where every point is a number. Zero is in the center. The positive numbers (1, 2, 3, …) are to its right, and the negative numbers (−1, −2, −3, …) are to its left. Together, the whole numbers, their negatives, and zero are called integers.

Three ideas about the number line:

  • The numbers grow to the right. Any number is greater than every number to its left. That is why −2 is greater than −5, even though "5" looks bigger than "2": −2 is closer to zero, so it is farther to the right.
  • The sign shows the direction. When the minus sign is in front of a number alone, it does not mean "subtract": it means the number is to the left of zero. You read −8 as "negative eight."
  • Each number has one place. So you can compare any two integers just by seeing which one is farther to the right.
In real life

A thermometer is a number line standing up. Zero is a reference point; degrees above zero are positive and degrees below zero are negative. A temperature of −10 °F is lower, and colder, than −3 °F.

Example: order from least to greatest: −4, 3, −7, 0, −1

1Step 1. Picture the numbers on the line. The negatives are to the left of 0, and the positives are to the right.

2Step 2. The one farthest to the left is the least: −7.

3Step 3. Move to the right: −7, −4, −1, 0, 3.

✓Result. −7 < −4 < −1 < 0 < 3

Watch out!

Between two negative numbers, the one with the bigger "number" is the smaller one: −9 < −2. Ask yourself which one is closer to zero. The one closer to zero is greater.

2Absolute value: the distance from zero

The absolute value of a number is its distance from zero on the number line. A distance is never negative, so the absolute value is always positive or zero.

You write it with two vertical bars around the number, and you read it "the absolute value of."

Examples of absolute value and what each one means
You writeIt isBecause
|5|55 is 5 units from zero.
|−5|5−5 is also 5 units from zero.
|0|0zero is 0 units from itself.

Watch out!

The absolute value measures distance. That is why |−12| = 12 and |12| = 12: both numbers are exactly the same distance from zero, on opposite sides. But a minus sign outside the bars stays: −|−6| means "the opposite of |−6|," so −|−6| = −6.

Compare sizes with the absolute value

The absolute value tells you the size of a quantity, whatever its sign. A debt of $80 (a balance of −$80) is a bigger debt than one of $30 (−$30), because |−80| > |−30|. But on the number line, −80 is the smaller number.

Example: |−7| + |4|

1Step 1. |−7| = 7, the distance from −7 to zero.

2Step 2. |4| = 4, the distance from 4 to zero.

✓Result. 7 + 4 = 11

3Opposites

Two numbers are opposites when they are the same distance from zero but on opposite sides of the number line. The opposite of 6 is −6, and the opposite of −6 is 6. Zero is its own opposite: it is not on either side.

Key idea

A number and its opposite always add up to zero: 6 + (−6) = 0. That is why the opposite is also called the additive inverse. You will use this idea in the next lesson to add integers.

Example: the opposite and the absolute value

1Step 1. The opposite of −13 is 13. Both have an absolute value of 13.

2Step 2. The opposite of 40 is −40. Both have an absolute value of 40.

✓Result. Two opposites always have the same absolute value.

Two minus signs

A minus sign in front of a number means "the opposite of." So −(−8) means "the opposite of −8," which is 8. Two minus signs in a row cancel each other.

Example: simplify −(−25)

1Step 1. −(−25) means "the opposite of −25."

✓Result. The opposite of −25 is 25.

🧮 Negative numbers on the calculator

The calculator has two different minus keys. − subtracts. (−) writes a negative number. To find −(−25), press (−) ( (−) 25 ) enter. The screen shows 25.

If you press − to start a negative number, the calculator shows an error. On the GED test, use (−) for every negative number.

4Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. Write and solve ten exercises that compare or order integers with >, <, and =; five that find an absolute value, like |−8| + |3|; and five that find an opposite, including some with two minus signs, like −(−9). Check each one on a number line you draw.
  • In your daily life. Find three situations in your week that use negative numbers: a weather report, a bank balance, a basement floor. Write each one as an integer, and say what zero means in each case.

📘Key words

integers
The whole numbers, their opposites, and zero: …, −3, −2, −1, 0, 1, 2, 3, …
positive number
A number greater than zero. It is to the right of zero on the number line.
negative number
A number less than zero. It is to the left of zero and has a minus sign: −4.
number line
A line where every point is a number. The numbers grow to the right.
absolute value
The distance from a number to zero. It is never negative: |−5| = 5.
opposite (additive inverse)
The number at the same distance from zero, on the other side. A number plus its opposite is 0.
