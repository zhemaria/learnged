1The four levels of PEMDAS

Welcome to Topic 3. In Topics 1 and 2 you built separate tools: integers, fractions with signs, powers, and square roots. In Topic 3 all those pieces come together under one set of rules: the order of operations. Without it, the same expression could give different answers depending on who works it. With it, everyone gets exactly the same answer.

The word PEMDAS sums up the order. Each letter is a stage, and the stages always go in the same order:

The four levels of the order of operations, from the first to the last
LevelLettersWhat you do
1PParentheses and other grouping symbols. Whatever is inside goes first.
2EExponents and roots. Find any power or root before you use its value.
3MDMultiplication and division, in the order they appear, from left to right. Neither one goes before the other.
4ASAddition and subtraction, in the order they appear, from left to right. Neither one goes before the other.

↔ Slide the table sideways to see all the columns.

Key idea

PEMDAS has six letters, but there are only four levels. M and D share a level, and so do A and S. Mixing this up causes most mistakes. Some people remember the order with the sentence "Please Excuse My Dear Aunt Sally."

The most common mistake

Thinking that multiplication always goes before division because "M comes first." It does not: in 20 ÷ 4 × 5, divide first because it is farther left (20 ÷ 4 = 5; 5 × 5 = 25). Multiplying first would give 20 ÷ 20 = 1, which is wrong.

2Step by step

The safest way is to copy the expression at every step, solving only the operation with the highest priority and leaving the rest as it is. That way you can clearly see the next move.

Example: 6 + 3 × 4 − 2

1MD. No parentheses and no exponents. The multiplication goes first: 6 + 3 × 4 − 2 → 6 + 12 − 2.

2AS. Add and subtract from left to right: 6 + 12 − 2 → 18 − 2.

✓Result. 18 − 2 = 16

Example: (8 − 3)2 + 12 ÷ 4

1P. Inside the parentheses: 8 − 3 = 5 → 52 + 12 ÷ 4.

2E. The exponent: 52 = 25 → 25 + 12 ÷ 4.

3MD. The division: 12 ÷ 4 = 3 → 25 + 3.

✓AS. 25 + 3 = 28

3Nested grouping symbols: from the inside out

When an expression has grouping symbols inside other grouping symbols, always work from the innermost to the outermost.

To tell them apart at a glance, we use three kinds of grouping symbols:

  • Parentheses ( ): the innermost level.
  • Brackets [ ]: the middle level.
  • Braces { }: the outermost level.

They all work exactly like parentheses: only the shape changes, so it is easier to match each opening symbol with its closing one.

Example: two levels   2 × [3 + (4 × 2)]

1Step 1. The inner parentheses: 4 × 2 = 8 → 2 × [3 + 8].

2Step 2. The brackets: 3 + 8 = 11 → 2 × 11.

✓Result. 2 × 11 = 22

Example: three levels   50 − {4 + [3 × (6 − 2)]}

1Step 1. Inner parentheses: 6 − 2 = 4.

2Step 2. Brackets: 3 × 4 = 12.

3Step 3. Braces: 4 + 12 = 16.

✓Result. 50 − 16 = 34

A visual strategy

Before you start, count and match the pairs. Each ( needs a ), each [ needs a ], and each { needs a }. This check prevents the most frustrating mistakes.

On the calculator

The calculator has only round parentheses. Type brackets and braces as parentheses too: 2 × [3 + (4 × 2)] is 2 × ((3 + (4 × 2))).

4Putting it all together

The plan for any expression is always the same: first the grouping symbols, from the inside out, and inside each one, use PEMDAS from the start. When all the grouping symbols are gone, use PEMDAS on what is left.

Example:   [22 + (15 − 3 × 4)] × 5

1Step 1. Inside the parentheses, multiply before subtracting: 3 × 4 = 12 → (15 − 12).

2Step 2. Finish the parentheses: 15 − 12 = 3 → [22 + 3] × 5.

3Step 3. Inside the brackets, the exponent before the addition: 22 = 4 → [4 + 3] × 5.

4Step 4. Finish the brackets: 4 + 3 = 7 → 7 × 5.

✓Result. 7 × 5 = 35

Watch out!

With grouping symbols, you do not use PEMDAS "across the whole expression" at once. Each grouping symbol is a small expression that you finish completely before you move to the next one.

5Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. Write and solve five expressions with the four operations (no parentheses), five with parentheses and an exponent, and five with nested grouping symbols. Write one line for each step, and underline the operation you do in that step.
  • In your daily life. Look at a receipt or a bill: 3 items at $4 plus a $5 fee. Write it as one expression, like 3 × 4 + 5, and solve it in the right order. Then check that the bill says the same.

📘Key words

order of operations
The rules that say which operation to do first, so everyone gets the same answer.
PEMDAS
Parentheses, Exponents, Multiplication and Division, Addition and Subtraction.
grouping symbols
Parentheses ( ), brackets [ ], and braces { }. What is inside goes first.
nested
One grouping symbol inside another. Work from the inside out.
left to right
The way to work two operations on the same level.
