Lesson 6.1 · Simple interest: I = P × r × t

Percents over time: interest on savings and loans with I = P × r × t, the total amount, matching the rate and the time, and solving for any value.

⏱️ About 4 hours with practice and homework · take breaks 🧮 Calculator to check

🎯What you will learn

When you finish this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Name the five quantities: P, r, t, I, and A.
  • Find the interest with I = P × r × t and the total amount with A = P + I.
  • Change the rate to a decimal and the time to the same unit as the rate.
  • Describe simple interest as growth in equal steps.
  • Solve for the principal, the rate, or the time.

1The bank and me: two sides of interest

In Lesson 5.3 you used percents for things that happen once: a discount, a tax, a tip. This lesson, the first of Topic 6, takes the next step: using a percent over time. Each year that money stays in an account or in a loan, the same percent is applied again.

Before any formula, look at two familiar situations. In both there is interest, but from opposite sides.

Two sides of interest: when you save and when you borrow

The key idea

You just figured simple interest without a formula. First you found the percent of the starting money for one year; then you multiplied by the number of years. The formula in section 3 just writes this idea with letters.

3The simple-interest formula

I = P × r × t

I = interest · P = principal · r = yearly rate as a decimal · t = time in years

A = P + I

The total amount is the principal plus the interest.

Example: find the interestHow much interest do $3,500 earn at 4% simple interest a year for 6 years?

1Data. P = 3,500; r = 4% = 0.04; t = 6.

2Formula. I = 3,500 × 0.04 × 6.

3In parts. 3,500 × 0.04 = 140 (one year's interest); 140 × 6 = 840.

✓Answer. The interest is $840.

Example: total amount of a savings accountWith the same data, how much money is in the account at the end of the 6 years?

1A = P + I. A = 3,500 + 840 = 4,340.

✓Answer. $4,340

Example: total amount of a loanA $1,800 loan has 12% simple interest a year and is paid back in 2 years. How much is paid back in all?

1Interest. I = 1,800 × 0.12 × 2 = 432.

2Total. A = 1,800 + 432 = 2,232.

✓Answer. $2,232. The loan costs $432.

4The rate and the time in the same unit

The formula only works if the rate is a decimal and the rate and the time use the same unit. If the rate is per year, the time must be in years. This is where most mistakes happen.

Percent to decimal: divide by 100 (move the point two places to the left): 3% = 0.03 · 4.5% = 0.045 · 12% = 0.12 · 0.5% = 0.005.

When the time is in days, divide by 365. Some banks use a “banker's year” of 360 days; the problem must say which one to use.

Example: time in monthsA $4,000 loan at 6% a year is paid back in 9 months. How much interest is paid?

1Change the time. 9 ÷ 12 = 0.75 years.

2Formula. I = 4,000 × 0.06 × 0.75 = 180.

✓Answer. $180

Common mistake

Using t = 9 without changing it gives I = 4,000 × 0.06 × 9 = $2,160: more than half the loan in only 9 months. Before you answer, ask yourself whether the result makes sense.

Example: a monthly rateA store finances a $1,200 purchase at 2% a month for 6 months. How much interest is paid?

AWay A. The rate is monthly and the time is in months: they already match. I = 1,200 × 0.02 × 6 = 144.

BWay B. Yearly rate = 2% × 12 = 24%; time = 6 ÷ 12 = 0.5 years. I = 1,200 × 0.24 × 0.5 = 144.

✓Answer. $144 either way. What matters is that the rate and the time match.

5Growth year by year

With simple interest, each year's interest is always the same, because it is figured on the original principal. That is why the money grows in equal steps: linear growth.

6Solving for P, r, or t

Sometimes what you do not know is not the interest but another value: how much to deposit, what rate a loan charges, or how long to wait. With I = P × r × t you can find any of the four quantities if you know the other three.

The formula solved for each quantity
You wantFormula
the principalP = I ÷ (r × t)
the rater = I ÷ (P × t), then × 100 for the percent
the timet = I ÷ (P × r)

↔ Slide the table sideways to see all the columns.

One rule for all three

Divide the interest by the product of the other two values. If the problem gives the total amount A instead of the interest, first find I = A − P.

Example: find the rateA $2,000 loan for 4 years cost $560 in interest. What yearly rate was charged?

1r = I ÷ (P × t). 560 ÷ (2,000 × 4) = 560 ÷ 8,000 = 0.07.

✓To a percent. 0.07 × 100 = 7% a year.

Example: find the timeHow long must $1,200 stay at 5% a year to earn $150 in interest?

1t = I ÷ (P × r). 150 ÷ (1,200 × 0.05) = 150 ÷ 60 = 2.5.

✓Answer. 2.5 years, that is, 2 years and 6 months.

Example: find the principalHow much must you deposit at 4% a year to earn $360 in 3 years?

1P = I ÷ (r × t). 360 ÷ (0.04 × 3) = 360 ÷ 0.12 = 3,000.

2Check. 3,000 × 0.04 × 3 = 360. ✓

✓Answer. $3,000

Example: when you are given the total amountFor a $4,000 loan, $4,960 was paid back in all after 2 years. What yearly rate was charged?

1First the interest. I = A − P = 4,960 − 4,000 = 960.

2r = I ÷ (P × t). 960 ÷ (4,000 × 2) = 960 ÷ 8,000 = 0.12.

✓Answer. 12% a year

7Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. Find the interest I in four problems (two with the time in months). Find the total amount A = P + I in three problems, with savings and with loans. Solve three problems for the principal, the rate, or the time.
  • In your daily life. Look at a real offer: a savings account, a store financing plan, or a loan ad. Write down P, r, and t, check whether the rate and the time use the same unit, and find the interest.

📘Key words

principal (P)
The starting money you deposit or borrow.
simple interest
Interest figured only on the principal: the same amount every year.
rate (r)
The percent per period, written as a decimal in the formula.
total amount (A)
Principal plus interest: A = P + I.
