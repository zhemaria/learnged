Math · Level 2 · Topic 5 — Percents

Lesson 5.3 · Discount, tax, tip, and mixed applications

Percents in daily life: sale prices, sales tax, tips, and purchases that combine several percents.

⏱️ About 4 hours with practice and homework · take breaks 🧮 Calculator to check

🎯What you will learn

When you finish this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Find a sale price by subtracting or with the factor (1 − r).
  • Find a total with sales tax using the factor (1 + r), and round to the cent.
  • Figure a 15% to 20% tip in your head.
  • Apply chained percents to the value at each moment, and know when the order matters.

1Start with a receipt

In Lesson 5.2 you met the change factor: multiply by (1 + r) to increase and by (1 − r) to decrease. This lesson, which closes Topic 5, uses it in the three most common percent situations in daily life: discounts, sales tax, and tips, and in the cases where they come together.

Before learning the steps, look at a receipt and check its numbers. This receipt is made up:

SAVE-MORE STORE
Jacket$60.00
Discount 25%−$15.00
T-shirt$12.00
Subtotal$57.00
Tax 8.25%$4.70
TOTAL$61.70

How was each line found? 25% of $60 is $15. The subtotal is 60 − 15 + 12 = $57. The tax is 8.25% of 57 = 4.7025, which rounds to $4.70. The total is 57 + 4.70 = $61.70.

About rounding

Money is written with two decimal places (cents). Round at the end of each calculation, not in the middle steps. If the third decimal digit is 5 or more, round up one cent.

The tax rate depends on where you are

Each state, county, and city sets its own rate, and it can change from year to year or by type of product. This lesson uses made-up rates; in real life, the rate is printed on the receipt.

2Discount and sales tax

A discount takes a percent off the original price. There are two ways that give the same answer.

Example: two methodsA pair of shoes costs $85 and has a 20% discount. What is the final price?

AFind it and subtract. Discount = 0.20 × 85 = $17; final price = 85 − 17 = $68.

BChange factor. With 20% off, you pay 80%: 85 × 0.80 = $68.

✓Answer. $68

Sales tax

Tax adds a percent to the price. With the change factor: final price = price × (1 + rate).

Example: sales taxA $120 purchase has an 8% tax. What is the total?

AFind it and add. Tax = 0.08 × 120 = $9.60; total = 120 + 9.60 = $129.60.

BFactor. 120 × 1.08 = $129.60.

✓Answer. $129.60

🧮 Discount and tax on the calculator

For the shoes, type 85 × 0.80 enter: 68. For the purchase with tax, type 120 × 1.08 enter: 129.6, which you write as $129.60.

3Tips

In the United States, people usually leave a tip at restaurants and for other services. It is usually between 15% and 20% of the bill, most often before tax. It is a great calculation to do in your head:

Example: a tip in your head on $48

110%. Move the decimal point one place to the left: $4.80.

25%. Half of 10%: $2.40.

315%. 10% + 5% = 4.80 + 2.40 = $7.20.

420%. Twice 10%: $9.60.

✓Answer. A fair tip is between $7.20 and $9.60.

4Chained percents

Many purchases combine two or more percents. The golden rule: each percent is applied to the value at that moment, not to the original price.

Example: discount and then taxA $600 TV has a 15% discount. An 8.25% tax is charged on the sale price. How much do you pay?

1Discount. 600 × 0.85 = $510.

2Tax on the sale price. 510 × 1.0825 = 552.075.

3Round to the cent. $552.08.

✓Answer. $552.08. In one step: 600 × 0.85 × 1.0825.

Does the order matter?

When both changes are percents, the order does not change the answer, because you are multiplying factors: 600 × 0.85 × 1.0825 = 600 × 1.0825 × 0.85. What does matter is this:

Mistake 1 · Adding the percents

A 20% discount followed by an extra 10% is not 30%. On $50: 50 × 0.80 = 40; 40 × 0.90 = $36. A 30% discount would give $35. The real discount is 1 − (0.80 × 0.90) = 1 − 0.72 = 28%.

Mistake 2 · Forgetting that a dollar coupon does depend on the order

When you combine a percent with a fixed dollar amount, the order changes the answer. With a $60 price, a $10 coupon, and 20% off: coupon first, (60 − 10) × 0.80 = $40; percent first, 60 × 0.80 − 10 = $38. That is why stores say in what order they apply their offers.

📘Key words

discount
A percent taken off the price. Factor: 1 − r.
sales tax
A percent added to the price. Factor: 1 + r.
tip
Money left for a service, usually 15% to 20% of the bill before tax.
chained percents
Two or more percents applied one after the other, each on the current value.
