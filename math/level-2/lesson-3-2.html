1PEMDAS with powers and roots

In Lesson 3.1 you learned the four levels of PEMDAS and worked nested grouping symbols with positive whole numbers. This lesson brings in every tool from Level 2: powers, square roots, fractions, and integers. The order does not change; only the ingredients are richer.

In PEMDAS, the E for "exponents" also includes roots. Both share the second level: right after the grouping symbols, and before any multiplication, division, addition, or subtraction.

Example: 3 + √25 × 2

1E. The root shares the level of exponents, so it goes first: √25 = 5 → 3 + 5 × 2.

2MD. Multiply before adding: 5 × 2 = 10 → 3 + 10.

✓AS. 3 + 10 = 13

Example: 23 + √16 + 9

1P. Under the radical sign, add first: 16 + 9 = 25 → 23 + √25.

2E. The power and the root: 23 = 8 and √25 = 5 → 8 + 5.

✓AS. 8 + 5 = 13

The radical sign groups

Everything under the √ sign is inside invisible parentheses. That is why √16 + 9 is not the same as √16 + √9. The first one is √25 = 5; the second is 4 + 3 = 7.

2The fraction bar groups

Like the radical sign, the fraction bar groups what is above it and what is below it. Work out the numerator and the denominator separately, and divide at the end.

Example: 32 + 72 × 4(32 + 7) ÷ (2 × 4)

1Numerator. 32 + 7 = 9 + 7 = 16 (E before A).

2Denominator. 2 × 4 = 8.

✓Divide. 16 ÷ 8 = 2

On one line or on the calculator

When you write a fraction on one line, put parentheses around the numerator and around the denominator: (32 + 7) ÷ (2 × 4). Without them, the calculator reads 32 + 7 ÷ 2 × 4, which gives 23, a completely different answer.

🧮 Fractions with expressions on the calculator

3PEMDAS with negative numbers

When minus signs appear, the key is to tell when the sign is part of the number and when it means a subtraction or an opposite.

Example: −6 + 2 × (−4)

1MD. Multiplication before addition. Different signs, negative: 2 × (−4) = −8 → −6 + (−8).

✓AS. Same signs (both negative): −6 + (−8) = −14

A negative base with an exponent

The difference between (−5)2 and −52 is one of the finest points of PEMDAS. Look at it carefully.

The rule

An exponent applies only to what is right before it. With no parentheses, the minus sign is out of the exponent's reach: E goes first, and the minus sign is applied after.

🧮 Negative bases on the calculator

For (−5)2, type the parentheses: ( (−) 5 ) x2 enter gives 25. If you type (−) 5 x2 enter, the calculator reads −52 and gives −25.

4Bringing it all together

An expression that brings it all together uses tools from all three topics of the level in one calculation. You solve it exactly like the others: PEMDAS, step by step, copying each line.

Example: (−3)2 + √64 ÷ 2 − 525/2 × 4

1E. The power and the root: (−3)2 = 9 and √64 = 8 → 9 + 8 ÷ 2 − 5/2 × 4.

2MD. From left to right: 8 ÷ 2 = 4, and 5/2 × 4 = 20/2 = 10 → 9 + 4 − 10.

3AS. From left to right: 9 + 4 = 13; 13 − 10 = 3.

5Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. Write and solve five expressions with powers and roots, five with a fraction bar, five with negative numbers (include (−a)2 and −a2), and five that bring everything together. Then check two of them on the calculator, with the parentheses in the right places.
  • In your daily life. Ask someone to solve 10 − 2 × 3 and −32 without a calculator. Compare their answers with the order of operations, and explain the right answers to them.

📘Key words

radical sign
The symbol √. What is under it is grouped, as if in parentheses.
fraction bar
The line of a fraction. It groups the numerator and the denominator and means division.
base
The number that is raised to a power. In (−5)2 the base is −5; in −52 it is 5.
expression
Numbers and operations written together, like (−3)2 + 8 ÷ 2.
