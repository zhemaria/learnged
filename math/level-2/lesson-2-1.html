Lesson 2.1 · Fractions with negative signs

The same sign rules as for integers, now with fractions: the three forms of a negative fraction and the four operations.

⏱️ About 4 hours with practice and homework · take breaks 🧮 Calculator to check

🎯What you will learn

When you finish this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Recognize the three equal forms of a negative fraction.
  • Decide the sign of a fraction, including one with two minus signs.
  • Multiply and divide fractions with signs: first the sign, then the value.
  • Add and subtract fractions with signs using a common denominator.
  • Combine an integer and a fraction, and write the answer as a fraction or a mixed number.

1The three forms of a negative fraction

Welcome to Topic 2. In Topic 1 you learned the key rule: same signs give a positive answer; different signs give a negative answer. That same rule opens this topic. In Level 1 fractions were always positive; now they get a sign. The good news: there are no new sign rules. Fractions follow exactly the same rules as integers.

A negative fraction can be written in three ways, and all three are the same number:

−34−3/4  =  −34−3/4  =  3−43/−4

The minus sign can go in front of the fraction, in the numerator, or in the denominator: the value does not change. Why? Because a fraction is a division, and −3 ÷ 4 and 3 ÷ (−4) both have different signs, so both are negative.

The usual way

We usually write the minus sign in front of the fraction or in the numerator. A sign in the denominator is correct, but it is easy to misread.

Two minus signs make a positive fraction

If the numerator and the denominator are both negative, the fraction is positive: the two signs cancel, just as in dividing integers.

Example: simplify the sign of −6−8−6/−8

1Step 1. A fraction is a division: −6 ÷ (−8).

2Step 2. Same signs (both negative): the answer is positive.

3Step 3. Simplify the value: 6/8 = 3/4.

✓Result. −6−8−6/−8 = 343/4

2Multiplying and dividing fractions with signs

To multiply or divide fractions with signs, work in two separate steps: first decide the sign of the answer; then operate with the absolute values, as you did in Level 1.

How to find the value and how to decide the sign when you multiply or divide fractions
OperationHow to find the valueHow to decide the sign
Multiplyingnumerator × numerator, denominator × denominatorsign rule: same (+), different (−)
Dividingmultiply by the reciprocal of the second fractionsign rule: same (+), different (−)

Example: multiply (−34−3/4) × (−25−2/5)

1The sign. Both fractions are negative: same signs, so the answer is positive.

2The value. Multiply the numerators (3 × 2 = 6) and the denominators (4 × 5 = 20): 6/20.

3Simplify. 6/20 = 3/10.

✓Result. 3103/10

Example: divide 232/3 ÷ (−49−4/9)

1The sign. One positive and one negative: different signs, so the answer is negative.

2The reciprocal. Dividing is multiplying by the reciprocal: 2/3 × 9/4.

3The value. 2 × 9 = 18 and 3 × 4 = 12: 18/12 = 3/2.

✓Result. −32−3/2 (or −1 1/2)

Watch out!

When you flip the second fraction to find its reciprocal, the sign does not get lost: you already decided it in step 1. That is why it helps to decide the sign first.

3Adding and subtracting fractions with signs

To add or subtract fractions with signs, you need a common denominator, just as in Level 1. Once the denominators are the same, add or subtract the numerators with the rule for adding integers from Lesson 1.2.

Example: add (−56−5/6) + 141/4

1Step 1. The common denominator of 6 and 4 is 12.

2Step 2. Rewrite: −5/6 = −10/12 and 1/4 = 3/12.

3Step 3. Add the numerators as integers: −10 + 3 = −7.

✓Result. −712−7/12

Example: subtract 131/3 − (−25−2/5)

1Step 1. Subtracting a negative fraction is adding its opposite: 1/3 + 2/5.

2Step 2. The common denominator of 3 and 5 is 15: 1/3 = 5/15 and 2/5 = 6/15.

3Step 3. Add the numerators: 5 + 6 = 11.

✓Result. 111511/15

Remember

"Subtracting is adding the opposite," from Lesson 1.2, works the same way with fractions. Subtracting −2/5 is adding +2/5: the two minus signs in a row become a plus sign.

4Expressions with an integer and a fraction

Some expressions mix an integer and a fraction with signs. Write the integer as a fraction with denominator 1, and then use everything above.

Example: −2 + (−34−3/4)

1Step 1. Write −2 with denominator 4: −2 = −8/4.

2Step 2. Add the numerators as integers: −8 + (−3) = −11.

3Step 3. The answer is −11/4. As a mixed number: −2 3/4.

✓Result. −114−11/4 = −2 34−2 3/4

A negative mixed number

−2 3/4 means −(2 + 3/4): the minus sign applies to the whole number and to the fraction. It is not −2 + 3/4.

📘Key words

negative fraction
A fraction less than zero. Its sign can go in front, in the numerator, or in the denominator: −3/4.
reciprocal
The fraction flipped: the reciprocal of 4/9 is 9/4. A number times its reciprocal is 1.
common denominator
A denominator that two fractions can share. You need it to add or subtract.
improper fraction
A fraction whose numerator is greater than or equal to its denominator in absolute value, like −11/4.
mixed number
An integer and a fraction together. −2 3/4 means −(2 + 3/4).
