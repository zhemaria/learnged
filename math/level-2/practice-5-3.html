Lesson 5.3 · Discount, tax, tip, and mixed applications

Everyday applications

›

A discount is subtracted from the price; sales tax and a tip are added; a commission is a percent of the sales. In every case, first find the percent of the amount, then decide whether to add it, subtract it, or leave it alone.

Practice: at the store and the restaurant

Type only the number. It may have cents, like 9.90; you may use the $ sign.

The tip in your head

›

To find 10%, move the decimal point one place to the left. 5% is half of that, 15% is 10% + 5%, and 20% is twice 10%.

Figure the tip step by step

Practice: tips

Type only the number. It may have cents, like 9.90; you may use the $ sign.

3

Chained percents

›

When a purchase has several percents, apply each one to the value at that moment. Two discounts in a row are not added: you multiply their factors.

Shopping simulator

Practice: chained purchases

Type only the number, with no % sign. It may have decimals; for money you may use the $ sign.

Does the order matter? The dollar coupon

›

With two percents, the order does not change the answer. But with a percent and a fixed dollar coupon, it does: the coupon is worth more if it comes off at the end.
