1Adding on the number line

In Lesson 1.1 you learned two tools: every number has an opposite, and the absolute value is the distance from zero. Now you will use them to add and subtract integers.

You can think of adding as moving on the number line. Start at the first number, then move:

  • To add a positive number, move to the right.
  • To add a negative number, move to the left.

−5 + 8: start at −5 and move 8 places to the right. You land on 3.

2 + (−6): start at 2 and move 6 places to the left. You land on −4.

Parentheses

When a negative number comes after a + or − sign, we write it in parentheses so the two signs do not run together: 2 + (−6), not 2 + −6.

2Two-color counters

Another way to see addition is with two-color counters. A yellow counter (+) is worth +1, and a red counter (−) is worth −1. One yellow and one red together make a zero pair, because +1 + (−1) = 0.

Example: −6 + 4 with counters

1Step 1. Put down 6 red counters (−6) and 4 yellow counters (+4).

2Step 2. Match each yellow counter with a red one. That makes 4 zero pairs. Take them away: they are worth 0.

3Step 3. 2 red counters are left without a partner.

✓Result. −6 + 4 = −2

Why it works

Zero pairs do not change the total, so you can take them away. What is left is the answer: if red counters are left, the answer is negative; if yellow counters are left, it is positive.

3The rule for adding integers

The number line and the counters lead to a rule. With it, you can add without drawing:

The rule for adding integers: what to do with the absolute values and what sign the answer gets
CaseWhat to do with the absolute valuesSign of the answer
Same signAdd them.The sign they share.
Different signsSubtract the smaller from the larger.The sign of the number with the larger absolute value.

↔ Slide the table sideways to see all the columns.

Example: same sign: −9 + (−7)

1Step 1. Both numbers are negative: same sign.

2Step 2. Add the absolute values: 9 + 7 = 16.

3Step 3. Keep the sign they share: negative.

✓Result. −9 + (−7) = −16

Example: different signs: −13 + 8

1Step 1. One is negative and one is positive: different signs.

2Step 2. |−13| = 13 and |8| = 8. Subtract: 13 − 8 = 5.

3Step 3. 13 is the larger absolute value, and −13 is negative: the answer is negative.

✓Result. −13 + 8 = −5

The most common mistake

With different signs, do not add the absolute values. In the example above, adding would give −21, which is wrong. With different signs, you always subtract.

4Subtracting is adding the opposite

Subtracting integers becomes easy with one change: subtracting a number is the same as adding its opposite.

a − b = a + (−b)

Once the subtraction is an addition, use the addition rule from section 3. There are no new rules to memorize.

Example: 5 − 12

1Step 1. Change it to adding the opposite: 5 + (−12).

2Step 2. Different signs: 12 − 5 = 7.

3Step 3. The larger absolute value, 12, belongs to a negative number: the answer is negative.

✓Result. 5 − 12 = −7

Example: subtract a negative: −4 − (−9)

1Step 1. Subtracting −9 is adding its opposite, +9: −4 + 9.

2Step 2. Different signs: 9 − 4 = 5.

3Step 3. The larger absolute value, 9, is positive: the answer is positive.

✓Result. −4 − (−9) = 5

Two minus signs

Two minus signs in a row become a plus sign: "− (−9)" becomes "+ 9". In real life, taking away a debt is like receiving money.

🧮 Two minus keys

For −4 − (−9), the first and the last minus signs are negative numbers, and the one in the middle subtracts. Press (−) 4 − (−) 9 enter. The screen shows 5.

5Real-life problems

Adding and subtracting integers describes many everyday situations. The key is to give each amount the right sign:

Words that usually mean a positive amount and words that usually mean a negative amount
Positive (+)Negative (−)
rises, goes up, above zerodrops, goes down, below zero
deposit, gain, incomewithdrawal, loss, debt, overdraft
above sea level, moves forwardbelow sea level, moves back

↔ Slide the table sideways to see all the columns.

Example: temperature

1The problem. At 6 a.m. the temperature was −8 °F. By noon it had risen 15 degrees. What was the temperature at noon?

2The signs. "Risen 15 degrees" is +15: −8 + 15.

3Add. Different signs: 15 − 8 = 7, with the sign of 15 (positive).

✓Result. At noon it was 7 °F.

Example: a bank balance

1The problem. An account has a balance of −$45 (it is overdrawn). The owner deposits $30. What is the new balance?

2The signs. The overdraft is −45 and the deposit is +30: −45 + 30.

3Add. Different signs: 45 − 30 = 15, with the sign of −45 (negative).

✓Result. The balance is −$15. The account is still overdrawn.

6Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. Write and solve four additions with the same sign, four with different signs, and four subtractions. Rewrite each subtraction as an addition of the opposite before you solve it. Check two of them with counters you draw.
  • In your daily life. Write two problems from your life, one about temperature and one about money (a deposit, a payment, a debt). Write each one as an addition or a subtraction with signs, and solve it.

📘Key words

sum
The answer to an addition.
difference
The answer to a subtraction.
zero pair
A +1 and a −1 together. They add up to 0.
same sign / different signs
Two numbers that are both positive or both negative / one positive and one negative.
adding the opposite
The way to subtract integers: a − b = a + (−b).
overdraft
A bank balance below zero: the account owes money.
