Lesson 4.2 · Adding and subtracting fractions

Four practice activities on one page. The exercises change every time. If you make a mistake, you get a hint and another try.

1

The same denominator

›

The denominator tells the size of the parts; the numerator tells how many there are. If the parts are the same size, add (or subtract) the numerators and keep the denominator: 2 fourths of a pizza plus 1 fourth are 3 fourths.

Join the pieces

The most common mistake: adding the denominators too. 2/5 + 1/5 is not 3/10: the size of the parts does not change when you join them. Besides, 3/10 is less than 2/5, and adding should give you more.

Practice: the same denominator

Type the fraction with a slash, for example 3/5.

Different denominators

›

If the parts are different sizes, first make the denominators the same. Go through the multiples of the larger denominator until you find one that the smaller one also goes into. If you do not find one, multiplying the two denominators always works. Then change each fraction to an equivalent fraction, and add or subtract the numerators.

Practice: which common denominator?

Practice: different denominators

Type the fraction with a slash, in simplest form. If it is more than 1, you can type a mixed number with a space, like 1 1/4.

🧮 Check it on the calculator

Write the first fraction with n/d (numerator, ▼, denominator, ►), press + or −, write the second fraction the same way, and press enter. The calculator shows the answer in simplest form. Solve on paper first, then check.

3

Simplify the result

›

Leave the answer in simplest form: 3/6 simplifies to 1/2. And if the result is more than 1, like 7/4, you can also write it as a mixed number: 7 ÷ 4 = 1 with remainder 3, so 7/4 = 1 3/4.

Practice: calculate and simplify

Type a fraction in simplest form (3/4), a mixed number with a space (1 3/4), or a whole number.
