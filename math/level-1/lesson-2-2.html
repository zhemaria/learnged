1What does dividing mean?

In Lesson 2.1 you learned to multiply. Division is its opposite operation. Multiplying puts equal groups together. Dividing splits a total into equal groups, or finds how many groups fit.

Imagine you have 24 chairs and you want to set them up in rows of 6. The question "How many rows can you make?" is a division: 24 ÷ 6 = 4 rows.

Words to know

In 24 ÷ 6 = 4: 24 is the dividend (the total you split), 6 is the divisor (the size of each group, or how many groups), and 4 is the quotient (the answer). If something is left over, that is the remainder.

2Long division, step by step

To divide big numbers we use long division. You repeat the same cycle of four steps until there are no more digits:

The cycle

DDivide. How many times does the divisor fit? Write that number in the quotient.

MMultiply. Multiply the divisor by that number.

SSubtract. Subtract that product to see what is left.

BBring down. Bring down the next digit and start the cycle again.

Example: 738 ÷ 6

Work from left to right.

1Divide: how many times does 6 fit in 7? 1 time. Write 1 in the quotient.

2Multiply and subtract: 1 × 6 = 6. Then 7 − 6 = 1.

3Bring down the 3. Now you have 13.

4Divide: how many times does 6 fit in 13? 2 times. 2 × 6 = 12, and 13 − 12 = 1.

5Bring down the 8. Now you have 18.

6Divide: how many times does 6 fit in 18? Exactly 3 times. 3 × 6 = 18, and 18 − 18 = 0.

✓Result: the quotient is 123, and the remainder is 0.

Watch out!

The remainder must always be smaller than the divisor. If you subtract and get a number that is equal to or bigger than the divisor, the digit in the quotient is too small. Make it bigger.

Watch out for zeros in the quotient

If the divisor does not fit, write 0 in the quotient before you bring down the next digit. Example: 624 ÷ 6. 6 fits in 6 one time. Bring down the 2: 6 does not fit in 2, so write 0. Bring down the 4: 24 ÷ 6 = 4. The answer is 104, not 14.

3When it does not divide evenly: the remainder

Not every division comes out even. When something is left over, that amount is the remainder.

Example: 50 ÷ 7

1How many times does 7 fit in 50? 7 times, because 7 × 7 = 49. (8 × 7 = 56 is too much.)

2Subtract: 50 − 49 = 1. That 1 is the remainder, and it is smaller than 7 ✓.

✓Result: 50 ÷ 7 = 7, remainder 1. Many books write this as 7 R1.

Check a division with multiplication

(quotient × divisor) + remainder must equal the dividend.
50 ÷ 7 = 7, remainder 1 → (7 × 7) + 1 = 49 + 1 = 50 ✓

4The most important part: what do I do with the remainder?

Finding the remainder is only half of the work. The other half, and the more important one, is deciding what the remainder means in the real situation. There are three possibilities, and the problem tells you which one to use.

Three problems, the same calculation: 26 ÷ 4 = 6, remainder 2.

AGive the remainder. "There are 26 rolls of bread to pack in 4 equal boxes. How many rolls are left over?" The answer is the remainder: 2 rolls are left over.

BRound the quotient up. "26 people are traveling in cars that hold 4 people each. How many cars do they need?" 6 cars are not enough: 2 people would stay behind. They need one more car: 7 cars.

CDrop the remainder. "I have $26, and each ticket costs $4. How many tickets can I buy?" Only whole tickets count: 6 tickets. The $2 left over is not enough for another one.

The question that decides it all

Ask yourself: does the problem ask for what is left over, for full groups, or for enough room for everyone? The answer tells you whether to give the remainder, drop it, or round up.

5Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open. Use the examples and the self-check checklist to review your work and decide what to practice next.

  • ThatQuiz. Go to ThatQuiz and practice division, first with one-digit divisors and then with two-digit divisors. Pay attention to the exercises with a remainder.
  • With paper and pencil. Do eight long divisions and say the four steps out loud: "Divide… Multiply… Subtract… Bring down." Check each one with multiplication: (quotient × divisor) + remainder.
  • In your daily life. Think about something you share or split: hours in a week, money across days, food among people. Write the division, solve it, and decide what the remainder means in that case.

📘Key words

dividend
The total you divide. In 24 ÷ 6 = 4, the dividend is 24.
divisor
The number you divide by. In 24 ÷ 6 = 4, the divisor is 6.
quotient
The answer to a division. In 24 ÷ 6 = 4, the quotient is 4.
remainder
What is left over when a division does not come out even. It is always smaller than the divisor. 50 ÷ 7 = 7, remainder 1 (7 R1).
long division
A way to divide big numbers step by step: divide, multiply, subtract, bring down, and repeat.
round up
Here: use the next whole number for the quotient, so that everyone or everything fits.
