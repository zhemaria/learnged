Lesson 4.4 · Operations with mixed numbers

Mixed numbers look hard, but there is a trick that always works: change them to improper fractions and use everything you already learned in this topic.

⏱️ About 4 hours with practice and homework · take breaks 🔢 Add, subtract, multiply, and divide

🎯What you will learn

When you finish this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Change mixed numbers to improper fractions, and back, with confidence.
  • Multiply and divide mixed numbers by changing them to improper fractions first.
  • Add and subtract mixed numbers, including cases where you take away more than the fraction you have.
  • Solve real-life problems with mixed numbers, and simplify the answer.

1An old friend

You have already met mixed numbers in this topic. In Lesson 4.2, the answer 5/4 became 1 141/4. In Lesson 4.3, the answer 3/2 became 1 121/2. Today, mixed numbers get a lesson of their own.

A mixed number is a whole number together with a fraction. For example, 2 121/2 means "two wholes and one half." We use them every day: a recipe calls for 1 343/4 cups of flour; a board is 5 121/2 feet long; a movie lasts 2 141/4 hours.

The key idea of this lesson

There are no new rules to memorize. The secret to working with mixed numbers is just one: change them to improper fractions, then do exactly what you already know how to do with fractions. At the end, write the answer as a mixed number again.

2Quick review: mixed numbers and improper fractions

The whole lesson rests on this skill, so let us review it carefully before we go on.

From a mixed number to an improper fraction

The rule

Whole number × denominator + numerator goes on top. The denominator does not change.

Example: change 3 141/4 to an improper fraction

1Step 1. Multiply the whole number by the denominator: 3 × 4 = 12. (Each whole has 4 fourths, so 3 wholes have 12 fourths.)

2Step 2. Add the numerator: 12 + 1 = 13. That is the new numerator.

3Step 3. The denominator stays the same: 4.

✓Result. 3 141/4 = 13413/4.

3 whole bars and 1 more fourth: 13 fourths in all.

From an improper fraction to a mixed number

The rule

Numerator ÷ denominator. The quotient is the whole number, the remainder is the new numerator, and the denominator does not change.

Example: change 17517/5 to a mixed number

1Step 1. Divide: 17 ÷ 5 = 3, with 2 left over (remainder 2).

2Step 2. The quotient (3) is the whole number. The remainder (2) is the numerator.

3Step 3. The denominator stays the same: 5.

✓Result. 17517/5 = 3 252/5.

3Multiply and divide mixed numbers

Here the rule is firm, with no exceptions: never multiply or divide the whole numbers on one side and the fractions on the other. Change everything to improper fractions first.

To multiply or divide mixed numbers

1) Change to improper fractions · 2) multiply or divide as in Lesson 4.3 · 3) simplify and change back to a mixed number.

Example (multiply): 2 121/2 × 1 131/3

1Change. 2 121/2 = 525/2 and 1 131/3 = 434/3.

2Multiply. Numerators: 5 × 4 = 20. Denominators: 2 × 3 = 6. You get 20620/6.

3Simplify. Divide the top and the bottom by 2: 20620/6 = 10310/3.

4Change back. 10 ÷ 3 = 3, remainder 1.

✓Result. 2 121/2 × 1 131/3 = 3 131/3.

Watch out! The most common mistake

It is tempting to multiply "2 × 1" and "121/2 × 131/3" separately. That gives 2 161/6, which is wrong: the right answer is 3 131/3. Always change to improper fractions before you multiply or divide.

Example (divide): 3 343/4 ÷ 1 121/2

1Change. 3 343/4 = 15415/4 and 1 121/2 = 323/2.

2Keep, change, flip. 15415/4 × 232/3 = 301230/12.

3Simplify. Divide the top and the bottom by 6: 301230/12 = 525/2.

✓Change back. 5 ÷ 2 = 2, remainder 1: 2 121/2.

4Add and subtract mixed numbers

To add and subtract, use the same plan: change to improper fractions, find a common denominator (as in Lesson 4.2), add or subtract, and change back to a mixed number. This one method works for every case.

To add or subtract mixed numbers

Change to improper fractions · find a common denominator · add or subtract the numerators · change back to a mixed number.

Example (add): 1 343/4 + 2 121/2

1Change. 1 343/4 = 747/4 and 2 121/2 = 525/2.

2Common denominator (4). 525/2 = 10410/4.

3Add the numerators. 747/4 + 10410/4 = 17417/4.

✓Change back. 17 ÷ 4 = 4, remainder 1: 4 141/4.

Subtracting from a whole number

Problems like 4 − 1 232/3 often scare students, because "there is no fraction to take from." With improper fractions, that problem disappears.

Example (subtract): 4 − 1 232/3

1Write the whole number as a fraction. 4 = 414/1 = 12312/3 (common denominator 3).

2Change the mixed number. 1 232/3 = 535/3.

3Subtract the numerators. 12312/3 − 535/3 = 737/3.

✓Change back. 7 ÷ 3 = 2, remainder 1: 2 131/3.

Why one method?

There is another way to add mixed numbers: wholes with wholes and fractions with fractions. It works, but when you subtract and the top fraction is too small, you must borrow a whole (for example, 1 = 333/3). Changing to improper fractions always works the same way, with no special tricks. Learn this one well, and you can solve all four operations with the same recipe. The practice page shows both ways, so you can compare them.

5Mixed numbers in real-life problems

Always use the 4-step method from Lesson 1.3: read and underline, choose the operation, solve, and check if the answer makes sense. Mixed numbers show up most in cooking, carpentry, and measurements.

📋 "A carpenter has a board 8 feet long and cuts off 3 121/2 feet. How much of the board is left?"

1Read and underline. A board 8 feet long; cuts off 3 121/2 feet; how much is left.

2Choose the operation. "Left" means subtract: 8 − 3 121/2.

3Solve. 8 = 16216/2 and 3 121/2 = 727/2. 16216/2 − 727/2 = 929/2 = 4 121/2.

✓Does it make sense? 4 121/2 feet are left, less than the 8 feet at the start. That makes sense, because a piece was cut off.

6Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open. With it, you finish Topic 4.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. Write six mixed numbers, change them to improper fractions, and then change them back to mixed numbers. If you do not get the same number back, the mistake is on the way there or on the way back: find it now. Then solve eight operations, two of each of the four.
  • In your kitchen. Take a recipe with mixed numbers (1 121/2 cups, 2 343/4 tablespoons). Find how much you would need to make double the recipe, and write it as a mixed number, not as an improper fraction: it is easier to read when you cook.

📘Key words

mixed number
A whole number together with a fraction, like 2 1/2 ("two and one half").
improper fraction
A fraction whose numerator is greater than or equal to its denominator, like 5/2. It is 1 or more.
quotient
The answer to a division. In 17 ÷ 5, the quotient is 3.
remainder
What is left over in a division. In 17 ÷ 5, the remainder is 2.
borrow
In the wholes-and-fractions way of subtracting, to break one whole into a fraction (like 1 = 3/3) when the top fraction is too small.
