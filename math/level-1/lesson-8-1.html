Lesson 8.1 · Reading and understanding data tables

Rows, columns, and cells: read a table, compare its data, and summarize it with the mean and the range.

⏱️ About 4 hours with practice and homework · take breaks 📋 Row and column

🎯What you will learn

When you finish this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Name the parts of a data table.
  • Find a value and compare values in a table.
  • Calculate totals, differences, the mean, and the range.
  • Make a frequency table from a list of answers.

1The parts of a table

A data table organizes information in rows (across) and columns (down). Tables are everywhere: bus schedules, pay stubs, nutrition labels, and many GED® questions.

The key idea of this lesson

To read a table, find the row, find the column, and look where they meet. Always read the title and the headers first, so you know what the numbers mean and in what unit.

2Find, compare, and choose

Most questions about a table ask you to find a value, to find the largest or the smallest, or to compare two values.

Example: in the food truck table, how many tortas were sold on Thursday?

1Find the row. Thursday is the last row.

2Find the column. Tortas is the third column.

✓Where they meet. 30 tortas.

Example: which day sold the most drinks?

1Look down one column. Drinks: 60, 52, 70, 65.

✓Find the largest. 70, on Wednesday.

Example: how many more tacos were sold on Wednesday than on Tuesday?

1Find both values. Wednesday: 50. Tuesday: 38.

✓"How many more" means subtract. 50 − 38 = 12 more tacos.

3Calculate with a table: mean and range

Tables also let you calculate: totals, differences, and two important summary numbers, the mean and the range.

The mean and the range of a set of data
NumberHow to find itWhat it tells you
mean (average)add all the values and divide by how many there area typical value, "if everything were shared equally"
rangelargest value − smallest valuehow spread out the values are

↔ Slide the table sideways to see all the columns.

Example: the mean number of tacos sold per day

1Add the values. 45 + 38 + 50 + 43 = 176.

2Divide by how many values. There are 4 days: 176 ÷ 4 = 44.

✓Answer. The truck sold a mean of 44 tacos per day.

Example: the range of the drinks

1Largest and smallest. Largest: 70. Smallest: 52.

✓Subtract. 70 − 52 = 18 drinks.

🧮 Check it on the calculator

The mean of the tacos: type ( 45 + 38 + 50 + 43 ) ÷ 4 enter. The screen shows 44. The parentheses make the calculator add first.

4Make a frequency table

Sometimes the data come as a long list, and you make the table. A frequency table counts how many times each answer appears. Tally marks (||||) help you count without losing your place.

Example: 12 people answered "What is your favorite fruit?"

1The list. apple, mango, banana, mango, apple, mango, grape, banana, mango, apple, mango, banana.

2Count each fruit. apple 3, mango 5, banana 3, grape 1.

✓Check the total. 3 + 5 + 3 + 1 = 12 ✓, the same as the number of people.

5Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. Make a table of your expenses for one week: one row for each day and columns for food, transportation, and other. Find the total for each column and the mean spent per day.
  • In your daily life. Ask 10 to 15 people a question with a few possible answers (favorite food, how they get to work). Make a frequency table with tally marks and check the total.

📘Key words

data
Facts or numbers collected to answer a question.
row and column
A row goes across a table; a column goes down.
cell
One box of a table, where a row and a column meet.
mean (average)
The sum of the values divided by how many values there are.
range
The largest value minus the smallest value.
frequency
How many times something appears in a set of data.
