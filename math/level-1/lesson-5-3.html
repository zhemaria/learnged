Lesson 5.3 · One-step word problems with the four operations

When you finish this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Picture the situation in a story and choose which of the four operations it needs.

1Everything comes together

This lesson closes Topic 5 and puts together everything you learned in Level 1. In Topics 1 and 2 you learned when to add, subtract, multiply, and divide in a word problem. In Lesson 5.2 you learned to write and solve equations with an unknown.

Now you will join both ideas: you will read a problem in words, find the number you do not know, write it as an equation, and solve it.

The key idea of this lesson

A word problem is a story with an unknown. Your job is to translate the story into an equation, solve the equation, and answer the question with the right unit.

Watch out! The words can fool you

Key words help, but the story decides. Look at this problem: "Pedro has $58. That is $15 more than Ana has. How much does Ana have?" It says "more," but Ana has less than Pedro. So we subtract: 58 − 15 = $43.

3From the story to the equation

When the number you do not know is not the final result, an equation helps a lot. Use the 4 steps from Lesson 1.3, now with an unknown:

The 4 steps with an unknown

1Read and name the unknown. Underline the numbers and the question. Call the number you do not know x.

2Write the equation. Follow the story in order, with x in its place.

3Solve with the inverse operation, as in Lesson 5.2.

4Check and answer. Put the value back into the story, and write the answer with its unit.

Example: "Rosa had some money in her account. She deposited $85, and now she has $310. How much did she have at first?"

1Name the unknown. x = the money Rosa had at first.

2Write the equation. She had x, and $85 was added: x + 85 = 310.

3Solve. Subtract 85 from both sides: x = 310 − 85 = 225.

✓Check and answer. $225 + $85 = $310 ✓. Rosa had $225 at first.

Example: "Marcos worked 8 hours and earned $136. He earns the same amount each hour. How much does he earn per hour?"

1Name the unknown. x = the money Marcos earns per hour.

2Write the equation. 8 equal hours of x dollars each: 8x = 136.

3Solve. Divide both sides by 8: x = 136 ÷ 8 = 17.

✓Check and answer. 8 × $17 = $136 ✓. Marcos earns $17 per hour.

Every one-step story fits one of these four equations:

The four kinds of one-step stories and their equations

4Does the answer make sense?

The last step is the one people skip most, and it catches many mistakes. Ask yourself three questions:

  • Is the size right? If something was added, the total must be bigger. If a total was split, each part must be smaller.
  • Does it fit the story? Put the answer back into the story, not only into the equation.
  • Does it have its unit? Dollars, miles, hours, people… A number alone does not answer the question.

Example: "A school bought 7 equal boxes of paper with 3,500 sheets in all. How many sheets are in each box?"

1Solve. 7x = 3,500, so x = 3,500 ÷ 7 = 500.

2Check the size. Each box holds part of the total, so the answer must be smaller than 3,500. 500 is smaller ✓.

✓Check the story and the unit. 7 boxes × 500 sheets = 3,500 sheets ✓. Each box has 500 sheets.

Watch out! Extra information

Some problems give you numbers you do not need. "A store sold 45 shirts and 38 pants. Each shirt costs $12. How much money did the shirts bring in?" The 38 pants do not matter for this question: 45 × $12 = $540.

5Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. Write four short stories of your own, one for each operation. For each one, name the unknown, write the equation, solve it, and check it. Then trade stories with a classmate or a family member and solve theirs.
  • In your daily life. Look for a real one-step problem this week: a bill split among roommates, the hours you need to work to earn a certain amount, the miles left on a trip. Solve it with the 4 steps and write the answer with its unit.

📘Key words

word problem
A math problem told as a story in words, with a question to answer.
unknown
The number we do not know yet. In an equation we call it x.
equation
A statement that two things are equal, like x + 85 = 310.
bar model
A drawing with bars that shows the parts and the whole of a problem.
reasonable
Said of an answer that makes sense in the story: it has the right size and the right unit.
unit
What a number counts or measures: dollars, miles, hours, people.
