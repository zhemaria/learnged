Lesson 5.3 · One-step word problems with the four operations

Four practice activities on one page. The exercises change every time. If you make a mistake, you get a hint and another try.

On this page

  1. Choose the operation
  2. Write the equation
  3. Solve word problems step by step
  4. Does the answer make sense?
1

Choose the operation

›

Before you calculate, picture what happens in the story. Then choose the operation that matches it.

Four situations, four operations
What happens in the storyOperation
Amounts come together, or something increasesaddition
Something is taken away, or you compare two amountssubtraction
Equal groups come togethermultiplication
A total is split into equal groupsdivision
The story decides, not one word. "Pedro has $58. That is $15 more than Ana has." Ana has less than Pedro, so you subtract, even though the problem says "more."

Practice: which operation?

2

Write the equation

›

When the number you do not know is not the final result, call it x and write the story as an equation. Follow the story in order.

Stories and their equations
In the story…Equation
something was added to the amount at firstx + 25 = 60
something was taken away from the amount at firstx − 15 = 40
there are equal groups of the unknown6x = 72
the unknown was split into equal partsx ÷ 4 = 9

Solve word problems step by step

›

The 4 steps with an unknown: 1. Name the unknown. 2. Write the equation. 3. Solve it with the inverse operation. 4. Check it in the story and answer with the unit. The bar model helps you see the parts and the whole.

4

Does the answer make sense?

›

Before you give an answer, ask: Is the size right? (If a total is split, each part is smaller. If groups come together, the total is bigger.) Does it fit the story? Does it have its unit?
