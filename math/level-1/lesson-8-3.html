Math · Level 1 · Topic 8 — Tables, graphs, and final project

Lesson 8.3 · Final project

Plan a celebration for 20 guests with $500, and use everything you learned in Level 1.

⏱️ About 4 hours with practice and homework · take breaks 🎉 All of Level 1 together

🎯What you will learn

When you finish this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Plan a real project with a budget, step by step.
  • Use whole numbers, decimals, and fractions to find costs and parts of the budget.
  • Use measures, perimeter, area, and time to plan the space and the schedule.
  • Present the plan with a table, a bar graph, and a summary.

1The final project

Congratulations: you have reached the end of Level 1! In this project you will use what you learned in all eight topics to solve one real problem, the way you will on the GED® test and in daily life.

The project

You are planning a family celebration for 20 guests with a budget of $500. You must decide what to buy, check that it fits the budget, plan the time and the space, and present your plan with a table and a graph.

2Step 1: the money

Start with the money. Decide how much of the budget each thing can use, and calculate each cost.

Example: food can use 2/5 of the budget. How much is that?

1A fraction of an amount. $500 ÷ 5 = $100 (one fifth).

✓Two fifths. 2 × $100 = $200 for food.

Example: each plate of food costs $8.50. How much for 20 guests? Does it fit in $200?

1Multiply. 20 × $8.50 = $170.

✓Compare with the limit. $170 is less than $200 ✓. There are $30 left from the food budget.

Example: how much of the $500 is left?

1Write it as an equation. $348.50 + x = $500, where x is the money left.

✓Solve with the inverse operation. x = $500 − $348.50 = $151.50 left for other things.

3Step 2: space, measures, and time

Now plan the space and the time. The room is 24 ft long and 16 ft wide. The party goes from 4:30 p.m. to 8:15 p.m.

Example: a banner will go all the way around the room. How many feet of banner do you need?

1Around means perimeter. P = 2 × 24 + 2 × 16 = 48 + 32 = 80.

✓Answer. 80 feet of banner (at $0.75 per foot, $60).

Example: the dance floor is 12 ft by 10 ft. How much floor does it cover?

1Covering means area. A = 12 × 10 = 120.

✓Answer. 120 square feet, out of 24 × 16 = 384 square feet in the room.

Example: 3 gallons of juice, served in 8-fluid-ounce cups. Is it enough for 2 cups per guest?

1Same unit. 3 × 128 = 384 fluid ounces.

2Number of cups. 384 ÷ 8 = 48.

✓Compare. 20 guests × 2 = 40 cups, and 48 > 40 ✓. Yes, it is enough.

Example: how long is the party, from 4:30 p.m. to 8:15 p.m.?

1Count up. 4:30 → 5:00 is 30 min; 5:00 → 8:00 is 3 h; 8:00 → 8:15 is 15 min.

✓Add the jumps. 3 h 45 min.

4Step 3: organize and present

Finally, organize and present your plan so another person can understand it with one look: an expense table, a bar graph, and a short summary.

Example: the mean cost for each guest

1Total ÷ number of guests. $348.50 ÷ 20 = $17.425.

✓Round to the cent. About $17.43 for each guest.

Your summary

Write 3 or 4 sentences: the total cost, the money left, the biggest expense, and one decision you made with the numbers (for example: "Food is half of the cost, so I chose a cheaper cake.").

5Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. Make your own plan: a celebration, a trip, or a home project (painting a room, a garden). Choose a budget. Make an expense table with at least 5 items, check that it fits the budget, find one perimeter or area and one elapsed time, make a bar graph of the costs, and write a summary of 3 or 4 sentences.
  • In your daily life. Present your plan to a family member or a classmate. Explain one decision you made with the numbers, and ask them to check one of your calculations.
