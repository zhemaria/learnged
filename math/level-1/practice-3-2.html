Lesson 3.2 · Operations with decimals

Three practice activities on one page. The exercises change every time. If you make a mistake, you get a hint and another try. Solve on paper first; then check with the calculator (button at the top right).

On this page

  1. Add and subtract: line up the points
  2. Multiply: count the places
  3. Divide: move the point
1

Add and subtract: line up the points

›

Line up the decimal points in one column. If the numbers have a different number of decimal places, add zeros on the right. Then work column by column, like with whole numbers. The point in the answer goes in the same column.

Line up and calculate

Practice: add and subtract

Type the number with a decimal point, for example 16.25. You can type $ for money.

🧮 Check it on the calculator

After you solve on paper, type the same operation on the calculator: the first number, + or −, the second number, and enter. On the calculator you do not line up anything; that is your job on paper.

2

Multiply: count the places

›

Ignore the points and multiply like whole numbers. Then count the decimal places of both factors together: the answer has that many decimal places. Estimate to check that the point is in the right place.

Step by step

Practice: multiply

Type the number with a decimal point, for example 10.58.

🧮 Check it on the calculator

Type the first factor, ×, the second factor, and enter. Compare three answers: your paper, your estimate, and the screen. When all three agree, you can trust your answer.

↑ Back to contents
3

Divide: move the point

›

Move the point of the divisor until it is a whole number. Move the point of the dividend the same number of places, in the same direction. Then divide as always, and bring the point straight up into the answer.

Step by step

Practice: divide

Type the number with a decimal point, for example 18.9.

🧮 Check it on the calculator

Type the dividend, ÷, the divisor, and enter. The calculator does not need you to move the point, so check that your paper agrees with the screen.
