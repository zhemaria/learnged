Multiply: straight across

›

Good news: to multiply, you do not need a common denominator. Numerator times numerator on top, denominator times denominator on the bottom, and simplify at the end. The word "of" almost always means multiply: half of half a pizza is 1/2 × 1/2 = 1/4.

The area model

Practice: multiply

Type the fraction with a slash, in simplest form, for example 2/5.

🧮 Check it on the calculator

Write the first fraction with n/d (numerator, ▼, denominator, ►), press ×, write the second fraction the same way, and press enter. The calculator shows the product in simplest form.

2

The reciprocal

›

The reciprocal of a fraction is the same fraction flipped upside down: the numerator goes to the bottom and the denominator to the top. The reciprocal of 3/4 is 4/3; the reciprocal of 1/8 is 8/1, which is 8; and the reciprocal of a whole number like 4 is 1/4 (every whole number is itself over 1). A number times its reciprocal is always 1.

3

Divide: keep, change, flip

›

To divide: keep the first fraction as it is, change ÷ to ×, and flip the second fraction (use its reciprocal). Then multiply straight across.

Step by step

Flip only the second fraction. If you flip the first one, or both, the answer comes out wrong.

Practice: divide

Type the fraction with a slash, in simplest form. If it is more than 1, you can type a mixed number with a space, like 1 1/2.

4

Fractions in real life

›

"Half of," "a third of" ask you to multiply. "How many pieces of …?", "how many servings of …?" ask you to divide.

Example: how many 1/4-pound servings can you get from 3 pounds of meat? 3 ÷ 1/4 = 3 × 4 = 12 servings.

Practice: word problems with fractions

Type a fraction (3/8), a mixed number with a space (1 1/2), or a whole number. Do not type the units.
