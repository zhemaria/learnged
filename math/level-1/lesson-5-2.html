1The language of algebra

In Lesson 5.1 you found the rules of patterns and said them in words: "add 5 each time," "the output is the input times 3." That works, but it is long.

Algebra gives us a shorter and more exact way: write those ideas with symbols. Instead of saying "a number plus five," we write x + 5. It means the same thing, but in the language of math.

The unknown

An unknown is a number we do not know yet. We show it with a symbol: a box □ or a letter like x or n. The unknown is not an impossible mystery: it is simply "the number we want to find."

Watch out! Two traps

Twice is written together. "Twice x" is written 2x, with no sign between the 2 and the x. In algebra, a number and a letter written together are multiplied: 2x means "2 times x."

"Less than" changes the order. "5 less than a number" means take 5 away from the number, so the number comes first: x − 5, not 5 − x.

3The equation: a balanced scale

An equation says that two things are equal: what is on one side of the = sign has the same value as what is on the other side. The best way to picture it is a balanced scale.

The scale is level: the left side weighs the same as the right side.

In the equation □ + 3 = 8, the box hides a number. Because the scale is balanced, that number must make the left side worth 8. What number plus 3 gives 8? The number 5.

The key idea of this lesson

To solve an equation is to find the value of the unknown that keeps the scale balanced: the number that makes the equation true.

4Solve with the inverse operation

With easy equations you can guess, but there is a sure method that always works: use the inverse operation. Remember from Topics 1 and 2: subtraction undoes addition, and division undoes multiplication.

The idea is to get the unknown alone. To do that, use the inverse of the operation that is with it.

Example: solve □ + 3 = 8

1Look at the unknown. It is added to 3. The inverse of adding is subtracting.

2Subtract 3 from both sides, so the scale stays balanced: □ + 3 − 3 = 8 − 3.

3Simplify. On the left, + 3 − 3 cancel out, and the unknown is alone: □ = 5.

✓Solution. □ = 5.

Example: solve 4x = 20

1Look at the unknown. It is multiplied by 4. The inverse of multiplying is dividing.

2Divide both sides by 4: 4x ÷ 4 = 20 ÷ 4.

3Simplify. On the left, the x is alone: x = 5.

✓Solution. x = 5.

The same idea works for the other two operations: addition undoes subtraction, and multiplication undoes division.

The rule of the scale

Whatever you do to one side of the equation, do it to the other side too. If you subtract 3 on the left, subtract 3 on the right. That way the scale never tips, and the equation stays true.

5Check the solution

After you solve, there is a simple way to know if you are right: substitute. That means you put the value you found in place of the unknown and see if the equation is true.

Example: check that x = 9 solves x + 6 = 15

1Substitute. Put 9 in place of x in the original equation: 9 + 6 = 15.

2Calculate the left side. 9 + 6 = 15.

✓Compare. 15 = 15. Both sides are equal, so x = 9 is the right solution.

A good habit

Checking by substituting works like checking with the inverse operation in Topics 1 and 2: it tells you that your answer is right, with no one else checking it for you.

🧮 Check it on the calculator

You can substitute with the calculator. Type the left side with your value in place of x, and compare the answer with the right side.

  1. Does x = 5 solve 4x = 20? Type 4 × 5 enter. The screen shows 20, the same as the right side: yes.
  2. Does x = 18 solve x ÷ 3 = 6? Type 18 ÷ 3 enter. The screen shows 6: yes.

6Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. Translate ten phrases into symbols: "twice a number," "5 less than a number," "a number divided by 3," and so on. Then go the other way: take x + 12 = 40 and write a real situation that this equation describes. Solve it and check it.
  • In your daily life. Think of a situation of your own with a number you do not know: "I had some money, I spent $20, and I have $35 left. How much did I have?" Write it as an equation, solve it with the inverse operation, and check it by substituting.

📘Key words

unknown (variable)
A number we do not know yet, shown with a box □ or a letter like x.
expression
Numbers, letters, and operation signs with no equal sign, like x + 5 or 3x.
equation
A statement that two things are equal, with an equal sign, like x + 3 = 8.
solve
To find the value of the unknown that makes the equation true.
inverse operation
The operation that undoes another one: subtraction undoes addition, and division undoes multiplication (and the other way around).
substitute
To put a number in place of the unknown, for example to check a solution.
