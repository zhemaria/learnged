Lesson 4.2 · Adding and subtracting fractions

Join and take away parts of a whole. The secret is simple: the parts must be the same size.

🎯What you will learn

When you finish this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Explain why only the numerators are added or subtracted.
  • Add and subtract fractions with the same denominator.
  • Find a common denominator for two fractions.
  • Add and subtract fractions with different denominators.
  • Simplify the result, and write it as a mixed number when it is more than 1.

1Why the denominator matters

In Lesson 4.1 you learned that the denominator tells the size of the parts, and the numerator tells how many parts you have.

Think about money. Two quarters plus one quarter are three quarters. You count the coins, and they are still quarters: putting them together does not change the size of each coin. Fractions work the same way:

2 fourths + 1 fourth = 3 fourths,   or   242/4 + 141/4 = 343/4

The key idea

To add or subtract fractions, the parts must be the same size (the same denominator). Then you add or subtract only the numerators. The denominator stays the same.

But what if the parts are different sizes, like halves and thirds? You cannot count them together until you cut them into parts of the same size. You already know how: with equivalent fractions.

2The same denominator

When the denominators are the same, add (or subtract) the numerators and keep the denominator.

Example: 252/5 + 151/5

1Step 1. The denominators are the same (5): the parts are fifths.

2Step 2. Add the numerators: 2 + 1 = 3.

✓Result. 252/5 + 151/5 = 353/5. The parts are still fifths.

2 fifths (dark) + 1 fifth (light) = 3 fifths

Subtracting works the same way. A board is 585/8 inch thick, and you sand off 282/8 inch. Now it is 5 − 2 = 3 eighths: 383/8 inch thick.

Watch out!

Never add the denominators. 252/5 + 151/5 is not 3103/10. Here is a quick way to catch that mistake: 3103/10 is less than the 252/5 you started with. If you add something to 252/5, the answer must be more than 252/5. Always ask yourself if your answer makes sense.

3Different denominators

When the denominators are different, the parts are different sizes. First, change the fractions to equivalent fractions with the same denominator (a common denominator). Then add or subtract the numerators.

How to find a common denominator

Go through the multiples of the larger denominator (2 times, 3 times, …) until you find one that the smaller denominator also goes into. If you do not find one quickly, multiplying the two denominators always works. You may get bigger numbers, but you can simplify at the end.

Example: 121/2 + 131/3

1Step 1. Find a common denominator. Multiples of 3: 3, 6, … The number 2 does not go into 3, but it does go into 6. The common denominator is 6.

2Step 2. Change each fraction to sixths: 121/2 = 363/6 (× 3), and 131/3 = 262/6 (× 2).

3Step 3. Now the parts are the same size. Add the numerators: 3 + 2 = 5.

✓Result. 121/2 + 131/3 = 363/6 + 262/6 = 565/6.

Cut into sixths, the half is 3 sixths and the third is 2 sixths: together, 5 sixths.

Example: 343/4 − 181/8 (inches on a ruler)

1Step 1. 8 is a multiple of 4 (4 × 2 = 8). The common denominator is 8.

2Step 2. Change 343/4 to eighths: 343/4 = 686/8. The 181/8 stays as it is.

3Step 3. Subtract the numerators: 6 − 1 = 5.

✓Result. 343/4 − 181/8 = 686/8 − 181/8 = 585/8 inch.

🧮 Check it on the calculator

  3. Press n/d, type 1, go down with ▼, type 3, and leave with ►.

4Simplify the result

After you add or subtract, look at the answer. If it can be simplified, simplify it, just as you learned in Lesson 4.1.

Example: 161/6 + 161/6

1Step 1. Same denominator: 1 + 1 = 2. So 161/6 + 161/6 = 262/6.

2Step 2. 2 and 6 can both be divided by 2: 262/6 = 131/3.

✓Result. 161/6 + 161/6 = 131/3.

When the answer is more than 1

Sometimes the numerator ends up greater than the denominator. For example, 343/4 + 242/4 = 545/4. A fraction like 545/4 is called an improper fraction: it is more than one whole.

Four fourths make one whole, and there is one fourth left over. So 545/4 is the same as 1 and 141/4, written 1 141/4. A number like this, with a whole number and a fraction, is called a mixed number. To find it, divide: 5 ÷ 4 = 1 with remainder 1.

On the GED

The answer choices are almost always in simplest form. If your answer is 363/6 and you do not see it among the choices, simplify it: look for 121/2.

5Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. Write and solve ten operations: five with the same denominator and five with different denominators. Mix additions and subtractions. Simplify each answer, and write any improper fraction as a mixed number. Then check each one: does the answer make sense? A sum must be more than each fraction you added.
  • In your day. You spend 141/4 hour on the bus, 121/2 hour cooking, and 131/3 hour helping with homework. How many hours is that in all? Write the answer as a mixed number, and then in hours and minutes. (Hint: the common denominator is 12, and 1 hour = 60 minutes.)

📘Key words

like denominators
Fractions with the same denominator, like 2/5 and 1/5. Their parts are the same size.
unlike denominators
Fractions with different denominators, like 1/2 and 1/3. Their parts are different sizes.
common denominator
A denominator that two fractions can share. It is a multiple of both denominators.
least common denominator
The smallest common denominator. It is the LCM of the denominators.
improper fraction
A fraction whose numerator is greater than or equal to its denominator, like 5/4. It is 1 or more.
mixed number
A whole number and a fraction together, like 1 1/4.
simplest form
A fraction whose top and bottom share no factor greater than 1, like 1/3.
