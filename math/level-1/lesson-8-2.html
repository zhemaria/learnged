Lesson 8.2 · Bar graphs and line graphs

Bars to compare, lines to see change: read any graph by its title, its axes, and its scale.

⏱️ About 4 hours with practice and homework · take breaks 📊 Read the scale first

🎯What you will learn

When you finish this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Name the parts of a graph and read its scale.
  • Read and compare the bars of a bar graph.
  • Read a line graph and describe how a value changes over time.

1The parts of a graph

A graph shows data as a picture. With one look you can see which value is the largest, which is the smallest, and how the values change. Newspapers, bills, and the GED® test use graphs all the time.

The parts of a graph and what each one tells you
PartWhat it tells you
titlewhat the graph is about
horizontal axis (bottom)the categories or the times
vertical axis (side)what is counted or measured, and its unit
scalehow much each line on the vertical axis is worth

Before you read a graph, read its title, its axes, and its scale. In the graph above, each line is worth 2 people, not 1.

2Bar graphs: compare categories

A bar graph compares categories: kinds of things, places, or groups. The taller the bar, the larger the value.

Example: how many people take the bus?

1Find the bar. The first bar is Bus.

2Go across to the scale. The top of the bar is on the line for 12.

✓Answer. 12 people take the bus.

Example: how many more people drive a car than take the bus?

1Read both bars. Car: 18. Bus: 12.

✓Subtract. 18 − 12 = 6 more people.

Watch out! A bar between two lines

If a bar ends halfway between two lines, its value is halfway between them. On a scale by 2, a bar halfway between 4 and 6 is worth 5.

3Line graphs: change over time

A line graph shows how a value changes over time: by hour, by month, by year. Each point is a value, and the line joins the points in order.

Watch out! This scale does not start at 0

The vertical axis starts at $60, so the changes look bigger than they are. Always read the numbers on the scale, not only the shape of the line.

What the line in a line graph tells you
The line…means the value…
goes up ↗increased
goes down ↘decreased
is flat →stayed the same
is steepestchanged the most

Example: between which two months did the bill go up the most?

1Find where the line goes up. April → May (70 → 80) and May → June (80 → 105).

2Find each change. 80 − 70 = 10 and 105 − 80 = 25.

✓Answer. The bill went up the most from May to June, by $25.

4Choose and make a graph

Each kind of graph has its job. Choose the graph that fits the question:

When to use a bar graph and when to use a line graph
Use a…to…Example
bar graphcompare categoriessales of 5 products
line graphshow change over timeyour weight each month for a year

To make a bar graph from a table:

Steps to make a bar graph

1Title and axes. Write a title. Label the bottom axis with the categories and the side axis with what you count.

2Choose the scale. Look at the largest value. Count by 1, 2, 5, or 10 so it fits, always starting at 0.

✓Draw the bars. Make each bar as tall as its value, all the same width, with spaces between them.

5Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. Make a bar graph of the frequency table you made in Lesson 8.1 (or of five of your monthly expenses). Choose a good scale, label both axes, and give it a title.
  • In your daily life. Find a graph in a newspaper, on a bill, or online. Write its title, what each axis shows, its scale, and one thing the graph tells you.

📘Key words

graph
A picture that shows data.
axis (plural: axes)
One of the two lines of a graph: horizontal (bottom) or vertical (side).
scale
The numbers on an axis, and how much each line is worth.
bar graph
A graph that uses bars to compare categories.
line graph
A graph that uses points joined by a line to show change over time.
trend
The general direction of a line graph: going up, going down, or staying the same.
