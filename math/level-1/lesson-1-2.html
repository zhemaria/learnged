Math · Level 1 · Topic 1 — Whole numbers

Lesson 1.2 · Adding and subtracting whole numbers

Add and subtract big numbers step by step: carry when adding, borrow when subtracting, and check your answer with the opposite operation.

⏱️ About 4 hours with practice and homework · take breaks 🎯 Calculator only to check

🎯What you will learn

When you finish this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Add whole numbers with several digits, carrying when a column adds up to 10 or more.
  • Subtract whole numbers with several digits, borrowing when you need to, even when there are zeros.
  • Check an addition or a subtraction with the opposite operation.

1Before we start: line up the numbers

In Lesson 1.1 you learned that each digit has a value because of its place: ones, tens, hundreds, thousands. That idea is what makes adding and subtracting work.

Today's golden rule is simple: only add or subtract digits that are in the same place. Ones with ones, tens with tens, hundreds with hundreds. So the first step is always to line up the numbers on the right, one under the other.

Key idea

Adding and subtracting in columns works because each column is a different place. Keeping the columns straight is not just neatness: it is what makes the answer correct.

2Adding with regrouping ("carrying")

Add one column at a time, from right to left. When a column adds up to 10 or more, the answer does not fit in one place. So we regroup: we write the ones digit and carry the 1 to the next column on the left.

It is like trading ten $1 bills for one $10 bill.

Example: 367 + 158

1Ones: 7 + 8 = 15. Write 5 and carry 1 to the tens.

2Tens: 1 (carried) + 6 + 5 = 12. Write 2 and carry 1 to the hundreds.

3Hundreds: 1 (carried) + 3 + 1 = 5. Write 5.

✓Result: 525.

Watch out!

When you add two numbers, the number you carry is always 1. (If you add many numbers, it can be 2 or more.) Do not forget to add it in the next column. This is the most common mistake.

3Subtracting with regrouping ("borrowing")

Subtract one column at a time, from right to left. When the top digit in a column is smaller than the bottom digit, you cannot subtract right away. So we borrow: we take 1 from the column on the left. That 1 is worth 10 in our column.

It is like breaking one $10 bill into ten $1 bills.

Example: 624 − 158

1Ones: 4 − 8? You cannot. Borrow from the tens: the 4 becomes 14, and the 2 in the tens becomes 1. Now 14 − 8 = 6.

2Tens: 1 is left, and 1 − 5? You cannot. Borrow from the hundreds: the 1 becomes 11, and the 6 becomes 5. Now 11 − 5 = 6.

3Hundreds: 5 is left. 5 − 1 = 4.

✓Result: 466.

The most common mistake

Some students subtract the smaller digit from the larger one in every column, no matter which one is on top. For 624 − 158 that gives 534, which is wrong:

624
−158
534

Always subtract the bottom digit from the top digit. If the top digit is smaller, borrow.

Subtracting when there are zeros

Subtracting from a number with zeros, like 4,003, looks hard, but the rule is the same. If the column on the left is 0, it has nothing to lend, so it borrows from the next column first. Each zero that lends becomes 9.

Example: 4,003 − 1,547

1Ones: 3 − 7? You cannot. The tens are 0 and the hundreds are 0, so the borrow comes from the thousands.

2The 4 becomes 3. The first 0 becomes 9. The second 0 becomes 9. The 3 in the ones becomes 13.

3Now subtract each column: 13 − 7 = 6, 9 − 4 = 5, 9 − 5 = 4, 3 − 1 = 2.

✓Result: 2,456.

4Checking with the opposite operation

Addition and subtraction are opposite operations (also called inverse operations): one undoes the other. This gives you a strong way to check your answer, without a calculator.

How to check

To check an addition: subtract one of the numbers you added from the answer. You should get the other number.
367 + 158 = 525 → check: 525 − 158 = 367 ✓

To check a subtraction: add the number you subtracted to the answer. You should get the number you started with.
624 − 158 = 466 → check: 466 + 158 = 624 ✓

About the calculator

In this lesson, use a calculator only to check your work, not to replace the steps on paper. Knowing the steps helps you catch mistakes, and part of the GED® math test is taken without a calculator.

5Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open. Use the examples and the self-check checklist to review your work and decide what to practice next.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • With paper and pencil. Do eight problems with the steps you learned: four additions and four subtractions that need regrouping. Check each one with the opposite operation. If the two do not match, there is a mistake in one of them.
  • In your daily life. Take a receipt or a bill from home and round the amounts to whole dollars. Add two amounts and subtract a third. Check your answer with the opposite operation before you look at a calculator.

📘Key words

sum
The answer to an addition. In 367 + 158 = 525, the sum is 525.
difference
The answer to a subtraction. In 624 − 158 = 466, the difference is 466.
line up
To write numbers one under the other so that ones are under ones, tens under tens, and so on.
regroup
To trade 10 of one place for 1 of the next place, or 1 of a place for 10 of the place to its right.
carry
To move a 1 to the next column on the left when a column adds up to 10 or more.
borrow
To take 1 from the column on the left, which is worth 10 in your column.
opposite (inverse) operations
Two operations that undo each other, like addition and subtraction.
