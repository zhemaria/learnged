Lesson 7.1 · Classifying 2D shapes: attributes and vocabulary

Sides, angles, and parallel lines: learn the words of geometry and name every shape by its attributes.

⏱️ About 4 hours with practice and homework · take breaks 📐 The right angle as a guide

🎯What you will learn

When you finish this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Use the basic words of geometry: side, vertex, parallel, perpendicular.
  • Classify angles as acute, right, obtuse, or straight.
  • Classify triangles by their sides and by their angles.
  • Name polygons and quadrilaterals by their attributes.

1The words of geometry

Geometry is the part of math that studies shapes. To talk about shapes clearly, we need a few words. These words appear in every geometry problem on the GED® test.

The basic words of geometry and what they mean
WordWhat it means
line segmentA straight piece of a line, with two endpoints.
sideEach line segment that forms a shape.
vertex (plural: vertices)A corner, where two sides meet.
parallelLines that never meet and are always the same distance apart, like the rails of a train track.
perpendicularLines that meet in a square corner, like the corner of a sheet of paper.
polygonA closed shape made only of straight sides.

The key idea of this lesson

We classify shapes by their attributes: how many sides they have, whether the sides are equal, whether they are parallel, and what kind of angles they have. The name of a shape is a short way to say all that.

2Angles

An angle is the opening between two sides that meet at a vertex. We measure it in degrees (°). The key angle is the right angle: a square corner, exactly 90°. We compare every other angle with it.

The four kinds of angles and their measures
AngleMeasureHow it looks
acuteless than 90°smaller than a square corner
rightexactly 90°a square corner (marked with a small square)
obtusemore than 90° and less than 180°more open than a square corner
straightexactly 180°a straight line

↔ Slide the table sideways to see all the columns.

A trick with a sheet of paper

The corner of a sheet of paper is a right angle. Put it on an angle: if the angle fits inside the corner, it is acute; if it opens wider, it is obtuse.

3Triangles

A triangle is a polygon with 3 sides and 3 angles. We can classify it in two ways: by its sides or by its angles. Equal sides are shown with the same little marks.

Triangles classified by their sides and by their angles
By its sidesBy its angles
equilateral: 3 equal sidesacute: 3 acute angles
isosceles: 2 equal sidesright: 1 right angle
scalene: no equal sidesobtuse: 1 obtuse angle

↔ Slide the table sideways to see all the columns.

Watch out! The three angles add up to 180°

In every triangle, the three angles add up to 180°. That is why a triangle can have only one right angle or one obtuse angle: two of them would already add up to 180° or more.

Quadrilaterals (4 sides) have their own names, depending on their sides and angles:

The kinds of quadrilaterals and their attributes
NameAttributes
parallelogram2 pairs of parallel sides
rectanglea parallelogram with 4 right angles
rhombusa parallelogram with 4 equal sides
square4 equal sides and 4 right angles
trapezoidonly 1 pair of parallel sides

↔ Slide the table sideways to see all the columns.

A family of shapes

A square has all the attributes of a rectangle (4 right angles) and all the attributes of a rhombus (4 equal sides). So every square is also a rectangle and a rhombus. But not every rectangle is a square.

5Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. Draw one example of each: an acute, a right, and an obtuse angle; an equilateral, an isosceles, and a scalene triangle; a square, a rectangle, a rhombus, and a trapezoid. Mark equal sides and right angles.
  • In your daily life. Go for a walk or look around your home and find 10 shapes: windows, signs, tiles, roofs. Write the name of each shape and one of its attributes.

📘Key words

vertex
A corner of a shape, where two sides meet.
angle
The opening between two sides that meet at a vertex, measured in degrees (°).
right angle
An angle of exactly 90°, a square corner.
parallel
Lines that never meet and are always the same distance apart.
perpendicular
Lines that meet in a right angle.
polygon
A closed shape made only of straight sides.
quadrilateral
A polygon with 4 sides.
