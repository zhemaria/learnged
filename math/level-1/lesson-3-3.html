1Two ways to write the same thing

In Lesson 3.1 you saw that a decimal is a part of a whole. A fraction is a part of a whole too. In fact, they are two ways to write the same value.

Think about money: half a dollar can be written as the fraction 121/2 of a dollar, or as the decimal $0.50. It is the same amount of money. Only the way you write it changes.

Key idea

The bar in a fraction means "divided by." The fraction 343/4 means, exactly, 3 ÷ 4. That is the key to changing any fraction to a decimal.

2From fraction to decimal: divide

To change a fraction to a decimal, divide the numerator (top) by the denominator (bottom). Use the division with decimals you learned in Lesson 3.2: if the numerator is smaller, write a point and zeros after it.

Example: change 343/4 to a decimal

1Step 1. Set up the division: 3 ÷ 4. Since 3 is less than 4, write 3 as 3.00 and put the point in the answer.

2Step 2. Divide: 30 ÷ 4 = 7, remainder 2. Bring down the next zero: 20 ÷ 4 = 5, with no remainder.

✓Result. 343/4 = 0.75, just like 75 cents.

🧮 Check it on the calculator

Solve on paper first. Then clear the memory (2ndsto→ and 1) and check in two ways:

  1. As a division: type 3÷4enter. The screen shows 0.75.
  2. As a fraction: press n/d, type 3, go down with ▼, type 4, leave with ►, and press enter. The screen shows 343/4. Now press ⟷: it changes to 0.75.

The calculator confirms; the paper teaches you.

3From decimal to fraction: read and simplify

To change a decimal to a fraction, use what you learned in Lesson 3.1: the name of the last place gives you the denominator.

  • If the decimal ends in the tenths → the denominator is 10.
  • If it ends in the hundredths → the denominator is 100.
  • If it ends in the thousandths → the denominator is 1,000.

Example: change 0.6 to a fraction

1Step 1. 0.6 is "six tenths." The numerator is 6 and the denominator is 10: 6106/10.

2Step 2. Simplify: 6 and 10 can both be divided by 2. 6 ÷ 2 = 3, and 10 ÷ 2 = 5.

✓Result. 0.6 = 353/5.

Watch out!

A fraction is simplified when the numerator and the denominator cannot both be divided by the same number anymore. You will study simplifying in depth in Topic 4. For now, it is enough to simplify the easy ones.

🧮 Check it on the calculator

The calculator can change a decimal to a fraction with the green function f◂▸d (above the table key):

  1. Type 0.6.
  2. Press 2nd and then table (f◂▸d).
  3. Press enter. The screen shows 353/5, already simplified.

A special case: 1/3

When you divide 1 ÷ 3, the answer is 0.3333… and the 3s never end. This is a repeating decimal. In daily life we round it to 0.33. You will see repeating decimals in more detail in later levels.

5Decimals in real-life problems

Decimals are everywhere: shopping, distances, measurements, and change. Always use the 4-step method from Lesson 1.3: read and underline, choose the operation, solve, and check that the answer makes sense.

Example: a trip to the grocery store

"You buy bread for $2.50, milk for $3.75, and eggs for $4.20. If you pay with $20, how much change do you get?"

1Underline: prices $2.50, $3.75, $4.20; you pay $20; the question asks for the change.

2Choose: first add the prices (put together), then subtract from what you paid (change). It takes two steps.

3Solve: 2.50 + 3.75 + 4.20 = 10.45. Then 20.00 − 10.45 = 9.55.

✓Does it make sense? The change ($9.55) is less than what you paid ($20). The answer is $9.55.

🧮 Check it on the calculator

Check each step: 2.5+3.75+4.2enter shows 10.45. Then 20−10.45enter shows 9.55.

Tip: right after the first answer, you can press − and the calculator writes ans (the last answer). For the change, though, you need 20 − 10.45, so type the whole subtraction.

6Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open. With it, you finish Topic 3.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. Write the table of pairs from memory (1/2, 1/4, 3/4, 1/5, 1/10, 1/3). Then check each one by dividing, on paper and with the calculator. Do it again the next day without looking.
  • In your daily life. Find a real price in an online store and write it as a fraction of a dollar. Then look at a recipe: write the measures that come as fractions (1/2 cup, 3/4 tablespoon) as decimals.

📘Key words

numerator, denominator
The top and the bottom numbers of a fraction. In 3/4, the numerator is 3 and the denominator is 4.
equivalent
Equal in value, even if written in a different way. 1/2 and 0.5 are equivalent.
simplify a fraction
To divide the numerator and the denominator by the same number until you cannot do it anymore. 6/10 simplifies to 3/5.
terminating decimal
A decimal that ends, like 0.75.
repeating decimal
A decimal with digits that repeat forever, like 0.333… (1/3).
change (money)
The money you get back when you pay more than the price.
