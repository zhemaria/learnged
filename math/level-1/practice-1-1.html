The place value chart

›

Where a digit is decides what it is worth. The digit 5 is worth five in 5, fifty in 50, and five hundred in 500. Each time a digit moves one place to the left, it is worth 10 times more. We split big numbers into groups of three digits: ones, thousands, and millions. In writing, a comma separates the groups: 3,482,651.

Reading and writing numbers

›

To read a number, read each group of three digits and then say the group's name, from left to right: "three million, four hundred eighty-two thousand, six hundred fifty-one." The last group (the ones) has no name at the end.

From numbers to words

Zero holds the place: in 208,040 the zeros tell you there are no ten thousands, no hundreds, and no ones. If you took them out, the number would change completely.

Comparing and ordering

›

We use three symbols: > means "is greater than," < means "is less than," and = means "is equal to." Think of the symbol as an open mouth: it always "eats" the bigger number.

How to compare two numbers
StepWhat to do
1Count the digits. The number with more digits is greater.
2If they have the same number of digits, compare from left to right.
3The first digit that is different decides.

    Rounding

    ›

    Rounding means changing a number to a close number that is easier to use: $19 is "about twenty dollars." Use the neighbor on the right rule: look at the digit just to the right of the place you are rounding to. If it is 0, 1, 2, 3, or 4, the place stays the same. If it is 5, 6, 7, 8, or 9, the place goes up by one. Every digit to the right becomes zero.
