Lesson 6.1 · U.S. customary units: length, weight, and capacity

Four practice activities on one page. The exercises change every time. If you make a mistake, you get a hint and another try.

On this page

  1. Choose the right unit
  2. Convert units

1

Choose the right unit

›

Picture the thing you want to measure. Small things use small units (inch, ounce, fluid ounce); big things use big units (mile, ton, gallon).

The units, from small to big
MeasureUnits
Lengthinch → foot → yard → mile
Weightounce → pound → ton
Capacityfluid ounce → cup → pint → quart → gallon

Practice: which unit?

Convert units

›
Equivalences
LengthWeightCapacity
1 ft = 12 in1 lb = 16 oz1 c = 8 fl oz
1 yd = 3 ft = 36 in1 T = 2,000 lb1 pt = 2 c · 1 qt = 2 pt
1 mi = 5,280 ft1 gal = 4 qt = 16 c
Big to small: multiply. Small to big: divide. Going to a smaller unit, you need more of them.

3

Measures with two units

›

Divide by the equivalence. The quotient is the big unit, and the remainder is the small unit: 75 in ÷ 12 = 6, remainder 3, so 75 in = 6 ft 3 in.

Practice: write it with two units

4

Compare measures

›

To compare 2 pounds and 30 ounces, first put both in the same unit: 2 lb = 32 oz. Now compare 32 and 30.

Practice: which is more?
