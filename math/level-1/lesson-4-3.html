Lesson 4.3 · Multiplying and dividing fractions

The good news: multiplying and dividing fractions is simpler than adding them. With this lesson, you complete the four operations with fractions.

⏱️ About 4 hours with practice and homework · take breaks 🔄 Multiply and divide

🎯What you will learn

When you finish this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Multiply fractions: numerator times numerator and denominator times denominator.
  • Find the reciprocal of a fraction (the fraction flipped upside down).
  • Divide fractions by multiplying by the reciprocal of the second one.
  • Solve real-life problems with fractions, and simplify the answer.

1A pleasant surprise

In Lesson 4.2, adding and subtracting fractions took some work: you had to find a common denominator first. Today there is good news.

Multiplying and dividing fractions is easier than adding them. You do not need a common denominator. The rules are short and direct. You only need to learn them well and use them carefully.

The key idea

Multiplying a fraction by a fraction answers the question "how much is a part of a part?" For example, half of half a pizza is a quarter of the pizza: 121/2 of 121/2 = 141/4. The word "of" almost always means multiply.

Half (purple) of a half (blue): the dark part is 1 of 4 equal parts, 141/4 of the whole.

2Multiply fractions: straight across

Multiplying fractions is the most direct operation of all.

To multiply fractions

Numerator × numerator on top, and denominator × denominator on the bottom. Then simplify if you can.

Example: 232/3 × 454/5

1Step 1. Multiply the numerators: 2 × 4 = 8.

2Step 2. Multiply the denominators: 3 × 5 = 15.

3Step 3. Check if you can simplify: 8 and 15 share no factor greater than 1. It is already in simplest form.

✓Result. 232/3 × 454/5 = 8158/15.

2 of the 3 columns and 4 of the 5 rows: they overlap in 8 of the 15 small parts, 8158/15.

Watch out!

When you multiply fractions, you do not look for a common denominator. That was for adding and subtracting. Here you multiply straight across. And always check at the end if the answer can be simplified.

🧮 Check it on the calculator

To check 232/3 × 454/5:

  1. Press n/d, type 2, go down with ▼, type 3, and leave with ►.
  2. Press ×.
  3. Press n/d, type 4, go down with ▼, type 5, and leave with ►.
  4. Press enter. The screen shows 8158/15.

3The reciprocal: the flipped fraction

Before you divide fractions, you need one simple idea: the reciprocal.

The reciprocal of a fraction is the same fraction flipped upside down: the numerator goes to the bottom, and the denominator goes to the top.

The reciprocal of 343/4 is 434/3.

Examples of reciprocals

1The reciprocal of 252/5 is 525/2.

2The reciprocal of 181/8 is 818/1, which is the same as 8.

3The reciprocal of a whole number like 4 is 141/4. Every whole number is itself over 1: 4 = 414/1.

A way to check

A number times its reciprocal always equals 1. For example, 343/4 × 434/3 = 121212/12 = 1.

4Divide fractions: keep, change, flip

Here is the most useful trick in Topic 4. To divide fractions, you do not really divide: you change the division into a multiplication.

To divide fractions: keep, change, flip

Keep the first fraction as it is · change ÷ to × · flip the second fraction (use its reciprocal). Then multiply.

Example: 343/4 ÷ 121/2

1Keep. The first fraction stays the same: 343/4.

2Change. The ÷ becomes ×.

3Flip. 121/2 becomes its reciprocal, 212/1.

4Multiply. 343/4 × 212/1 = 646/4.

5Simplify. Divide the top and the bottom by 2: 646/4 = 323/2 = 1 121/2.

✓Result. 343/4 ÷ 121/2 = 323/2, or 1 121/2.

Does it make sense? Dividing by 121/2 asks "how many halves fit in 343/4?" One half fits, and then half of another half: one and a half. That is why dividing by a fraction less than 1 gives a bigger answer.

Watch out! The most common mistake

Flip only the second fraction, the one after the ÷ sign. The first one stays exactly the same. And never forget to change the ÷ to ×.

🧮 Check it on the calculator

The calculator divides fractions too. For 343/4 ÷ 121/2, write 343/4 with n/d, press ÷, write 121/2, and press enter. The screen shows 323/2.

5Fractions in real-life problems

Always use the 4-step method from Lesson 1.3. Remember the key clue: the word "of" usually means multiply. Cutting or sharing something into pieces of a fractional size ("how many pieces of …?", "how many servings of …?") usually means divide.

📋 "A recipe calls for 343/4 cup of sugar. You are making only half of the recipe. How much sugar do you need?"

1Read and underline. 343/4 cup of sugar; you want half (121/2) of the recipe.

2Choose the operation. "Half of 343/4": the word "of" means multiply. It is 121/2 × 343/4.

3Solve. 121/2 × 343/4 = 383/8.

✓Does it make sense? 383/8 cup is less than 343/4 cup. That makes sense, because you are making half the recipe. You need 383/8 cup of sugar.

6Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you. In the last activity, multiplying and dividing are mixed: the hard part is not the math, it is deciding which one the problem needs.
  • With a sheet of paper. Fold a sheet in half and shade that half. Fold it in half again the other way, and shade half of the shaded part with another color. Open it and count the parts: there it is, in your hands, why 121/2 × 121/2 = 141/4. Repeat with thirds: fold in three, then in half. The area model on the practice page shows the same idea.
  • In your kitchen. Take a recipe. Find how much of each ingredient you would need for half the recipe (multiply by 121/2) and for double the recipe (multiply by 2). Write the two columns next to the original amounts.

📘Key words

product
The answer to a multiplication. The product of 1/2 and 3/4 is 3/8.
quotient
The answer to a division. The quotient of 3/4 ÷ 1/2 is 3/2.
reciprocal
A fraction flipped upside down. The reciprocal of 3/4 is 4/3. A number times its reciprocal equals 1.
keep, change, flip
The steps to divide fractions: keep the first fraction, change ÷ to ×, and flip the second fraction.
"of"
In a problem with fractions, "of" usually means multiply: half of 3/4 is 1/2 × 3/4.
area model
A rectangle cut into rows and columns that shows the product of two fractions as the part where they overlap.
