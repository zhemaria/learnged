Math · Level 1 · Topic 5 — Patterns and algebraic thinking

Lesson 5.1 · Number and shape patterns

Find the hidden rule behind a list of numbers or shapes: your first step toward algebra.

⏱️ About 4 hours with practice and homework · take breaks 🔍 Find the rule

🎯What you will learn

When you finish this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Find the rule of a number pattern or a shape pattern.
  • Continue a pattern for at least three more terms.
  • Find a missing term in the middle of a pattern.
  • Explain the rule of a pattern in clear words.

1A new kind of thinking

In Topics 1 to 4 you learned to calculate: to add, subtract, multiply, and divide whole numbers, decimals, and fractions. That is arithmetic.

Topic 5 takes a new step. Now you will not only find an answer; you will discover the idea hidden behind the numbers. When you see 2, 4, 6, 8, … your mind notices something: "they go up by two." That is a pattern, and seeing it is the first step toward algebra.

The key idea of this lesson

A pattern (or sequence) is a list of numbers or shapes that follows a rule. If you discover the rule, you can predict what comes next, even if you have never seen it.

2Number patterns

In a number pattern, each number is called a term. The rule tells you how to go from one term to the next. To discover it, look at what changes from one term to the next.

Patterns that add or subtract

Look at this pattern. What happens from one number to the next?

13
+5
18
+5

Example: find the rule of 3, 8, 13, 18, …

1Find the jump. From 3 to 8 is + 5; from 8 to 13 is + 5; from 13 to 18 is + 5.

2Say the rule. Add 5 each time.

✓Use the rule. The next term is 18 + 5 = 23.

A pattern can also go down. In 40, 36, 32, 28, … the jump is − 4 each time, so the rule is subtract 4 each time, and the next term is 28 − 4 = 24.

Patterns that multiply

Not every pattern adds. In this one, you get each term by multiplying:

2
×2
4
×2
8
×2
16
×2
?

Here the rule is multiply by 2 each time. The next term is 16 × 2 = 32.

A tip to find the rule

First ask yourself: do the numbers grow slowly or very fast? If they grow slowly and evenly, the rule usually adds. If they grow very fast (they double or triple), the rule usually multiplies. Then check your rule on every pair of terms, not just the first two.

🧮 Check it on the calculator

The calculator can repeat a rule for you. If you press enter on an empty line, it does the last calculation again with the new answer.

  1. Type the first term, 3, and press enter.
  2. Press + 5 enter. The calculator writes ans+5 (the last answer plus 5) and shows 8.
  3. Press enter again and again. Each time it adds 5 one more time: 13, 18, 23, 28.
  4. For a pattern that multiplies, do the same with ×: 2 enter, then × 2 enter, then enter enter. You get 4, 8, 16, 32.

3Shape patterns

Patterns are not only made of numbers: shapes can follow a rule too. Look at how these squares grow:

The number of small squares makes a number pattern: 1, 4, 9, … Each figure is a square with as many rows as its number: figure 4 will have 4 rows of 4, that is, 4 × 4 = 16 squares.

The trick

You can turn any shape pattern into a number pattern by counting something: how many squares, how many dots, how many sides. Once you have the numbers, look for the rule just as you did in section 2.

4Input-output tables

A very useful way to show a rule is an input-output table. The input is the number you put in. The output is the number that comes out after you use the rule. You can think of a machine: a number goes in, the machine uses its rule, and a new number comes out.

Input-output table: each input and its output
InputOutput
14
25
36
47

Example: find the rule of the table

1Look across each row. 1 gives 4, 2 gives 5, 3 gives 6.

2Ask what happened to each input. 3 was added to each one: 1 + 3 = 4, 2 + 3 = 5, 3 + 3 = 6, 4 + 3 = 7.

✓Say the rule. The output is the input plus 3. If the input were 10, the output would be 13.

Watch out!

In an input-output table, the rule connects each input with its own output. It does not compare one row with the next. Always read across the row: input → output.

5Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. Make three patterns of your own: one that adds, one that subtracts, and one that multiplies. Write five terms of each. For each one, write the rule in words before you write the next term: if you cannot say the rule, you have not found it yet. Then make an input-output table with five rows and write its rule.
  • In your daily life. Look for a real pattern: the money you save each week, the times a bus stops at your corner, the house numbers on your street. Write the numbers as a list, describe the rule in words, and predict the next two terms.

📘Key words

pattern (sequence)
A list of numbers or shapes that follows a rule, like 3, 8, 13, 18, …
term
Each number in a pattern. In 3, 8, 13, the second term is 8.
rule
What you do to go from one term to the next, or from an input to its output, like "add 5 each time."
input
The number you put into a rule or a table.
output
The number you get after you use the rule on the input.
input-output table
A table that shows each input next to its output. The rule connects each input with its own output.
