Math · Level 1 · Topic 4 — Fractions

Lesson 4.1 · Equivalent fractions: simplify and compare

The same amount can be written in many ways. Learn to recognize those ways, simplify them, and compare them.

⏱️ About 4 hours with practice and homework · take breaks 🍕 Fraction bars

🎯What you will learn

When you finish this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Name the parts of a fraction: the numerator and the denominator.
  • Recognize and make equivalent fractions (fractions with the same value).
  • Simplify a fraction to its simplest form.
  • Compare two fractions to decide which one is greater.

1What is a fraction?

In Topic 3 you learned that a decimal is a part of a whole. A fraction is another way to write those parts, and many times it is the most natural one: "half an hour," "a quarter pound," "three quarters of a tank of gas."

A fraction has two numbers separated by a bar:

  • The numerator (top): how many parts we have.
  • The denominator (bottom): how many equal parts the whole was divided into.

For example, 343/4 means: the whole was divided into 4 equal parts, and we take 3 of them.

Three fourths: 3 parts shaded out of 4 = 343/4

Connection to Topic 3

Remember from Lesson 3.3: the fraction bar means "divided by." 343/4 is the same as 3 ÷ 4 = 0.75. Fractions and decimals are two languages for the same idea.

2Equivalent fractions

Two fractions are equivalent when they show the same amount, even though they are written with different numbers. Look at these two bars:

121/2 — one half

242/4 — also one half

The shaded part is exactly the same size: 121/2 and 242/4 are equivalent fractions.

The golden rule

If you multiply (or divide) the numerator and the denominator by the same number, you get an equivalent fraction. Whatever you do to the top, you do to the bottom.

Example: make fractions equivalent to 232/3

1Step 1. Multiply the top and the bottom by 2: 2 × 23 × 22 × 2/3 × 2 = 464/6.

2Step 2. Multiply the top and the bottom by 3: 2 × 33 × 32 × 3/3 × 3 = 696/9.

✓Result. 232/3 = 464/6 = 696/9. They all have the same value.

3Simplify a fraction

To simplify (or reduce) a fraction means to write it with the smallest numbers possible, without changing its value. You do it by dividing the numerator and the denominator by the same number.

A fraction is in its simplest form when no number greater than 1 divides both the numerator and the denominator evenly.

Example: simplify 8128/12

1Step 1. Find a number that divides both 8 and 12. The number 4 works: 8 ÷ 4 = 2, and 12 ÷ 4 = 3.

2Step 2. You get 232/3.

3Step 3. Can you keep going? 2 and 3 share no factor greater than 1. It is already in simplest form.

✓Result. 8128/12 = 232/3.

Tip

If you do not find the biggest number right away, that is fine. Divide by a small number (like 2) and repeat: 8128/12 → ÷ 2 → 464/6 → ÷ 2 → 232/3. You get the same answer in more steps.

🧮 Check it on the calculator

The calculator always shows fractions in simplest form. Use it to check your work:

  1. Press n/d, type 8, go down with ▼, type 12, and leave with ►.
  2. Press enter. The screen shows 232/3.

Simplify on paper first. If your answer and the screen do not match, check whether you can still divide.

4Compare fractions

To decide which of two fractions is greater, there are two cases.

Case 1: the same denominator

If the two fractions have the same denominator, it is easy: the greater one has the greater numerator. The parts are the same size, so more parts means more.

Example: compare 383/8 and 585/8

1Step 1. Same denominator (8): the parts are the same size.

✓Result. 5 parts are more than 3 parts: 585/8 > 383/8.

Case 2: different denominators

If the denominators are different, first make them the same: find equivalent fractions with the same denominator. Then compare the numerators.

Example: compare 232/3 and 343/4

1Step 1. Find a common denominator. 12 works for both 3 and 4.

2Step 2. Change them: 232/3 = 8128/12 (× 4), and 343/4 = 9129/12 (× 3).

3Step 3. Compare the numerators: 9 is more than 8.

✓Result. 343/4 > 232/3.

Watch out!

A greater denominator does not mean a greater fraction. 181/8 is less than 121/2: the more parts you cut the whole into, the smaller each part is.

🧮 Another way to check: change to decimals

Fractions and decimals are two languages for the same idea. To compare 232/3 and 343/4, you can change each one to a decimal with ÷: 2÷3enter shows 0.666… and 3÷4enter shows 0.75. Since 0.75 is greater, 343/4 is greater.

5Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. Draw two bars of the same length. Divide one into 4 parts and the other into 8. Shade 3/4 of the first one, and find how many eighths you need to shade to reach the same length. Do it again with 1/2 and sixths, and with 2/3 and twelfths. Seeing the bars side by side is what convinces you that two different fractions can be worth the same.
  • In your kitchen. Look at your measuring cups and spoons. How many 1/4 cups fill a 1/2 cup? How many teaspoons make a tablespoon? Write each answer as an equation with fractions, for example 2 × 1/4 cup = 1/2 cup.

📘Key words

fraction
A number that shows parts of a whole, like 3/4.
numerator
The top number of a fraction: how many parts you have.
denominator
The bottom number of a fraction: how many equal parts the whole was divided into.
equivalent fractions
Fractions with the same value, like 1/2, 2/4, and 5/10.
simplify (reduce)
To divide the top and the bottom by the same number to write the fraction with smaller numbers.
simplest form
A fraction whose top and bottom share no factor greater than 1, like 2/3.
common denominator
The same denominator for two or more fractions, so you can compare, add, or subtract them.
