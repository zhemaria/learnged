Decimals never end

To order decimals, you place them on the number line. But the number line hides three ideas that often confuse people: between two decimals there are endless more decimals; adding zeros on the right does not change the value; and rounding means choosing where to stop. Explore each idea with your own hands.

Between 0 and 1 there are endless decimals. And between any two of them, too. Zoom in and see for yourself: each step makes the piece of the line ten times smaller… and it never ends.

2. Is 0.1 the same as 0.10 and 0.100?

Many people think 0.10 is “greater than” or “different from” 0.1 because it has more digits. Watch the orange point: when you add zeros, the ruler gets finer, but the point does not move at all.

The orange point marks the value 0.1. Press “Add a zero” and see that the label changes, but the position does not.

3. So what does rounding mean?

Going down forever is impossible, so at some point you have to stop at one level. That is exactly what rounding is: choosing how many decimal places to keep and taking the closest mark. Let’s see where 1/7 = 0.142857… lands.

Choose how many decimal places to round to. The orange point is the real value of 1/7; the blue mark is the closest one, the one we round to.

Why do we never reach the end?

Show the math explanation

Take two different decimals, no matter how close; for example, 0.14 and 0.15. Their average, 0.145, sits exactly between them and is different from both. You can average that number again with either neighbor to get another number in between, and so on, forever.

This property is called the density of numbers: between any two numbers there is always a third one. So any piece of the number line, no matter how tiny, holds endless decimals.

Zeros on the right add no value because they do not change the position of the point: 0.1, 0.10, and 0.100 name the same place. And rounding does not go against the endless decimals; it accepts them: since going down never ends, we decide which level to stop at and choose the closest mark.
