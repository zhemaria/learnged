Place value after the decimal point

›

Decimals are the place value chart continued to the right. The point separates the whole part from the parts: in $3.99 there are 3 whole dollars and 99 cents. To the right of the point are the tenths, the hundredths, and the thousandths. Each place is worth 10 times less than the place before it.

Put a decimal in the chart

Press a digit in the chart to see what it is worth.

The last place gives the name: 0.7 is seven tenths; 0.07 is seven hundredths; 0.007 is seven thousandths.

Compare and order decimals

›

Compare place by place, from left to right. If the numbers have a different number of digits, add zeros on the right. They do not change the value: 0.6 = 0.60 = 0.600.

Compare on the number line

The most common mistake: thinking that 0.58 is greater than 0.6 "because it has more digits." With decimals, more digits do not mean more value.

3

Round decimals

›

It is the same neighbor-on-the-right rule from Lesson 1.1: if the neighbor is 0 to 4, the digit stays the same; if it is 5 to 9, the digit goes up by one. The difference: the digits on the right are dropped. They do not turn into zeros.
