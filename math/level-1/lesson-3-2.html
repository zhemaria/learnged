Lesson 3.2 · Operations with decimals

Add, subtract, multiply, and divide decimals. And meet the TI-30XS calculator, your tool to check your work.

⏱️ About 4 hours with practice and homework · take breaks 🧮 TI-30XS calculator

🎯What you will learn

When you finish this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Add and subtract decimals by lining up the decimal points.
  • Multiply decimals by counting the decimal places in the answer.
  • Divide decimals by moving the point in the divisor and in the dividend.
  • Use the TI-30XS calculator to check your answers, without replacing your work on paper.

1Your new tool: the TI-30XS calculator

Starting with this lesson, you will use the TI-30XS MultiView calculator. It is the calculator you can use on the GED test. It is a powerful tool, but you need to use it with good judgment.

🧮 The calculator on this page

This page has a calculator that works like the TI-30XS MultiView. Open it with the Calculator button at the top right of the screen, or with the button below. You can move it around the screen. The ? button on the calculator explains every key, and the Reference sheet button shows the formulas you get on the test. If you have a real TI-30XS, practice with it too, so you learn where its keys are.

Three golden rules before you start

  • Clear the memory before you calculate. Press 2nd and then sto→ (the green words above it say clear var), and choose 1 (Yes). This way no numbers from an earlier calculation get into yours. Make it a habit.
  • The calculator checks; it does not think. Solve on paper first. Use the calculator only to check. On the GED you need to understand the steps, not just press keys.
  • Check what you typed. The calculator does exactly what you type. If you put a decimal point in the wrong place, the answer will be wrong, and the calculator will not warn you.
Why paper first?

When you know how to work with decimals by hand, you can notice when the calculator shows something that makes no sense. Without that skill, a typing mistake goes unnoticed. The calculator is your assistant, not your replacement.

2Add and subtract decimals: line up the points

Adding and subtracting decimals works just like it does with whole numbers (Lesson 1.2), with one new rule: line up the decimal points. The points go one under the other, in a straight column.

If the numbers have a different number of decimal places, add zeros on the right so they match. Remember from Lesson 3.1: those zeros do not change the value.

Example: 12.4 + 3.85

1Step 1. Write 12.4 as 12.40, so it has as many decimal places as 3.85.

2Step 2. Line up the points, one under the other, and add column by column, like with whole numbers.

3Step 3. The point in the answer goes in the same column as the other points.

✓Result. 16.25.

12.40
+3.85
16.25
Watch out!

The most common mistake is lining up the numbers on the right, as if they were whole numbers. With decimals, you line them up by the point. Always add 12.40 and 3.85, with the points in one column.

🧮 Check it on the calculator

You already solved 12.4 + 3.85 on paper. Now check it:

  1. Type 12.4
  2. Press +, then type 3.85
  3. Press enter. The screen should show 16.25, the same as your paper.

On the calculator you do not need to add zeros or line up anything. That is exactly why you need to know how it works on paper: to tell if the screen makes sense.

3Multiply decimals: count the places

Here is a nice surprise: to multiply decimals, you ignore the point at first. Multiply as if they were whole numbers (Lesson 2.1). At the end, place the point by counting.

The counting rule

Count how many decimal places the two factors have together. The answer has that same number of decimal places.

Example: 4.6 × 2.3

1Step 1. Ignore the points and multiply: 46 × 23 = 1,058.

2Step 2. Count the decimal places: 4.6 has 1, and 2.3 has 1. That is 2 decimal places in all.

3Step 3. Place the point so the answer has 2 decimal places: 1058 → 10.58.

✓Result. 4.6 × 2.3 = 10.58.

Check with an estimate

4.6 is about 5, and 2.3 is about 2, so the answer should be close to 5 × 2 = 10. Since 10.58 is close to 10, the point is in the right place. If you got 105.8 or 1.058, you would know the point was wrong.

🧮 Check it on the calculator
  1. Type 4.6, press ×, and type 2.3
  2. Press enter. The screen should show 10.58.

Compare three answers: your paper, your estimate (about 10), and the calculator. When all three agree, you can trust your answer.

4Divide decimals: move the point

Dividing is easier when the divisor (the number you divide by) is a whole number. The trick: move the decimal point of the divisor until it is a whole number, and move the point of the dividend the same number of places.

Example: 9.45 ÷ 0.5

1Step 1. The divisor is 0.5. Move its point 1 place to the right: 0.5 → 5. Now it is a whole number.

2Step 2. Move the point of the dividend the same amount, 1 place: 9.45 → 94.5.

3Step 3. Now divide 94.5 ÷ 5 with long division. The point in the answer goes straight up from the dividend.

✓Result. 9.45 ÷ 0.5 = 18.9.

Watch out!

Both points move the same number of places, in the same direction. If you move one and forget the other, the answer will be off by 10 times or 100 times.

🧮 Check it on the calculator
  1. Type 9.45, press ÷, and type 0.5
  2. Press enter. The screen should show 18.9.

Here you do not move any point: the calculator does it for you. One more reason to check on paper first.

5Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open. Golden rule: solve on paper first, and use the calculator only to check.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. Solve twelve operations with decimals, three of each operation, and check each one with the calculator. When the answers do not match, find the mistake before you go on. It is almost always in the decimal point.
  • In your daily life. Take a real receipt. Add two prices, subtract one, and check everything with the calculator. Remember to clear the memory before you start.

📘Key words

line up the decimal points
To write the numbers so their points are one under the other, in the same column.
decimal places
The digits to the right of the point. 4.62 has 2 decimal places.
factor
Each number you multiply. In 4.6 × 2.3, the factors are 4.6 and 2.3.
dividend, divisor, quotient
In 9.45 ÷ 0.5 = 18.9, the dividend is 9.45 (what you divide), the divisor is 0.5 (what you divide by), and the quotient is 18.9 (the answer).
estimate
A quick answer with rounded numbers, to see if the exact answer makes sense. 4.6 × 2.3 is about 5 × 2 = 10.
clear the memory
To erase the numbers stored in the calculator before you begin: 2nd, sto→ (clear var), 1 (Yes).
