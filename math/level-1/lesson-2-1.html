Math · Level 1 · Topic 2 — Multiplication and division

Lesson 2.1 · Multiplying whole numbers

Multiplying is a fast way to add the same number many times. Learn the standard method with partial products.

⏱️ About 4 hours with practice and homework · take breaks ✖️ Times tables up to 12 × 12

🎯What you will learn

When you finish this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Understand multiplication as repeated addition and use the times tables up to 12 × 12.
  • Multiply numbers with several digits using the standard method with partial products.
  • Use the commutative, associative, and distributive properties to make calculations easier.
  • Check a product with an estimate (round, then multiply).

1What does multiplying mean?

In Topic 1 you learned to add. Multiplication comes from addition: multiplying is adding the same number several times, in a shorter and faster way.

Imagine you buy 4 packs of water, and each pack has 6 bottles. You could add: 6 + 6 + 6 + 6 = 24. Or you can multiply: 4 × 6 = 24. The answer is the same, but multiplying is much faster, and with big numbers you really need it.

4 rows of 6 = 24. This "array" shows why 4 × 6 = 24.

Words to know

In 4 × 6 = 24, the numbers 4 and 6 are called factors, and the answer, 24, is called the product.

2Times tables and properties

To multiply quickly, it helps to know the times tables up to 12 × 12. This is not about memorizing under pressure. Think of them as a tool you keep close, like a carpenter knows the measurements.

Three properties that make life easier

1Commutative: the order of the factors does not change the product. 7 × 8 = 8 × 7 = 56

2Associative: when you multiply three numbers, you can group them in any way. (2 × 5) × 3 = 2 × (5 × 3) = 30

3Distributive: you can split a factor into easier parts. 25 × 4 = (20 × 4) + (5 × 4) = 80 + 20 = 100

Zero and one

Any number times 0 is 0 (8 × 0 = 0). Any number times 1 stays the same (8 × 1 = 8).

3Multiplying by a one-digit number

With big numbers we use the standard method: multiply one column at a time, from right to left, and regroup ("carry") the same way you do when you add.

Example: 327 × 4

Multiply each digit by 4, from right to left.

1Ones: 4 × 7 = 28. Write 8 and carry 2.

2Tens: 4 × 2 = 8, plus the 2 you carried = 10. Write 0 and carry 1.

3Hundreds: 4 × 3 = 12, plus the 1 you carried = 13. Write 13.

✓Result: 1,308.

Watch out!

First multiply, then add the number you carried. When you multiply, the number you carry can be bigger than 1.

4Multiplying by a two-digit number

When the second factor has two digits, we do two partial products and then add them. Each partial product comes from one digit of the second factor.

Example: 246 × 35

Multiply by 5, then by 30, and add.

1First partial product, by the ones (5): 246 × 5 = 1,230.

2Second partial product, by the tens (3): 246 × 3 = 738. But this 3 is worth 30, so move the answer one place to the left: it becomes 7,380.

3Add the partial products: 1,230 + 7,380 = 8,610.

✓Result: 8,610.

The second partial product moves one place to the left because the 3 is in the tens place.

The most common mistake

Do not forget to move the second partial product one place to the left. Many students write a 0 in the ones place of the second row so they do not forget. That zero "holds the place."

5Checking with an estimate

After you multiply big numbers, check that the answer is reasonable. The tool is an estimate: round the factors, then multiply the round numbers, which is easy.

Check 246 × 35 = 8,610 with an estimate

1Round the factors: 246 is about 250, and 35 is about 40.

2Multiply the round numbers: 250 × 40 = 10,000 (about).

✓The exact answer, 8,610, is close to 10,000. It is reasonable. If you got 861 or 86,100, you would know there is a mistake.

Why it helps

An estimate does not give the exact answer, but it catches big mistakes: a missing zero or a partial product in the wrong place. It is your safety alarm.

6Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open. Use the examples and the self-check checklist to review your work and decide what to practice next.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Multiplication bingo. Play multiplication bingo for about ten minutes a day. A little practice often helps you remember the tables much better than one long session.
  • In your daily life. Choose something you buy often. Find how much you would spend on 12, on 24, and on 52 of them (52 is one a week for a year). Check one of your answers with an estimate.

📘Key words

factor
A number you multiply. In 4 × 6 = 24, the factors are 4 and 6.
product
The answer to a multiplication. In 4 × 6 = 24, the product is 24.
array
Objects in equal rows and columns. 4 rows of 6 show 4 × 6.
partial product
The product of a number and one digit of the other factor. You add the partial products to get the final product.
commutative property
The order of the factors does not change the product: 7 × 8 = 8 × 7.
associative property
You can group three factors in any way: (2 × 5) × 3 = 2 × (5 × 3).
distributive property
You can split a factor into parts and multiply each part: 25 × 4 = (20 × 4) + (5 × 4).
estimate
An answer that is close to the exact one, found with round numbers.
