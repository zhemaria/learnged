Lesson 7.2 · Perimeter of polygons

Fences, frames, and borders: find the distance around any shape.

1What is perimeter?

The perimeter is the distance around a shape. If you walk all the way around a park, the distance you walk is the perimeter of the park. You need the perimeter to put a fence around a yard, a frame around a picture, or a border around a room.

The key idea of this lesson

To find the perimeter of any polygon, add the lengths of all its sides. The perimeter is a length, so its unit is a unit of length: inches, feet, meters…

Example: the perimeter of the park

1List all the sides. 4, 6, 9, 8, 12.

2Add them. 4 + 6 + 9 + 8 + 12 = 39.

✓Answer with the unit. The perimeter is 39 meters.

Watch out! Count every side

The most common mistake is to forget a side. Put your finger on one corner and go around the shape, adding each side once, until you are back where you started.

2Shortcuts: rectangles and squares

Some shapes have equal sides, so we can multiply instead of adding one by one.

Perimeter formulas for rectangles, squares, and regular polygons
ShapeWhyFormula
rectangle2 lengths and 2 widthsP = 2l + 2w
square4 equal sidesP = 4s
regular polygon (all sides equal)n equal sidesP = n × s

In the formulas, l is the length, w is the width, and s is the side. You will find formulas like these on the GED® formula sheet.

Example: the rectangle above, 12 ft by 8 ft

1Use the formula. P = 2 × 12 + 2 × 8.

2Calculate. 24 + 16 = 40.

✓Check by adding. 12 + 8 + 12 + 8 = 40 ✓. The perimeter is 40 feet.

3Find a missing side

Sometimes you know the perimeter and need a side. Work backward: subtract the sides you know, or divide when the sides are equal.

Example: a rectangle has a perimeter of 50 ft and a length of 15 ft. What is its width?

1Half the perimeter. One length and one width make half of the way around: 50 ÷ 2 = 25.

2Take away the length. 25 − 15 = 10.

✓Check. 2 × 15 + 2 × 10 = 30 + 20 = 50 ✓. The width is 10 feet.

Example: a square has a perimeter of 36 cm. How long is each side?

14 equal sides. 36 ÷ 4 = 9.

✓Check. 4 × 9 = 36 ✓. Each side is 9 cm.

4Perimeter in real life

Real perimeter problems often have one more step: a gate that does not need fence, a price for each foot, or laps around a field.

Example: a yard is 30 ft by 20 ft. You will fence it, leaving a 4-ft gate. Fence costs $12 per foot. How much will the fence cost?

1Perimeter. 2 × 30 + 2 × 20 = 100 ft.

2Take away the gate. 100 − 4 = 96 ft of fence.

3Multiply by the price. 96 × $12 = $1,152.

✓Answer. The fence will cost $1,152.

Watch out! Perimeter is not area

The perimeter measures the border (fence, frame, baseboard). The area measures the surface inside (carpet, paint, grass). You will learn area in Lesson 7.3. If the problem talks about going around, it is perimeter.

5Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. Draw a rectangle, a square, a triangle, and a regular hexagon. Choose and write the length of each side, and find each perimeter. Then draw a rectangle with a perimeter of 30 units and find its missing side.
  • In your daily life. Measure the perimeter of a table, a door, and a room in your home with a tape measure. Then figure out how many feet of baseboard the room would need, leaving out the door.

📘Key words

perimeter
The distance around a shape. For a polygon, the sum of its sides.
length and width
The two different sides of a rectangle (l and w in the formulas).
regular polygon
A polygon with all its sides equal (and all its angles equal).
formula
A rule written with letters, like P = 4s.
