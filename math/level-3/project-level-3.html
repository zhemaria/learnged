Project instructions

›

This project brings together what you learned in the eight topics of Level 3, and it is the last piece of the level. Here the separate skills are all used on the same data: yours. Take your time; it is not one more exercise, it is the proof that you learned the level.

Choose a real data set from your own life (your expenses for a month, the ages in your family, the temperatures of the week, your grades for the semester) and analyze it completely. The project must include five parts:

  1. Data set: 10 to 20 real values, with their source and context described.
  2. Graph: a box plot or a histogram, with all its labels.
  3. Statistics: at least three calculations (mean, median, IQR, or another).
  4. Written interpretation: what the data say about their center, spread, and shape.
  5. Probability: at least one probability calculation connected to the context of the data.
The template calculates the statistics, draws the graph, and offers checks, but you must show your work in writing: the automatic calculation is only there to check yours.

Your work is saved only in this browser. To keep it, press “Export as PDF” and choose “Save as PDF” as the destination.

4 · Written interpretation

›

Write at least five complete sentences. A graph with no interpretation is incomplete: explain what your data say.

Writing guide: the three things to cover
  • Center: around what value are the data grouped? (mean, median)
  • Spread: how far apart are they? (range, IQR, length of the whiskers)
  • Shape: are they spread evenly, are they pulled to one side, are there unusually high or low values?
  • End with a conclusion of your own: what did you learn about the situation you measured?

Project rubric

›

Five criteria at four levels (20 points in all). Read them before you start, and come back to them when you finish to grade your own work honestly.

Rubric for the final project, with five criteria and four levels
CriterionBeginning (1)Developing (2)Proficient (3)Outstanding (4)
1. Data setMade-up data, or data with no description.Real data, but with no clear source.A real data set, well described.Real, relevant data, with the source and context explained.
2. GraphNo valid graph.A graph with mistakes or no labels.A correct box plot or histogram with all its labels.A clear, correct, well-labeled graph that fits the data.
3. StatisticsNo statistics.Fewer than three, or with mistakes.At least three correct statistics.Several statistics with no mistakes, connected to each other.
4. Written interpretationNo interpretation.A vague interpretation, or one that does not match the data.A written interpretation that matches the data.A clear, deep interpretation (center, spread, and shape).
5. ProbabilityNo probability.A probability with mistakes.A correct probability connected to the context.A correct probability, well explained and connected to the analysis.

The project grade is combined with the grade on the Level 3 final check (25 questions).
