Lesson 8.3 · Final project and strategies for the GED math test

🎓 Standards for this lesson (GED® and CCR)

This lesson works on these GED® Assessment Targets:

  • Q.6.b Represent, display, and interpret data involving one variable plots on the real number line including dot plots, histograms, and box plots.
  • Q.7.a Calculate the mean, median, mode and range. Calculate a missing data value, given the average and all the missing data values but one, as well as calculating the average, given the frequency counts of all the data values, and calculating a weighted average.
  • Q.8.b Determine the probability of simple and compound events.

Mathematical Practices you use:

  • MP.1.a Search for and recognize entry points for solving a problem.
  • MP.3.a Build steps of a line of reasoning or solution pathway, based on previous step or givens.
  • MP.4.c Display data or algebraic expressions graphically.

College and Career Readiness (CCR) Standards for Adult Education:

  • 6.SP.2 Level D Understand that a set of data collected to answer a statistical question has a distribution which can be described by its center, spread, and overall shape.
  • 6.SP.4 Level D Display numerical data in plots on a number line, including dot plots, histograms, and box plots.
  • 6.SP.5 Level D Summarize numerical data sets in relation to their context, such as by reporting the number of observations, describing the attribute under investigation and its units of measurement, and giving quantitative measures of center (median and/or mean) and variability.
  • 7.SP.8 Level D Find probabilities of compound events using organized lists, tables, tree diagrams, and simulation.

1The project “My data analysis”

This lesson closes Level 3. It brings together the number sense of Topics 1 to 3 (exponents, scientific notation, real numbers), the algebra of Topics 4 and 5 (equations, inequalities, formulas), the geometry of Topics 6 and 7 (Pythagoras, similarity, volume), and the statistics and probability of Topics 7 and 8. The final project and the preparation for the GED test put everything into practice at once.

The final project, “My data analysis”, is to analyze a real data set and present it completely. It must include five parts:

The five parts of the final project “My data analysis”
PartWhat is expected
Data setReal data (ages, expenses, temperatures…) described with their source.
GraphA box plot or a histogram, with all its labels.
StatisticsAt least three calculations: mean, median, IQR, or another one.
InterpretationA text that explains what the data say.
ProbabilityAt least one probability calculation connected to the context.

Data from your own life are more interesting

Choose a data set from your own world: your expenses for the month, the ages in your family, the temperatures this week. Analyzing something of your own makes the project more meaningful and easier to interpret.

2How to build the project

Build the project in a clear order, using the tools of Topics 7 and 8.

  1. Collect 10 to 20 real data values and put them in order.
  2. Find the statistics (mean, median, Q1, Q3, IQR).
  3. Draw the graph you chose (box plot or histogram) with its labels.
  4. Write the interpretation: shape, center, and spread of the data.
  5. Ask and answer a probability question about the context.
The interpretation is the key

The project does not end with the graph. The most valuable part is the text that explains what the data mean: where they are concentrated, how spread out they are, and what you can conclude. A graph with no interpretation is incomplete.

The project template

Use the Final project template: My data analysis. You type your data and your work, and it checks your statistics, draws the graph, checks your probability, and saves your work in this browser. When you finish, save it as a PDF.

3Strategies for the GED test

The GED Mathematical Reasoning test measures reasoning, not only calculation. These strategies help you do your best:

Strategies for the GED Mathematical Reasoning test
StrategyHow to use it
Use the formula sheetThe test gives you formulas for area, volume, and more: you do not memorize them, you learn where they are and how to use them.
Manage your timeDo not spend too long on one question; flag it and come back at the end.
Use the TI-30XS calculatorUse it for roots, powers, π, nPr, and nCr; clear it before each calculation.
Estimate firstA quick estimate helps you rule out choices that make no sense in multiple-choice questions.
Read what is askedUnderline the question and the units. Many wrong answers are right answers to a different question.

5Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. This is the last homework of Level 3 and the longest: give yourself two or three calm sessions. (1) Complete “My data analysis” in the Final project template: My data analysis, with its five parts, and save it as a PDF. (2) Let it rest for a day; then read it as if it were someone else's, with the rubric in hand, and explain it out loud for three or four minutes: what you cannot explain yet, you do not understand yet. (3) Review the whole GED formula sheet and mark the formulas you used in this level. (4) Take the Level 3 final check in one sitting, with no interruptions and without looking at the lessons.
  • In your daily life. Choose the data for your project from your own life: what you spend each day for two weeks, the minutes you sleep each night, the prices of a product in several stores, or the temperatures of the month in your city.

7Self-check checklist

Use this checklist to see how you are doing. For each skill, choose Got it, Getting there, or Needs more practice. Read it before you start the homework. Use it after to check your own work honestly.

Rubric for the final project “My data analysis” and what Got it, Getting there, and Needs more practice look like
SkillGot itGetting thereNeeds more practice
Data setReal, relevant data, with the source and context explained.Real data, but with no clear source.Made-up data, or data with no description.
GraphA correct box plot or histogram with all its labels, right for the data.A graph with mistakes or no labels.No valid graph.
StatisticsAt least three statistics with no mistakes, connected to each other.Fewer than three, or with mistakes.No statistics.
Written interpretationA clear interpretation that matches the data (center, spread, and shape).A vague interpretation, or one that does not match the data.No interpretation.
ProbabilityA correct probability, well explained and connected to the analysis.A probability with mistakes.No probability.
