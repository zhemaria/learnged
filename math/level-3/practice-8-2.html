The probability of one event

›

The probability of an event is the ratio of the favorable cases to the possible cases. It is always between 0 (impossible) and 1 (certain).

P(event) = favorable casespossible cases

Two events with replacement

›

If the object goes back after the first draw, the bag is the same for the second one: the events are independent. The probability of both is the product.

P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B)

3

Two events without replacement

›

If the object does not go back, the bag changes: there is one fewer in all, and one fewer of the kind that came out. The events are dependent.

P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B after A)

The bag that loses one

Adjust both numbers: on the second draw the denominator always goes down by 1, and the numerator goes down by 1 only if you want the same color that already came out.

Practice: without replacement

Write a fraction (like 2/7), a decimal, or a percent. You do not need to simplify.

4

From the tree to the table

›

A tree diagram shows all the possibilities of two draws: on each branch you write its probability, and the probability of a whole path is the product of its branches. The probabilities of all the paths add up to 1. A two-way table organizes the same results.

Build a tree step by step, with or without replacement, and see it become a table in Explore: from the tree to the table.
To check a tree: add the probabilities of all the paths. If they do not add up to 1, a branch is wrong.

Fraction, decimal, and percent

›

A probability can be written in three ways. The test often asks for one in particular.
