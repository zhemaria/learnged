The equation as a balance scale

›

An equation is a balanced scale: what is on the left of the equal sign weighs the same as what is on the right. To keep the balance, whatever you do to one side you do to the other. The goal is to leave the x box alone.

Solve with the balance scale

Each box weighs x and each circle weighs 1. Also try taking a weight off one side only to see what happens.

Inverse operations: to undo addition, subtract; to undo subtraction, add; to undo multiplication, divide; and to undo division, multiply. Always on both sides.

One-step and multi-step equations

›

Undo the operations in reverse order: in 2x + 5, x is first multiplied by 2 and then 5 is added; to undo it, first remove the 5 and last the 2. This five-step strategy works for any linear equation:

1Distribute to clear the parentheses, if there are any.
2Combine like terms on each side.
3Move the variables to one side of the equal sign.
4Isolate the variable with the properties of equality.
5Check by substituting into the original equation.

Type only the number, for example 4 or −3.

3

Variables on both sides

›

When x appears on both sides, first gather all the x terms on one side by subtracting one of the terms from both sides, and the constants on the other. Then isolate the variable as usual.

Which x do you move?

Both choices lead to the same solution. Compare which one leaves a positive coefficient.

A useful trick: subtract the term with the smaller coefficient. That way x keeps a positive coefficient and you avoid sign mistakes.

4

Checking the solution

›

You check every solution by substituting it into the original equation. If both sides give the same value, the solution is right. One minute of checking catches almost every sign mistake.
