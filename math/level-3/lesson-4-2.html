Lesson 4.2 · Solving linear equations: properties of equality and variables on both sides

1The equation as a balance scale

In Lesson 4.1 you learned to simplify and evaluate algebraic expressions. When two expressions are joined by an equal sign, you get an equation: 2x + 3 = 11. Unlike an expression, an equation has a solution, the value of the variable that makes it true, and this lesson teaches you to find it step by step.

An equation is like a balance scale: what is on the left of the equal sign weighs the same as what is on the right. To keep it balanced, whatever you do to one side you must do to the other. That is the idea of the properties of equality: you can add, subtract, multiply, or divide both sides by the same number.

The goal is always the same: get the variable alone on one side of the equal sign (isolate it). Each operation you use undoes something around the variable, using the inverse operation.

2Isolating the variable: one-step and multi-step equations

Undo the operations in the reverse of the PEMDAS order: first the addition or subtraction, then the multiplication or division.

Example 1: solve x + 7 = 15

1Undo “+ 7.” Subtract 7 from both sides: x + 7 − 7 = 15 − 7.

✓Answer. x = 8

Example 2: solve 2x + 5 = 13

1Subtract 5 from both sides. 2x = 8.

2Divide both sides by 2. x = 4.

✓Answer. x = 4

Reverse order

In 2x + 5, the variable is first multiplied by 2 and then 5 is added. To undo it, go backward: first remove the + 5 (subtract), and last the × 2 (divide).

3The five-step strategy

For any linear equation, this ordered list is a reliable guide.

  1. Distribute to clear the parentheses, if there are any.
  2. Combine like terms on each side.
  3. Move the variables to one side of the equal sign.
  4. Isolate the variable with the properties of equality.
  5. Check by substituting the solution into the original equation.

4Variables on both sides

When the variable appears on both sides of the equal sign, first gather all the variables on one side (by subtracting one of them from both sides) and all the constants on the other. Then isolate the variable as usual.

Example 3: solve 5x + 2 = 3x + 10

1Subtract 3x from both sides. 2x + 2 = 10.

2Subtract 2 from both sides. 2x = 8.

3Divide by 2. x = 4.

✓Answer. x = 4

Example 4: solve 7x + 1 = 2x + 16

1Subtract 2x from both sides. 5x + 1 = 16.

2Subtract 1 from both sides. 5x = 15.

3Divide by 5. x = 3.

✓Answer. x = 3

A useful trick

Move the variables to the side where the coefficient stays positive. In Example 3, subtracting 3x (the smaller one) leaves 2x, which is positive, so you avoid working with a negative coefficient.

5Checking the solution

You check every solution by substituting it into the original equation. If both sides give the same value, the solution is right.

Example 5: check that x = 4 solves 2x + 1 = 9

1Substitute. 2(4) + 1.

2Work it out. 8 + 1 = 9.

✓Answer. The left side gives 9, the same as the right side: x = 4 is correct.

Checking is not optional

Substituting the solution is the best way to catch a sign mistake made along the way. One minute of checking keeps you from losing all the points on a problem.

6Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. Solve four equations with two or three steps, four equations with variables on both sides, and two equations with parentheses that you must distribute first. Check two given solutions by substitution. Check every solution before you count the exercise as done.
  • In your daily life. A taxi charges $3 to start plus $2 per mile, and a ride share charges $7 to start plus $1.50 per mile. Write an equation to find the number of miles at which both cost the same, solve it, and check your answer. Which would you choose for a 5-mile trip?

📘Key words

equation
Two expressions joined by an equal sign, like 2x + 3 = 11.
solution
The value of the variable that makes the equation true.
inverse operation
The operation that undoes another: subtraction undoes addition, and division undoes multiplication.
isolate
To get the variable alone on one side of the equal sign.
