1

Exponential notation

›

In a power an, the base a is the factor that repeats and the exponent n says how many times it repeats. The exponent counts factors: if you can count the factors, you always get the right exponent, even if you do not remember the rule.

Power explorer

Choose a base and an exponent, and look at the written-out form and the value.

Parentheses decide the base: in (−3)4 the base is −3 and the sign is multiplied four times, so the answer is positive. In −34 the base is only the 3; the minus sign stays outside and the answer is −81. Choose a negative base in the explorer to see it.

2

The product rule

›

When you multiply two powers with the same base, the factors pile up. That is why the exponents are added:

am · an = am + n

Factor counter

Change the two exponents and count the chips: each chip is one factor of the base.

Two common mistakes: the exponents are added, not multiplied (a3 · a2 = a5, not a6). And the rule works only with the same base: x2 · y3 stays as it is.

Practice: write the exponent of the answer

Type a whole number. If it is negative, start with the minus sign: −2 or -2.

Practice: with numbers in front and with two bases

The numbers in front are multiplied; the exponents of each base are added separately.

3

The power of a power

›

When a power is raised to another exponent, the whole power repeats as a factor. Count the chips and you will see that the exponents are multiplied:

(am)n = am · n

Groups of factors

The inside exponent says how many chips each group has; the outside exponent says how many groups there are.

Do not mix up the two rules: in a5 · a4 the powers are multiplied and the exponents are added (a9); in (a5)4 one power is raised to the other and the exponents are multiplied (a20).

Practice: add or multiply?

Both shapes are mixed here. Before you choose, look for parentheses with an exponent.

4

The power of a product

›

When the thing being raised is a product, the exponent is shared out to each factor, the number in front too:

(a · b)n = an · bn

Exponent sharer

Build a product inside the parentheses and watch the outside exponent reach each factor.

The most common mistake: forgetting to raise the number. (2x)3 is not 2x3: the 2 is also cubed, and the answer is 8x3. It is not 6x3 either: cubing is not multiplying by 3.

5

The three rules together

›
The three rules for multiplying with exponents: name, pattern, and example.
RulePatternExample
Product of powersam · an = am+n (add)x4 · x3 = x7
Power of a power(am)n = am·n (multiply)(x4)3 = x12
Power of a product(a · b)n = an · bn (share it out)(2x)3 = 8x3
Strategy: work out the parentheses with an exponent first (power of a power or of a product), then combine with the product rule. Working from the inside out prevents most mistakes.
