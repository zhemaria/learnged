Lesson 3.1 · Simplifying radicals with perfect-square factors

Take the largest perfect square out of the radical and leave inside only what cannot come out.

1The product property of radicals

Topic 2 finished the work with powers of 10 and scientific notation. The square root is the opposite of squaring: if 62 = 36, then √36 = 6. In Level 2 you worked with the roots of perfect squares; now you will learn to simplify radicals whose radicand is not a perfect square, taking out of it the largest square part you can.

The key idea is that the root of a product can be split into the product of the roots:

√(a · b) = √a · √b

This property lets you “take out” of the radical any factor that is a perfect square, and leave inside only what is not. To spot those factors, you need the perfect squares at your fingertips.

2Simplifying number radicals

The procedure: find the largest perfect square that divides the radicand, split it off with the product property, and take its root.

√72 = √(36 · 2) = √36 · √2 = 6√2

Example 1: simplify √50

1Largest square. The largest perfect square that divides 50 is 25: 50 = 25 · 2.

2Split. √50 = √25 · √2.

3Take the root. √25 = 5.

✓Answer. √50 = 5√2

Example 2: simplify √48

1Largest square. 48 = 16 · 3.

2Split and take the root. √48 = √16 · √3 = 4√3.

✓Answer. √48 = 4√3

Look for the largest

If you use a small perfect square, there is still something left to simplify. For example, √72 = √(4 · 18) = 2√18, but √18 can still be reduced. Look for the largest one right away: 36.

3Simplifying radicals with variables

A variable with an even exponent is a perfect square, because its square root is the same variable with half the exponent: √x4 = x2, since (x2)2 = x4. In this lesson x is always positive, as in the problems on the test.

Example 3: simplify √x6

1Even exponent. 6 is even, so x6 is a perfect square.

2Half. 6 ÷ 2 = 3.

✓Answer. √x6 = x3

Example 4: simplify √9x2

1Split. √9 · √x2.

2Take each root. √9 = 3 and √x2 = x.

✓Answer. √9x2 = 3x

When is it simple?

A radical is in its simplest form when the radicand has no factor (other than 1) that is a perfect square. √15 is already simple, because 15 = 3 · 5 and neither 3 nor 5 is a perfect square. With an odd exponent, one x stays inside: √x5 = x2√x.

4Checking with the calculator

Simplifying does not change the value of the radical, only its form. A reliable check is to work out both expressions on the calculator: they must give the same decimal.

Example 5: check that √72 = 6√2

1On the calculator. √72 ≈ 8.485.

2The other side. 6 × √2 ≈ 6 × 1.414 = 8.485.

✓Answer. Both give 8.485, so √72 = 6√2 is right.

🧮 Square roots on the calculator

The square root is the second function of the x2 key: 2nd x2 7 2 enter shows 8.485281374. Then 6 × 2nd x2 2 enter shows the same number.

Same value is not enough

Matching decimals show the value is right, but not that the answer is finished. 2√18 has the same value as √72 and can still be simplified. Checking does not replace the procedure; it confirms it.

5Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. Simplify six number radicals by taking out the largest perfect square, two radicals with variables, and decide for two more whether they are already simple, explaining why. Review the perfect squares up to 400 (202): without them in your head, you cannot see the perfect square hiding in the radicand.
  • In your daily life. A square patio has an area of 72 square feet. Its side is √72 feet. Write it in simplest form and find it as a decimal on the calculator. Then measure a square floor tile at home and find its side from its area.

📘Key words

radical
An expression with the root sign √, like √50.
radicand
The number or expression inside the radical sign. In √50, the radicand is 50.
perfect square
A number that is a whole number times itself: 1, 4, 9, 16, 25…
simplest form
A radical whose radicand has no perfect-square factor other than 1, like 5√2.
