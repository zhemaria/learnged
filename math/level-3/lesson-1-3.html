Math · Level 3 · Topic 1 — Rules of exponents

Lesson 1.3 · Simplifying with several rules

No new rules: the five you know, combined in order, with an answer that has only positive exponents.

⏱️ About 4 hours with practice and homework · take breaks 🧮 Calculator to check

🎯What you will learn

When you finish this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Simplify expressions that need two or more rules of exponents in a row.
  • Follow an order: parentheses with an exponent first, then products and quotients.
  • Give the final answer with positive exponents only.
  • Find and fix the most common mistakes in a simplification.

1The five rules in view

In Lesson 1.1 you learned the rules for multiplying (product, power of a power, and power of a product), and in Lesson 1.2 the rules for dividing, the zero exponent, and negative exponents. Those five rules are complete. This lesson adds no new rules: it teaches you to combine them in order in one expression and to give the answer in its cleanest form, with positive exponents.

The five rules of exponents: name, pattern, and example
RulePatternExample
Product of powersam · an = am+nx4 · x3 = x7
Power of a power(am)n = am·n(x4)3 = x12
Power of a product(a · b)n = an · bn(2x)3 = 8x3
Quotient of powersam ÷ an = am−nx7 ÷ x2 = x5
Zero and negative exponentsa0 = 1 · a−n = 1/an50 = 1 · 3−2 = 1/9

2An order for simplifying

When an expression combines several operations, follow the same route every time. This order prevents mistakes and gives consistent answers.

  1. Parentheses with an exponent (power of a power and power of a product). Remember to raise the numbers too.
  2. Product and quotient of the same base: add or subtract exponents.
  3. Negative exponents: rewrite them as fractions with positive exponents.
  4. Check that the answer is as simple as it can be.

Example 1: simplify (x2)3 · x4

1Parentheses. Power of a power: (x2)3 = x6.

2Product. x6 · x4 = x6 + 4.

✓Answer. x10

Example 2: simplify (2a3)2 · a

1Parentheses. Share out the exponent and raise the number: (2a3)2 = 22 · a6 = 4a6.

2Product. 4a6 · a = 4a7 (the a on its own is a1).

✓Answer. 4a7

3Fractions and negative exponents

When the expression is a fraction, use the quotient rule on each base separately. If an exponent ends up negative, rewrite it as a fraction with a positive exponent in the last step.

Example 3: simplify x6y2x2y(x6y2) ÷ (x2y)

1Each base. Subtract the exponents of each base separately: x6 − 2 and y2 − 1.

2Work it out. x4 · y1 = x4y.

✓Answer. x4y

Example 4: simplify a4 ÷ a6

1Quotient. a4 − 6 = a−2.

2Positive exponent. The exponent is negative: write the reciprocal. a−2 = 1/a2.

✓Answer. 1/a2

The final form

An answer is not simplified while it still has negative exponents. x3y−2 has the same value as x3/y2, but only the second is the answer the test expects. The last step is always to turn a−n into 1/an.

4The exponent referee: finding mistakes

Knowing how to simplify includes noticing when someone else's answer is wrong. The mistakes almost always follow the same patterns: mixing up when to add and when to multiply, or forgetting to raise the number in front.

Example 5: someone wrote a5 · a3 = a15

1Which rule? The powers are multiplied and have the same base: the product rule.

2The mistake. The exponents should be added, not multiplied: 5 + 3 = 8.

✓Correction. Wrong: a15 → Right: a8

Example 6: someone wrote (3x)2 = 3x2

1Which rule? A power of a product: the exponent goes to each factor.

2The mistake. The 3 must be squared too: 32 = 9.

✓Correction. Wrong: 3x2 → Right: 9x2

5A complete expression, step by step

This example brings it all together: parentheses with an exponent, a number in front, a product, a quotient, and an answer with positive exponents.

Example 7: simplify (2x3)2 · x ÷ x9

1Parentheses. (2x3)2 = 4x6.

2Product. 4x6 · x = 4x7.

3Quotient. 4x7 ÷ x9 = 4x7 − 9 = 4x−2.

4Positive exponent. 4x−2 = 4/x2. The 4 stays on top: the negative exponent belongs only to the x.

✓Answer. 4/x2

A quick check

Replace x with 2 in the original and in the answer. Original: (2 · 8)2 · 2 ÷ 512 = 256 · 2 ÷ 512 = 1. Answer: 4 ÷ 22 = 1. They match, so the simplification is reliable.

🧮 Check with x = 2 on the calculator

Type ( 2 × 2 ^ 3 ▸ ) x2 × 2 ÷ 2 ^ 9 enter: the screen shows 1. Then 4 ÷ 2 x2 enter also shows 1.

6Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. Simplify six expressions that combine three or more rules, following the order of the lesson and writing the rule at each step; and four expressions with fractions and negative exponents, with the answer in positive exponents. Then be the referee four times: write a simplification with one mistake, swap it with a classmate or check it the next day, and say in which step it went wrong. Last, solve one long expression twice, on different days, without looking at the first try, and compare.
  • In your daily life. Explain the route (parentheses, then product and quotient, then positive exponents) to someone at home using one of your homework expressions. Teaching a method is one of the best ways to make it stick.

📘Key words

simplify
Write an expression in its shortest equal form, with each base once and positive exponents.
final form
An answer with no parentheses left, each base written once, and no negative exponents.
route
The fixed order of steps: parentheses, then product and quotient, then positive exponents.
test value
A number, like 2 or 3, put in place of the letter to check that two expressions are equal.
