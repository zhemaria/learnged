1Multiplying in three steps

In Lesson 2.1 you converted between decimals and scientific notation and compared amounts by their order of magnitude. Now you will calculate with those amounts. The key is that a number in scientific notation has two separate parts, the coefficient and the power of 10, and each one is worked on its own: the coefficients with ordinary arithmetic, and the powers of 10 with the rules of exponents from Topic 1.

To multiply, always follow the same three steps:

  1. Multiply the coefficients.
  2. Add the exponents of the powers of 10 (the product rule).
  3. Check that the coefficient is from 1 to 10, and adjust it if needed.

Example 1: find (3 × 104)(2 × 105)

1Coefficients. 3 × 2 = 6.

2Powers of 10. Add the exponents: 4 + 5 = 9.

3Check. 6 is from 1 to 10: no adjustment needed.

✓Answer. 6 × 109

2When the coefficient needs adjusting

Sometimes the product of the coefficients is 10 or more. Then the answer is not yet valid scientific notation, and you have to rewrite the coefficient, which changes the exponent.

Example 2: find (5 × 103)(4 × 102)

1Coefficients. 5 × 4 = 20.

2Powers of 10. 3 + 2 = 5. Partial answer: 20 × 105.

3Adjust. 20 is not from 1 to 10: rewrite 20 = 2.0 × 101, and add that exponent to the 5.

✓Answer. 20 × 105 = 2 × 106

What one loses, the other gains

If the coefficient becomes 10 times smaller, the power of 10 becomes 10 times larger. That is why the value of the number does not change when you adjust.

3Dividing in scientific notation

Division follows the same logic with the opposite operations: the coefficients are divided and the exponents of 10 are subtracted (the quotient rule).

  1. Divide the coefficients.
  2. Subtract the exponents: the top one minus the bottom one.
  3. Check the coefficient and adjust it. In division it often ends up less than 1.

Example 3: find (8 × 107) ÷ (2 × 103)

1Coefficients. 8 ÷ 2 = 4.

2Powers of 10. Subtract the exponents: 7 − 3 = 4.

3Check. 4 is from 1 to 10: no adjustment needed.

✓Answer. 4 × 104

Example 4: find (3 × 104) ÷ (6 × 107)

1Coefficients. 3 ÷ 6 = 0.5.

2Powers of 10. 4 − 7 = −3. Partial answer: 0.5 × 10−3.

3Adjust. 0.5 is less than 1: rewrite 0.5 = 5 × 10−1, and add that exponent to the −3.

✓Answer. 0.5 × 10−3 = 5 × 10−4

A common mistake

Leaving the answer as 0.5 × 10−3 or 20 × 105. Even if the arithmetic is right, it is not valid scientific notation until the coefficient is from 1 to 10.

4A real calculation and the calculator

In a word problem, the first thing to decide is the operation. A useful hint:

  • Multiply when an amount repeats many times: speed times time, amount per day times number of days.
  • Divide when a total is shared out, or the question asks “how many fit” or “how much for each one.”

Example 5: light travels 3 × 105 km per second. How far does it go in 2 × 102 seconds?

1Operation. Distance = speed × time: (3 × 105)(2 × 102).

2Three steps. 3 × 2 = 6; 5 + 2 = 7; 6 is already valid.

✓Answer. 6 × 107 km

🧮 Check with the calculator

Type each number with the ×10n key: 3 ×10n 5 × 2 ×10n 2 enter. The screen shows 60000000, that is, 6 × 107. The key puts in the power of 10 without typing “× 10,” which prevents mistakes with very large or very small numbers. Work it out by hand first, then use the calculator to check.

📘Key words

adjust
Rewrite a coefficient that is 10 or more, or less than 1, so it is from 1 to 10, changing the exponent to keep the same value.
product rule
10m × 10n = 10m+n: the exponents are added.
quotient rule
10m ÷ 10n = 10m−n: the exponents are subtracted.
×10n key
The calculator key that types the power of 10 of a number in scientific notation. Some books call it EE.
