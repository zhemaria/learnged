1

The product property of radicals

›

The root of a product can be split into the product of the roots. That lets you “take out” of the radical any factor that is a perfect square and leave inside only what is not.

√(a · b) = √a · √b

Split the radical

Type a number. The table marks the perfect squares that divide it, and the darkest one is the largest. Try splitting the radical with each one and compare what is left inside.

Split with the perfect square…

Look for the largest: if you use a small perfect square, something is still left to simplify. √72 = √(4 · 18) = 2√18, but √18 can still be reduced. With the largest, 36, you get there at once: √72 = 6√2.

Practice: what is the largest perfect square that divides it?

Simplifying number radicals

›

The procedure has three steps: find the largest perfect square that divides the radicand, split it off with the product property, and take its root.

Step-by-step simplifier

Choose a radical and reveal one step at a time. Try to predict each step before you show it.

When is it simple? When the radicand has no factor, other than 1, that is a perfect square. √15 is already simple, because 15 = 3 · 5 and neither 3 nor 5 is a perfect square.

Practice: choose the simplified form

Write it yourself: simplify the radical

√

For example, for 6√2 type 6 and 2. If there is no coefficient left (as in √15), leave the first box blank. If no radical is left (as in √36 = 6), type 1 as the radicand.

Radicals with variables

›

A variable with an even exponent is a perfect square: its root is the same variable with half the exponent, because (x3)2 = x6. If the exponent is odd, split off one x: the even part comes out and the extra x stays inside.

In this whole module, x is positive, as in the problems on the test.

Checking the answer

›

Simplifying does not change the value of the radical, only its form. So a reliable check is to work out both expressions on the calculator: they must give the same decimal. For example, √72 ≈ 8.485 and 6 × √2 ≈ 8.485.

But careful: matching decimals are not enough for the answer to be finished. 2√18 has the same value as √72, and it can still be simplified.

Practice: is this simplification right?

Using the calculator: checking does not replace the procedure, but it confirms that the simplification keeps the value. It is a great tool for catching a factoring mistake.
