Math · Level 3 · Topic 5 — Inequalities and formulas

Lesson 5.1 · Solving linear inequalities and graphing them on the number line

Solve inequalities with the same steps as equations, flip the sign when you multiply or divide by a negative, and graph the solution.

⏱️ About 4 hours with practice and homework · take breaks ✏️ No calculator needed

🎯What you will learn

When you finish this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Solve linear inequalities in one variable with the same steps as an equation.
  • Apply the critical rule: flip the inequality sign when you multiply or divide by a negative number.
  • Graph the solution on the number line (open or closed point; direction of the arrow).
  • Recognize when the point is open (<, >) and when it is closed (≤, ≥).
  • Check the solution with a test value.

1What is an inequality?

In Topic 4 you solved linear equations, where the solution is a single value. An inequality uses the signs <, >, ≤, or ≥ instead of the equal sign, and its solution is not one number but a range of values. The good news: you solve it with the same steps as an equation. The only difference, and it is crucial, appears when you multiply or divide by a negative number.

A solution is a range

x > 3 does not mean one value, but every number greater than 3 (3.1, 4, 100, …). That is why the solution is shown on the number line, not as a single point.

2Solving with the same steps as an equation

As long as you do not multiply or divide by a negative number, you solve an inequality exactly like an equation: add, subtract, multiply, or divide both sides.

Example 1: solve x + 5 < 12

1Subtract 5 from both sides. x < 7.

✓Answer. x < 7 (every value less than 7).

Example 2: solve 3x ≤ 15

1Divide by 3. 3 is positive, so the sign does not flip: x ≤ 5.

✓Answer. x ≤ 5

3The critical rule: flipping the sign

When you multiply or divide both sides by a negative number, the inequality sign flips. This does not happen with equations; it is the only real difference.

Why it flips

You know that 6 > 4. If you multiply both sides by −1, you get −6 and −4. But −6 > −4 is false (−6 is farther to the left). For the statement to stay true, the sign has to flip: −6 < −4.

Example 3: solve −2x < 8

1Divide both sides by −2. You divide by a negative number…

2Flip the sign. x > −4.

✓Answer. x > −4

Only with negatives

The sign flips only when you multiply or divide by a negative number. Adding or subtracting a negative number does not flip it. The most common mistake is to flip it when you should not.

5Checking with a test value

To check a solution, choose three values: one clearly inside the range, the boundary number, and one outside. When you substitute them, only the values in the range should make the inequality true.

Example 4: check the solution x > −4 of −2x < 8

1A value inside, x = 0. −2(0) = 0, and 0 < 8 is true. Correct.

2A value outside, x = −5. −2(−5) = 10, and 10 < 8 is false. It is right that it is left out.

✓Answer. The solution x > −4 is confirmed.

6Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. Solve four inequalities, at least two of which require flipping the sign. Graph the solution of three inequalities on the number line, and decide for three given values whether they satisfy the inequality.
  • In your daily life. Look for limits in your day written as inequalities: a speed limit of 55 mph (s ≤ 55), a minimum age of 18 to vote (a ≥ 18), an elevator for fewer than 13 people (p < 13). Write three of them, graph each one, and say whether the boundary is included.

📘Key words

inequality
A statement that compares two expressions with <, >, ≤, or ≥.
boundary number
The number where the solution starts, like 7 in x < 7.
open point
A hollow circle on the number line: the boundary is not included (< or >).
closed point
A filled circle on the number line: the boundary is included (≤ or ≥).
