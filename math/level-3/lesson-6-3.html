Lesson 6.3 · Similar figures and scale drawings (Thales' theorem)

1Similar figures

Lesson 6.2 worked with a special kind of triangle. This lesson studies figures that have the same shape but a different size: similar figures. To work with them you use proportions, a Level 2 tool that here is applied to geometry, and the algebra of Topics 4 and 5 to solve for the unknown side.

Two figures are similar when they have the same shape: their corresponding angles are equal and their corresponding sides are proportional. The number that relates the sides is called the scale factor.

The scale factor

To find it, divide a side of the large figure by the matching side of the small one. In the diagram, 8 ÷ 4 = 2: the large triangle is twice the small one. Every pair of sides has the same ratio (6 ÷ 3 = 2, 10 ÷ 5 = 2).

Congruent or similar?

Congruent figures have the same shape and the same size (scale factor 1). In Explore: congruent and similar triangles, drag the corners of a triangle and watch the angles stay the same while the sides grow by the scale factor.

2Solving proportions to find a side

If two figures are similar, their corresponding sides form a proportion. To find an unknown side, write the proportion and solve it with cross multiplication.

Example 1: two triangles are similar. The sides of the small one are 3, 4, and 5. In the large one, the side that matches 3 measures 6. Find the side x that matches 5.

1Write the proportion. 36 = 5x.

2Cross multiply. 3 · x = 6 · 5, that is, 3x = 30.

3Solve. x = 10.

✓Answer. The unknown side measures 10.

Match the sides correctly

In the proportion, each side of the small figure goes with its matching side of the large one, always in the same order: small over large in both fractions, or large over small in both. Mixing the order gives a wrong answer.

3Scale drawings

A map or a floor plan is a scale drawing: a similar, smaller version of something real. The scale tells how much of the real thing each unit of the drawing stands for.

Example 2: a map of Illinois. On a map, 1 inch stands for 5 miles. Two places are 4 inches apart on the map. What is the real distance?

1Each inch is worth 5 miles. Multiply: 4 × 5 = 20.

✓Answer. The real distance is 20 miles.

Example 3: a floor plan at a scale of 1:100. A real wall measures 300 inches. How long is it on the plan?

1A scale of 1:100 means the plan is 100 times smaller. Divide by 100.

2Divide. 300 ÷ 100 = 3.

✓Answer. On the plan, the wall measures 3 inches.

Multiply or divide?

From the drawing to the real thing, the measure grows: multiply. From the real thing to the drawing, the measure shrinks: divide. A scale like 1:100 uses the same unit on both sides; a scale like 1 inch = 5 miles uses two different units.

4Thales' theorem: indirect measurement

Thales' theorem lets you find heights or distances that cannot be measured directly, using the proportion between an object and its shadow (or between a known reference and the unknown).

Example 4: the height of a tree. A 4-foot post casts a 6-foot shadow. At the same time, a tree casts a 24-foot shadow. How tall is the tree?

1Write the proportion of height to shadow. 46 = x24.

2Cross multiply. 6 · x = 4 · 24, that is, 6x = 96.

3Solve. x = 16.

✓Answer. The tree is 16 feet tall.

Same time, same proportion

The shadow method works because, at the same time of day, the sun's rays hit everything at the same angle. So the object and its shadow form a triangle similar to the one formed by the reference and its shadow. Thales' theorem says it in general: parallel lines cut two transversals into proportional segments.

5Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. Solve three exercises that find a side with a proportion, three on map and plan scales, and two that measure a height with shadows. Then try Explore: congruent and similar triangles and the practice page.
  • In your daily life. On a sunny day, measure your own shadow and the shadow of a tree, a pole, or a building at the same time. Use your height and the proportion of height to shadow to find how tall the object is. Then find the scale on a map of your city and work out the real distance between your home and a place you go often.

📘Key words

similar figures
Figures with the same shape but possibly a different size: equal angles and proportional sides.
congruent figures
Figures with the same shape and the same size.
scale factor
The number you multiply a side by to get the matching side of the similar figure.
proportion
An equation that says two ratios are equal, like 3/6 = 5/10.
scale drawing
A drawing similar to a real object, like a map or a floor plan.
