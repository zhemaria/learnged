1Inequalities that need simplifying

In Lesson 5.1 you solved simple inequalities and graphed their solutions. This lesson adds two layers. First, inequalities that, like the equations of Topic 4, need simplifying before you solve: distribute, combine like terms, and move variables. Second, word problems: translate a real situation into an inequality and explain what the solution means.

When an inequality has parentheses or repeated terms, first simplify it with the usual algebra tools, then solve it. The sign rule still holds if you divide by a negative number.

Example 1: solve 2(x + 3) > 10

1Distribute. 2x + 6 > 10.

2Subtract 6. 2x > 4.

3Divide by 2. 2 is positive, so the sign does not flip: x > 2.

✓Answer. x > 2

Example 2: solve 5x − 3 > 2x + 9

1Subtract 2x from both sides. 3x − 3 > 9.

2Add 3. 3x > 12.

3Divide by 3. x > 4.

✓Answer. x > 4

Do not forget the sign rule

If after simplifying you must divide by a negative number (for example, in −2(x − 1) ≥ 8), the inequality sign flips, just as in Lesson 5.1.

2The language of inequalities

“At least” and “at most” include the boundary

“At least 10” means 10 or more (≥ 10), and it includes 10. “At most 10” means 10 or less (≤ 10), and it includes 10. That is why they use the signs with the little bar (≤, ≥) and a closed point on the graph.

3Translating and solving sentences

To solve a word problem: identify the variable, translate the sentence into an inequality with the key words, and solve it.

Example 3: “A number increased by 5 is at least 12.”

1Translate. “Increased by 5” is x + 5; “at least 12” is ≥ 12. You get x + 5 ≥ 12.

2Subtract 5. x ≥ 7.

✓Answer. The number must be 7 or greater.

Example 4: “Twice a number does not exceed 14.”

1Translate. “Twice” is 2x; “does not exceed 14” is ≤ 14. You get 2x ≤ 14.

2Divide by 2. x ≤ 7.

✓Answer. The number must be 7 or less.

4Problems in context: budget and time

Inequalities model real-life limits: a budget you cannot go over, a minimum number of hours you must work, a maximum number of items you can buy.

Example 5: a budget

1The situation. You have at most $50. You already spent $30. How much more (x) can you spend? The total cannot exceed 50: 30 + x ≤ 50.

2Subtract 30. x ≤ 20.

✓Answer. You can spend at most $20 more.

Example 6: time

1The situation. A project needs at least 24 hours. You already worked 9. How many more hours (x) do you need? The total must be at least 24: 9 + x ≥ 24.

2Subtract 9. x ≥ 15.

✓Answer. You need at least 15 more hours.

5Explaining the solution

The last part of a word problem is to explain the solution in words, with units. The inequality gives a range; the situation tells you which value of that range is the “maximum” or the “minimum” you are looking for.

Example 7: from the range to the limit

1The range. The solution of a shopping problem is x ≤ 6, where x is the number of items. The range includes 0, 1, 2, … up to 6.

2Whole items. You do not buy parts of an item, so the greatest whole number possible is 6.

✓Answer. You can buy at most 6 items.

Answer in words

A word problem does not end with “x ≤ 6.” It ends with a sentence like “you can buy at most 6 items.” Explaining the result in the situation is part of the answer. If the solution is x ≤ 6.4 items, the most is 6 (round down); if it is x ≥ 3.2 days, the least is 4 (round up), because 3 days are not enough.

6Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. Solve four inequalities that require simplifying (distributing or combining) first. Translate four sentences into inequalities and solve them. Solve two problems in context and explain the solution in words, with units.
  • In your daily life. Plan a real purchase: decide on a maximum budget, write down a fixed cost (for example, delivery) and the price of each item, and write an inequality for the number of items you can buy. Solve it and answer in a sentence.
