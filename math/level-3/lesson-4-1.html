1Variables, terms, and coefficients

Algebra is not completely new: in Level 1 you already used simple unknowns to solve one-step problems. What changes here is the depth. After completing number sense in Topics 1 to 3 of this level (exponents, scientific notation, and the real numbers), Level 3 picks up that language again and makes it formal: you will translate fully between words and symbols, and you will learn to simplify expressions by distributing and combining like terms. That base will let you solve equations in the next lessons.

A variable is a letter that stands for a number that is unknown or that can change. An algebraic expression combines variables, numbers, and operations. Its parts have precise names:

Parts of the expression 3x plus 5: term, coefficient, variable, and constant
PartIn 3x + 5Meaning
Term3x and 5Each part separated by + or −.
Coefficient3The number that multiplies the variable.
VariablexThe letter that stands for the unknown value.
Constant5The term that has no variable.

The invisible coefficient

When a variable appears alone, like x, its coefficient is 1 (x = 1x). In the same way, −x means −1x. Remembering this prevents mistakes when you combine terms.

2Translating between words and algebra

Much of algebra is translating a sentence in words into the language of symbols. Certain words always point to the same operation.

Order matters in subtraction

“A number decreased by 4” is x − 4, but “4 decreased by a number” is 4 − x. And “4 less than a number” is x − 4: you take 4 away from the number. Subtraction is not commutative, so read carefully to see which comes first.

3Combining like terms

Two terms are like terms if they have the same variable raised to the same exponent. Only like terms can be combined, by adding or subtracting their coefficients.

Example 1: simplify 3x + 5x

1Like terms? Both terms have the variable x: they are like terms.

2Add the coefficients. 3 + 5 = 8.

✓Answer. 3x + 5x = 8x

Example 2: simplify 7x − 2x + 4

1The x terms. 7x − 2x = 5x.

2The constant. 4 has no x: it stays apart.

✓Answer. 7x − 2x + 4 = 5x + 4

Different terms do not combine

4x + 2y cannot be simplified, because x and y are different variables. And 3x + 5 does not become 8x, because 5 has no x. Also, 3x2 + 2x is not 5x2: the exponents are different.

4The distributive property

The distributive property lets you multiply a number by a sum by sharing it out to each term in the parentheses.

a(b + c) = a · b + a · c

Example 3: simplify 3(x + 4)

1Share out the 3. 3 · x + 3 · 4.

✓Answer. 3(x + 4) = 3x + 12

Example 4: simplify 2(x + 3) + 4x

1Distribute. 2x + 6 + 4x.

2Combine like terms. 2x + 4x = 6x.

✓Answer. 2(x + 3) + 4x = 6x + 6

Signs are shared out too

−2(x − 5) = −2x + 10, because (−2)(−5) = +10. The most common mistake is to multiply only the first term: 3(x + 4) is not 3x + 4.

5Evaluating expressions

To evaluate an expression means to replace the variable with a given value and work out the result, following the order of operations (PEMDAS).

Example 5: evaluate 2x + 5 when x = 3

1Substitute. 2(3) + 5.

2Multiply first. 6 + 5.

✓Answer. 2x + 5 = 11 when x = 3.

Example 6: evaluate x2 + 1 when x = 4

1Substitute. (4)2 + 1.

2The power first. 16 + 1.

✓Answer. x2 + 1 = 17 when x = 4.

Use parentheses when you substitute

Writing 2(3) instead of 23 keeps you from confusing the multiplication with a two-digit number. With negative numbers it is essential: if x = −3, then x2 = (−3)2 = 9, but −x2 = −(−3)2 = −9.

6Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. Translate four phrases in words into algebraic expressions, simplify four expressions by combining like terms and using the distributive property, and evaluate four expressions with the given values (whole numbers or fractions).
  • In your daily life. A plumber charges $45 for the visit plus $60 for each hour of work. Write an expression for the cost of h hours. Then evaluate it for 2 hours and for 3.5 hours. Look for another service you use (a phone plan, a taxi, a gym) that charges a fixed amount plus an amount per unit, and write its expression.

📘Key words

variable
A letter that stands for a number that is unknown or that can change, like x.
coefficient
The number that multiplies the variable. In 3x, it is 3.
like terms
Terms with the same variable raised to the same exponent, like 3x and 7x.
evaluate
To replace the variable with a value and work out the result.
