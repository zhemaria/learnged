Math · Level 3 · Topic 1 — Rules of exponents

Lesson 1.1 · The product rule, power of a power, and power of a product

Three rules for multiplying with exponents. Each one comes from counting factors.

⏱️ About 4 hours with practice and homework · take breaks 🧮 Calculator to check

🎯What you will learn

When you finish this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Use the product rule: am · an = am+n.
  • Use the power of a power rule: (am)n = am·n.
  • Use the power of a product rule: (a · b)n = an · bn.
  • Check each answer by writing the powers out as repeated multiplication.
  • Choose the right rule from the shape of the expression.

1Review: exponential notation

In Level 2 you met the exponent as a short way to write repeated multiplication: 24 means 2 · 2 · 2 · 2. That idea is the starting point of Level 3. In this topic, instead of writing out every power by hand, you will learn rules that let you work with exponents directly. This first lesson sets out the three rules for multiplying; the next one adds division, the zero exponent, and negative exponents.

A power has two parts. In an, the number a is the base (the factor that repeats) and n is the exponent (how many times the factor repeats).

an = a · a · a · … · a (n factors)

So a5 = a · a · a · a · a. Every rule in this lesson comes from this definition and from counting factors.

The key idea

The exponent counts factors. If you count correctly how many times the base appears as a factor, you always get the right exponent, even if you do not remember the rule.

Parentheses decide the base

In (−3)4 the base is −3, so the sign is multiplied four times and the answer is positive: 81. In −34 the base is only the 3; the minus sign stays outside, and the answer is −81.

2The product rule

When you multiply two powers with the same base, the factors simply pile up. Write them out to see the rule:

a3 · a2 = (a · a · a)(a · a) = a5

Three factors plus two factors make five factors. The exponent of the answer is the sum of the exponents:

am · an = am + n

Example 1: simplify x4 · x6

1Same base. Both powers have base x, so the product rule applies.

2Add. 4 + 6 = 10.

✓Answer. x4 · x6 = x10

Example 2: simplify 23 · 25

1Same base. The base is 2: add the exponents, 3 + 5 = 8.

2Value. 28 = 256. The base stays 2; it is not 48.

✓Answer. 23 · 25 = 28 = 256

Two common mistakes

The exponents are added, not multiplied: a3 · a2 = a5, not a6. And the rule works only with the same base: x2 · y3 cannot be combined.

A letter with no exponent has exponent 1: a = a1. So a7 · a = a8.

3The power of a power

When a power is raised to another exponent, the whole power repeats as a factor. Writing it out shows why the exponents are multiplied:

(a2)3 = a2 · a2 · a2 = a6

a2 repeats three times. By the product rule, 2 + 2 + 2 = 6, which is the same as 2 × 3. The exponent of the answer is the product of the exponents:

(am)n = am · n

Example 3: simplify (x5)4

1Look at the shape. A power is raised to another power.

2Multiply. 5 × 4 = 20.

✓Answer. (x5)4 = x20

Do not mix up the two rules

In a5 · a4 the powers are multiplied, so the exponents are added: a9. In (a5)4 one power is raised to the other, so the exponents are multiplied: a20. Look for parentheses with an exponent outside.

4The power of a product

When the thing being raised is a product of several factors, the exponent is shared out to each factor:

(ab)3 = (ab)(ab)(ab) = a3b3

(a · b)n = an · bn

Example 4: simplify (2x)3

1Share out. The exponent 3 goes to each factor: to the 2 and to the x.

2Raise. 23 = 8 · x3 = x3.

✓Answer. (2x)3 = 8x3

Example 5: simplify (3y2)2

1Share out. (3)2 · (y2)2.

2Two rules. 32 = 9, and for (y2)2 multiply the exponents: y4.

✓Answer. (3y2)2 = 9y4

The most common mistake

Forgetting to raise the number. (2x)3 is not 2x3: the 2 is also cubed, so the answer is 8x3. And it is not 6x3 either: cubing is not multiplying by 3.

5The three rules together

These three rules cover all multiplication with exponents. The table sums them up.

The three rules for multiplying with exponents: name, pattern, and example
RulePatternExample
Product of powersam · an = am+n (add)x4 · x3 = x7
Power of a power(am)n = am·n (multiply)(x4)3 = x12
Power of a product(a · b)n = an · bn (share it out)(2x)3 = 8x3

Example 6: simplify (x2)3 · x4

1Parentheses first. Power of a power: (x2)3 = x6.

2Then the product. x6 · x4 = x6 + 4.

✓Answer. (x2)3 · x4 = x10

A strategy

When there are parentheses with an exponent, work them out first (power of a power or power of a product). Then combine with the product rule. Working from the inside out prevents most mistakes.

6Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. Write and simplify eight products of powers with the same base (four with numbers, four with letters), writing the sum of the exponents before the answer. Then six powers of a power, two of them with a number in front; six powers of a product; and four expressions that need all three rules, writing the rule you used at each step.
  • In your daily life. Area and volume use exponents: a square room 12 feet on each side has an area of 122 square feet. Find two square or cube-shaped things at home, measure them, and write their area or volume as a power. Then check the value on the calculator.

📘Key words

base
The factor that repeats in a power. In a5, the base is a.
exponent
The small number that says how many times the base repeats as a factor.
power
A base with an exponent, like 23 or x5.
coefficient
The number that multiplies the letters in a term. In 8x3, the coefficient is 8.
factor
Each of the numbers or letters that are multiplied.
