1The right triangle and the theorem

In Lesson 6.1 you worked with the angles of triangles, including the right triangle (the one with a 90° angle). This lesson studies the relationship between its sides: the Pythagorean theorem. To solve for the unknown side you will use exactly the algebra of Topics 4 and 5, and you will take a square root as in Topic 3.

In a right triangle, the two sides that form the 90° angle are called the legs (a and b). The side across from the right angle, which is the longest one, is called the hypotenuse (c). The Pythagorean theorem relates the three:

a² + b² = c²

The hypotenuse is always c

It is the longest side, and it is across from the right angle. Finding it correctly is the first step, because in the formula a² + b² = c², c always takes the place of the hypotenuse.

2Finding the hypotenuse

When you know the two legs, substitute them into the formula and take the square root to find the hypotenuse.

Example 1: the 3-4-5 triangle. The legs measure 3 and 4. What is the length of the hypotenuse?

1Substitute. 3² + 4² = c², that is, 9 + 16 = c².

2Add. 25 = c².

3Take the square root. c = √25 = 5.

✓Answer. The hypotenuse measures 5.

Example 2: the legs measure 6 and 8. What is the length of the hypotenuse?

1Substitute. 6² + 8² = 36 + 64 = 100 = c².

2Take the square root. c = √100 = 10.

✓Answer. The hypotenuse measures 10.

🧮 With the calculator

When the sum of the squares is not a perfect square, use the calculator: for legs 5 and 7, c² = 25 + 49 = 74, and √74 ≈ 8.6. Round to the tenth unless the problem says otherwise.

3Finding a leg

If you know the hypotenuse and one leg, solve for the other leg: subtract the square of the known leg and take the square root.

Example 3: the hypotenuse measures 13 and one leg measures 5. What is the length of the other leg?

1Write the equation. 5² + b² = 13², that is, 25 + b² = 169.

2Solve for b². b² = 169 − 25 = 144.

3Take the square root. b = √144 = 12.

✓Answer. The other leg measures 12.

For a leg, subtract

When you look for a leg, the square of the hypotenuse is alone on one side, and you subtract the square of the known leg from it. A common mistake is to add instead of subtract. Check your answer: a leg is always shorter than the hypotenuse.

Multiples work too

If 3-4-5 is a triple, so are 6-8-10 (everything times 2) and 9-12-15 (everything times 3). Recognizing a multiple of 3-4-5 lets you find the answer almost right away.

5The converse of the theorem

The converse goes the other way: if the three sides of a triangle make a² + b² = c² true, then the triangle is a right triangle. It is a way to check whether a corner is a right angle.

Example 4: checking with the converse. Is a triangle with sides 30, 40, and 50 a right triangle?

1The longest side (50) would be the hypotenuse. Check: 30² + 40² = 900 + 1,600 = 2,500.

2Compare. 50² = 2,500. They match, so a² + b² = c² is true.

✓Answer. Yes, the triangle is a right triangle.

On the job

Carpenters mark 3 feet on one wall and 4 feet on the other. If the diagonal between the marks measures 5 feet, the corner is square. That is the converse of the theorem.

6Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. Solve four exercises that find the hypotenuse or a leg, four word problems about building or distance, and two exercises that use the converse to check whether a triangle is a right triangle. Then practice in Explore: the Pythagorean theorem in real life: once the square root comes easily, all that is left is to decide which side is missing.
  • In your daily life. Check a corner at home with the 3-4-5 rule: measure 3 feet along one wall and 4 feet along the other from the corner, and mark both points. Measure the distance between the marks. If it is 5 feet, the corner is square. Then measure the width and height of a TV or computer screen and use the theorem to find its diagonal; compare it with the size on the box.

📘Key words

right triangle
A triangle with a 90° angle.
legs
The two sides that form the right angle.
hypotenuse
The side across from the right angle. It is always the longest side.
Pythagorean triple
Three whole numbers that make a² + b² = c² true, like 3, 4, 5.
converse
The theorem turned around: if a² + b² = c², the triangle is a right triangle.
