Similar figures

›

Two figures are similar when they have the same shape: their corresponding angles are equal and their corresponding sides are proportional. The number that relates them is the scale factor: divide a side of the new figure by the matching side of the original.

Make it bigger or smaller

If the factor is greater than 1, the figure grows; if it is between 0 and 1, it shrinks. The angles never change: that is why the shape stays the same.

Finding a side with a proportion

›

The corresponding sides of two similar figures form a proportion. To find an unknown side, write the proportion and solve it with cross multiplication.

Step by step

Match the sides correctly: in the proportion, each side of one figure goes with its matching side of the other, always in the same order (small over large in both fractions, or large over small in both).

Practice: find the unknown side

Type the number; it can be a decimal, like 7.5.

Scale drawings

›

A map or a floor plan is a drawing similar to the real thing, but smaller. The scale tells how much of the real thing each unit of the drawing is worth. It is written in two ways:

The two ways to write a scale

Thales' theorem: indirect measurement

›

At the same time of day, the sun's rays reach every object at the same angle. So an object and its shadow form a triangle similar to the one formed by another object and its shadow. With a known reference (a post, a person), you can find the height of something you cannot measure directly.

height of the postshadow of the post = height of the treeshadow of the tree

The tree's shadow

Same time, same proportion: if the shadows were measured at different times, the angle of the sun would change and the triangles would no longer be similar.
