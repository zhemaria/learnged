Lesson 5.3 · Solving literal formulas, and a general strategy

1What does it mean to solve a formula?

Throughout Topic 4 and the first part of Topic 5 you solved equations and inequalities to find the value of x. Solving a formula is exactly the same, with one difference: the variable you want is surrounded by other letters instead of numbers. As the key idea says, “solving a formula is solving an equation where the variable is a symbol, not a number.”

A literal formula relates several amounts with letters, like d = rt (distance = rate × time). Solving for one of them means rewriting the formula so that this variable is alone on one side. You treat the other letters as if they were known numbers.

Example 1: in d = rt, solve for t

1t is multiplied by r. To isolate it, divide both sides by r.

2Divide. dr = t.

✓Answer. t = dr

The other letters are “numbers”

When you solve d = rt for t, you treat r just like a number. Dividing by r is like dividing by 5 or any other number: an inverse operation to isolate the variable you want.

2The four-step strategy

To solve for any variable, you always follow the same sequence.

  1. Identify the variable you want to solve for.
  2. Isolate it with the properties of equality (inverse operations), treating the other letters as numbers.
  3. Simplify the result, with a fraction if needed.
  4. Check that the variable you want is alone on one side.

4Two-step formulas

Some formulas need more than one operation to solve, just like multi-step equations. Undo the operations in reverse order.

Example 4: temperature. In F = (9/5)C + 32, solve for C

1Subtract 32 from both sides. F − 32 = 95C.

2Multiply both sides by 5/9. That undoes the 95: 59(F − 32) = C.

✓Answer. C = 59(F − 32)

Example 5: a line. In y = mx + b, solve for b

1Subtract mx from both sides. y − mx = b.

✓Answer. b = y − mx

Reverse order, and the parentheses matter

In F = (9/5)C + 32, first remove the “+ 32” (subtract), then the “× 9/5” (multiply by the reciprocal). It is the same reverse order as two-step equations. And 59(F − 32) is not the same as 59F − 32: first subtract 32 from the whole F, then multiply the whole result.

5Using the solved formula

Once it is solved, the formula lets you work out the variable you want by substituting the known values.

Example 6: a car traveled d = 120 miles at a rate of r = 40 miles per hour. How long did it take?

1Use the solved formula. t = dr.

2Substitute. t = 12040 = 3.

✓Answer. The car took 3 hours.

A link to science

The same idea works with F = ma (force = mass × acceleration). To find the acceleration a, solve for a = F/m. Solving formulas is a general tool that appears in geometry, money, and science.

6Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. Solve a given formula for a variable six times, and use a solved formula four times in a problem with numbers. Write each answer with its units.
  • In your daily life. Plan a trip: choose a city you would like to drive to, look up the distance in miles, and decide on an average speed. Solve d = rt for t and find how many hours the trip would take. Then check the weather forecast there in °F and convert it to °C with C = (5/9)(F − 32).

📘Key words

literal formula
A formula that relates several amounts with letters, like d = rt.
solve for
To rewrite a formula so that one variable is alone on one side.
reciprocal
The fraction turned upside down: the reciprocal of 9/5 is 5/9. Multiplying by it undoes the 9/5.
simple interest
I = Prt: the principal P times the rate r times the time t in years.
