Lesson 6.1 · Angles: relationships and parallel lines cut by a transversal

Recognize how angles are related, use parallel lines and a transversal to find unknown angles, and use the 180° of a triangle.

⏱️ About 4 hours with practice and homework · take breaks 📐 Angles, parallel lines, and triangles

🎯What you will learn

When you finish this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Identify complementary angles (they add up to 90°), supplementary angles (they add up to 180°), and vertical angles (they are equal).
  • Recognize the angles formed by two parallel lines cut by a transversal: corresponding, alternate interior, alternate exterior, and same-side interior.
  • Use these relationships to find unknown angles.
  • Use the fact that the angles of a triangle add up to 180°.
  • Write simple equations to find an unknown angle.

1Basic angle relationships

With Topic 5, the linear algebra of Level 3 is complete. Topic 6 turns to geometry, but algebra does not go away: many angle problems are solved by writing and solving a simple equation, exactly what you learned in Topics 4 and 5. This lesson studies how angles are related, especially the angles formed when a line called a transversal cuts two parallel lines.

Three relationships come up again and again in geometry:

Example 1: an angle measures 35°. What is the measure of its complement?

1Complementary angles add up to 90°. 90° − 35° = 55°.

✓Answer. The complement measures 55°.

Which is which?

“Complementary” and “corner” (90°) both start with c; “supplementary” and “straight” (180°) both start with s. An angle of 90° or more has no complement.

2Parallel lines cut by a transversal

When a transversal cuts two parallel lines, eight angles are formed. There are only two measures: all the acute angles are equal, all the obtuse angles are equal, and an acute angle plus an obtuse angle add up to 180°.

3Finding unknown angles

If you know one of these relationships, you can find any unknown angle. Sometimes it helps to write an equation, like the ones in Topic 4: “they are equal” gives an equation with =, and “they add up to 180°” or “to 90°” gives a sum.

Example 2: with a transversal. In the diagram, angle 1 measures 70°. What are the measures of angle 5 and angle 3?

1Angle 5 corresponds to angle 1. So it measures 70°.

2Angle 3 and angle 1 form a straight line. They are supplementary: 180° − 70° = 110°.

✓Answer. Angle 5 = 70° and angle 3 = 110°.

Example 3: with an equation. Two supplementary angles measure x and 2x. What is the value of x?

1They add up to 180°. x + 2x = 180.

2Combine and divide by 3. 3x = 180, x = 60.

✓Answer. x = 60: the angles measure 60° and 120°.

4The angles of a triangle

A basic property: the three interior angles of any triangle add up to 180°. If you know two angles, you can always find the third one.

angle A + angle B + angle C = 180°

Example 4: a triangle has angles of 50° and 60°. What is the measure of the third angle?

1The three angles add up to 180°. 50° + 60° + C = 180°.

2Add the known angles. 110° + C = 180°, so C = 70°.

✓Answer. The third angle measures 70°.

The right triangle

If a triangle has a 90° angle, the other two angles must add up to 90° (because the total is 180°). So in a right triangle, the two angles that are not right angles are always complementary.

5Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. Solve three exercises on complementary and supplementary angles, four on finding angles with a transversal and parallel lines, and three on finding the third angle of a triangle. Then practice in Explore: angles, parallel lines, and a transversal until you can tell at a glance whether two angles are corresponding, alternate interior, or same-side interior.
  • In your daily life. Look for parallel lines cut by a transversal around you: a ladder with its rungs, the lines of a parking lot, railroad tracks crossing a street, or a window with a diagonal brace. Sketch one, number its eight angles, and mark which ones are equal and which ones add up to 180°.

📘Key words

complementary angles
Two angles that add up to 90°.
supplementary angles
Two angles that add up to 180°.
vertical angles
The angles across from each other where two lines cross. They are equal.
transversal
A line that cuts two or more lines.
corresponding angles
Angles in the same position at each intersection with the transversal.
alternate interior angles
Angles between the parallel lines, on opposite sides of the transversal.
