Lesson 7.2 · Box plots

Summarize a data set with five key values, draw a box plot, find the IQR and the outliers, and compare two groups.

1The five key values

Lesson 7.1 measured how much fits inside a solid. This lesson turns to data: how to summarize a set of numbers so you can see it at a glance. A box plot shows, in a single picture, where the data are concentrated, how spread out they are, and whether there are unusual values.

A box plot is built from five numbers that summarize the set: the minimum, the first quartile Q1, the median (Q2), the third quartile Q3, and the maximum. The box goes from Q1 to Q3, with a line at the median, and the whiskers reach the minimum and the maximum.

Each part holds one quarter

The quartiles split the data into four groups with 25% of the data each. The lowest 25% is between the minimum and Q1; the box (from Q1 to Q3) holds the middle 50% of the data.

2Building the plot

The procedure has five steps in order.

Five steps to build a box plot
StepWhat to do
1Put the data in order from least to greatest.
2Find the median (the middle value).
3Find Q1 (the median of the lower half) and Q3 (the median of the upper half).
4Draw the box from Q1 to Q3, with a line at the median.
5Draw the whiskers out to the minimum and the maximum.

Example 1: for the data 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, what is the median?

1They are already in order. There are five values, so the middle one is the third.

✓Answer. The median is 6.

Odd or even number of data

With an odd number of data, the median is the middle value, and it does not go into either half. With an even number, the median is the average of the two middle values, and each half keeps half of the data.

3The interquartile range (IQR)

The interquartile range (IQR) measures how spread out the middle half of the data is. Find it by subtracting:

IQR = Q3 − Q1

Example 2: in the plot above, Q1 = 25 and Q3 = 55. What is the IQR?

1Subtract. 55 − 25 = 30.

✓Answer. The IQR is 30. It is the width of the box.

The IQR resists extreme values

Unlike the range (max − min), the IQR is not affected by one or two very large or very small values, because it only looks at the middle 50%. That is why it is a reliable measure of spread.

4Outliers

An outlier is a data value that is far away from the rest. The most common rule to find outliers uses the IQR: a value is an outlier if it is more than 1.5 × IQR above Q3 or below Q1.

lower limit = Q1 − 1.5 × IQR     upper limit = Q3 + 1.5 × IQR

Example 3: the upper limit. With Q3 = 55 and IQR = 30, what is the upper limit for outliers?

1Find 1.5 × IQR. 1.5 × 30 = 45.

2Add it to Q3. 55 + 45 = 100.

✓Answer. Any value greater than 100 would be an outlier on the high side.

5Reading and comparing

The real value of a box plot is in reading it. A wide box means the data are spread out; a narrow box means the data are close together. Comparing two plots on the same scale shows which group has the higher median or more variability.

Example 4: comparing two groups. Group A has a median of 70 and a narrow box; group B has a median of 60 and a wide box. What can you conclude?

1Compare the medians. A's median (70) is higher than B's (60): A tends to have higher values.

2Compare the boxes. B's box is wider: B has more variability (a larger IQR).

✓Answer. A has higher, more consistent values; B is more spread out.

Median is not mean

The line in the box is the median. A box plot does not show the mean (the average).

📘Key words

quartiles
The values that split ordered data into four parts with 25% each: Q1, the median (Q2), and Q3.
median
The middle value of ordered data, or the average of the two middle values.
interquartile range (IQR)
Q3 − Q1: the spread of the middle 50% of the data.
range
Maximum − minimum.
outlier
A value more than 1.5 × IQR below Q1 or above Q3.
