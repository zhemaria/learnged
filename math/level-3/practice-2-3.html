Adding with equal exponents

›

You can only add amounts directly when they have the same power of 10, just as you only add things of the same kind: 4 hundreds plus 5 hundreds are 9 hundreds. Add the coefficients and the power stays the same.

(a × 10n) + (b × 10n) = (a + b) × 10n

Check it with ordinary addition

Choose the coefficients and the exponent. The column addition shows that the power does not change, and the box below shows what the mistake of adding the exponents too would give.

The power does not change: 3 × 104 + 2 × 104 is 5 × 104, never 5 × 108. And if the sum of the coefficients is 10 or more, adjust it as in the last lesson: 14 × 105 = 1.4 × 106.

2

Making the exponents equal

›

If the powers of 10 do not match, first rewrite one of the amounts so both have the same exponent. Then add or subtract the coefficients and, at the end, adjust if needed.

Two ways, the same answer

Try both ways of matching. Both give the same answer; notice which one saves you the final adjustment.

Why match the larger exponent: the term you rewrite gets a coefficient less than 1 (like 0.3), but the sum almost always lands right in valid form, with no extra adjustment.

Practice: match, then add

Step 2: write the final answer in valid scientific notation.

× 10

Use a decimal point (4.5). For a negative exponent, type the minus sign: −3 or -3.

Subtracting in scientific notation

›

Subtraction follows the same idea: with equal exponents, subtract the coefficients; if they are different, match them first. Watch for one case: when the two amounts are close, the difference can be less than 1 and needs a new adjustment.

(a × 10n) − (b × 10n) = (a − b) × 10n

Subtraction in columns

Type two amounts, with the same exponent or different ones, and compare ordinary subtraction with subtraction in scientific notation.

Science and technology applications

›

Here the four operations are mixed in real settings: space, cells, and technology. Before you calculate, decide what the problem asks for.

Hints for choosing the operation
If the question says…It is usually
“in all,” “are added,” “together”an addition
“is left,” “how much more,” “difference”a subtraction
“each hour for…,” “for each one, how many in…”a multiplication
“how many fit,” “per,” “share”a division
