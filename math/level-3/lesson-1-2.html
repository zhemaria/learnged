Math · Level 3 · Topic 1 — Rules of exponents

Lesson 1.2 · The quotient rule, zero exponents, and negative exponents

Dividing powers: the exponents are subtracted. And from that rule come a0 = 1 and negative exponents.

1The quotient rule

In Lesson 1.1 you learned that when you multiply powers with the same base, the exponents are added. This lesson takes on the opposite operation, division, where the exponents are subtracted. Followed all the way, that rule also explains two surprising ideas: the zero exponent and negative exponents.

When you divide two powers with the same base, each factor on the bottom cancels a factor on the top. Write it out to see it:

a5a2a5/a2 = a · a · a · a · aa · aa · a · a · a · a/a · a = a3

Five factors on top minus two on the bottom leave three factors. The exponent of the answer is the difference of the exponents, always top minus bottom:

am ÷ an = am − n (a ≠ 0)

Example 1: simplify x9 ÷ x4

1Same base. Both have base x: the quotient rule applies.

2Subtract. 9 − 4 = 5.

✓Answer. x9 ÷ x4 = x5

Example 2: simplify 106 ÷ 104

1Same base. The base is 10: 6 − 4 = 2.

✓Answer. 106 ÷ 104 = 102 = 100

Watch the order

Always subtract the top exponent minus the bottom exponent, never the other way around. The base cannot be zero, because dividing by zero is not defined.

2The zero exponent

What happens when you divide a power by itself? On one hand, any number other than zero divided by itself is 1. On the other hand, the quotient rule gives a zero exponent:

a3 ÷ a3 = 1 and also a3 ÷ a3 = a3 − 3 = a0

Both describe the same division, so they must be equal:

a0 = 1 (a ≠ 0)

A pattern of shrinking powers gives the same answer: each time the exponent goes down by one, the value is divided by the base.

Powers of 2 going down: 8, 4, 2, 1
23222120
8421

8 ÷ 2 = 4, 4 ÷ 2 = 2, and 2 ÷ 2 = 1. Dividing by the base once more leads naturally to 20 = 1. This works for any base other than zero: 50 = 1, 1000 = 1, (3x)0 = 1.

The zero affects only its own base

(3x)0 = 1, because the zero reaches everything in the parentheses. But 3x0 = 3 · 1 = 3, because the zero reaches only the x. In the same way, −50 = −1, while (−5)0 = 1.

3Negative exponents

If the pattern keeps going past zero, each step still divides by the base, and fractions appear:

Powers of 2 going below zero: 2, 1, 1/2, 1/4, 1/8
21202−12−22−3
211/21/41/8

A negative exponent means the reciprocal of the power with a positive exponent:

a−n = 1an1/an (a ≠ 0)

Example 3: write 2−3 as a fraction

1Reciprocal. 2−3 = 1 ÷ 23.

2Power. 23 = 8.

✓Answer. 2−3 = 1/8 = 0.125

Example 4: rewrite x−4 with a positive exponent

1Definition. A negative exponent means the reciprocal.

✓Answer. x−4 = 1/x4

Crossing the fraction bar

Moving a factor from the top of a fraction to the bottom, or from the bottom to the top, changes the sign of its exponent. So 1 ÷ a−2 = a2: when the factor moves up, the exponent goes from negative to positive.

A negative exponent does not make the number negative

2−3 is not −8 and not −6: it is the positive fraction 1/8. The sign of the exponent only means “reciprocal,” never the sign of the answer.

5Checking with the calculator and with numbers

Negative exponents show up when you measure very small amounts, an idea you will use again in scientific notation in Topic 2. The calculator lets you check any answer.

Example 5: check that 5−2 = 1/25

1By hand. 5−2 = 1 ÷ 52 = 1 ÷ 25 = 0.04.

2On the calculator. 5 ^ (−) 2 enter shows 0.04.

✓Answer. 5−2 = 1/25 = 0.04

🧮 The negative sign of an exponent

Type the minus sign of the exponent with the (−) key, not with the subtraction key −. Before you start, clear the screen with clear so old values are not carried along.

Check with a number

A correct simplification gives the same number as the original expression for any value of the letter. Replace x with 2 or 3 in both: if the values are different, there is a mistake. Do not use 0 (there may be a division by zero) or 1 (every power of 1 is 1, so the test tells you nothing).

6Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. Write and simplify six quotients of powers with the same base (two of them with a negative exponent in the answer); four expressions with a zero exponent, including one like (−5)0 and one like −50, with a sentence explaining why they are not equal; and six expressions with negative exponents rewritten as fractions. Then check four of your answers on the calculator.
  • In your daily life. Halving shows negative powers of 2: cut a sheet of paper in half, then in half again, three times. Each piece is 2−1, then 2−2, then 2−3 = 1/8 of the sheet. Count the pieces to check.

📘Key words

quotient
The answer to a division.
reciprocal
The number you multiply by to get 1. The reciprocal of 8 is 1/8.
zero exponent
a0 = 1 for any base other than zero.
negative exponent
It means the reciprocal: a−n = 1/an. It does not make the number negative.
