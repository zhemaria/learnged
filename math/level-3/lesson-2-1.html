Lesson 2.1 · Scientific notation and orders of magnitude

1What is scientific notation?

Topic 1 gave you the rules of exponents, including powers of 10 and negative exponents. Scientific notation is the most useful application of those rules: it uses powers of 10 (a positive exponent for large numbers and a negative one for small numbers) to write, in a short form, amounts as huge as the mass of the Sun or as tiny as the mass of an electron.

Scientific notation writes any number as the product of two parts: a coefficient and a power of 10.

a × 10n with 1 ≤ a < 10

The coefficient a is always at least 1 and less than 10: one digit, not zero, before the decimal point. The exponent n tells how many places the decimal point moved, and in which direction.

What it is for

Writing 5,970,000,000,000,000,000,000,000 is easy to get wrong. In scientific notation it is 5.97 × 1024 (the mass of the Earth in kilograms). Scientific notation makes numbers that would be impossible to read or compare easy to handle.

2From a decimal to scientific notation: large numbers

For a large number, move the decimal point to the left until only one digit (1 to 9) is left before the point. The number of places you moved it is the positive exponent of 10.

Example 1: write 4,500,000 in scientific notation

1Coefficient. Put the point after the first digit that is not zero: 4.5.

2Count. The point moved 6 places to the left (from the end of 4500000).

3Exponent. Large number: the exponent is +6.

✓Answer. 4,500,000 = 4.5 × 106

The coefficient rules

45 × 105 uses a power of 10, but it is not valid scientific notation, because 45 is not between 1 and 10. The right form is 4.5 × 106.

3From a decimal to scientific notation: small numbers

For a small number (less than 1), move the decimal point to the right until it passes the first digit that is not zero. The number of places you moved it is the negative exponent of 10.

Example 2: write 0.00032 in scientific notation

1Coefficient. Move the point to the right until it passes the 3: 3.2.

2Count. 4 places to the right.

3Exponent. Small number: the exponent is negative, −4.

✓Answer. 0.00032 = 3.2 × 10−4

The direction rule

A number 10 or larger → a positive exponent. A number less than 1 → a negative exponent. The sign of the exponent tells you right away whether the amount is huge or tiny.

4From scientific notation to a decimal

To go back, the exponent tells you which way the point moves and how many places. Positive exponent: the point moves to the right (the number grows). Negative exponent: the point moves to the left (the number gets smaller). Fill in with zeros as needed.

Example 3: write 3.7 × 105 as a decimal

1Direction. The exponent +5 says: move the point 5 places to the right.

2Fill in. 3.7 → 370000, adding the zeros you need.

✓Answer. 3.7 × 105 = 370,000

Example 4: write 5.6 × 10−3 as a decimal

1Direction. The exponent −3 says: move the point 3 places to the left.

2Fill in. 5.6 → 0.0056, adding zeros on the left.

✓Answer. 5.6 × 10−3 = 0.0056

To type 3.7 × 105, use the ×10n key: 3 . 7 ×10n 5 enter. The screen shows 370000. For a negative exponent, use (−): 5 . 6 ×10n (−) 3 enter shows 0.0056.

5Order of magnitude and comparing

The exponent of 10 is the order of magnitude: it tells the “size” of an amount. To compare two numbers in scientific notation, look at the exponent first. Only if the exponents are equal do you compare the coefficients.

AmountScientific notationOrder of magnitude
Mass of the Sun1.989 × 1030 kg1030
Distance from the Earth to the Sun1.496 × 1011 m1011
Mass of an electron9.109 × 10−31 kg10−31

Example 5: which is larger, 3 × 108 or 9 × 107?

1Exponents first. 8 > 7.

2Decide. The number with the larger exponent is larger, whatever the coefficient.

✓Answer. 3 × 108 is larger than 9 × 107.

Do not be fooled by the coefficient

Even though 9 is larger than 3, the exponent 8 beats the 7. In scientific notation, the order of magnitude (the exponent) decides first. Each step up in the exponent multiplies the amount by 10.

6Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. Do four conversions from a decimal to scientific notation (two large numbers and two small ones), four from scientific notation to a decimal (two with a positive exponent and two with a negative one), and two exercises comparing and ordering amounts in scientific notation. Scientific notation is place value in different clothes: if moving the point and counting places comes easily, the conversion comes easily too.
  • In your daily life. Find two large numbers and two small ones in the news, on a food label, or on a medicine box (for example, a population, a budget, or a dose in grams). Write each one in scientific notation and say its order of magnitude.

📘Key words

scientific notation
A number written as a × 10n, with a from 1 up to, but not including, 10.
coefficient
The first part, a. It has one digit, not zero, before the decimal point.
order of magnitude
The exponent of 10. It tells the size of an amount.
standard form (decimal)
The usual way of writing a number, like 370,000 or 0.0056.
