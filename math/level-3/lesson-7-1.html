Lesson 7.1 · Volume of prisms and cylinders

Find the volume of boxes, prisms, and cylinders with V = area of the base × height, solve for a missing measure, and solve real problems about capacity.

When you finish this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Find the volume of rectangular and triangular prisms with V = area of the base × height.
  • Find the volume of cylinders with V = πr²h.
  • Identify the base and the height of each solid.
  • Solve for the height or the radius when the volume is known.
  • Give the answer in the right cubic units (in³, ft³) or in gallons.

1What is volume?

Topic 6 closed the geometry of flat figures (two dimensions). Topic 7 takes the step to three dimensions: volume measures how much space a solid takes up, or how much fits inside it. To find it, you use again the formulas and the solving of Topic 5, and the area of the base that you already worked with in earlier levels.

Volume is the amount of space that a three-dimensional object takes up. For a prism or a cylinder, it is found with one powerful idea: the area of the base times the height.

V = area of the base × height

Cubic units

Volume multiplies three lengths, so the answer is in cubic units: cubic inches (in³), cubic feet (ft³), and so on. Two useful facts: 1 cubic foot = 1,728 cubic inches (12 × 12 × 12), and 1 cubic foot holds about 7.48 gallons.

2Volume of prisms

In a rectangular prism (a box), the base is a rectangle, so V = length × width × height. In a triangular prism, the base is a triangle: first find the area of the triangle, then multiply it by the height (length) of the prism.

Example 1: rectangular prism. A box is 5 inches long, 3 inches wide, and 4 inches high. What is its volume?

1Multiply the three dimensions. 5 × 3 × 4 = 60.

✓Answer. V = 60 in³.

Example 2: triangular prism. A prism has a triangular base with an area of 6 square inches and a height of 10 inches. What is its volume?

1Use V = area of the base × height. 6 × 10 = 60.

✓Answer. V = 60 in³.

3Volume of cylinders

In a cylinder, the base is a circle with radius r, and its area is πr². So the volume is that area times the height:

V = πr²h

In the calculations, use π ≈ 3.14 (or the π key on the calculator). The formula is on the GED reference sheet; open it with the Reference sheet button.

🧮 About the calculator

The GED test uses the TI-30XS MultiView calculator. The calculator on this page does the same operations, although some keys have other names or are in other places. If you have a TI-30XS, practice with it too so you learn where its keys are.

Example 3: a cylinder has a radius of 2 inches and a height of 5 inches. What is its volume? (π ≈ 3.14)

1Find the area of the base. πr² = 3.14 × 2² = 3.14 × 4 = 12.56.

2Multiply by the height. 12.56 × 5 = 62.8.

✓Answer. V ≈ 62.8 in³.

Square only the radius

In πr²h, the exponent applies only to the radius (r²), not to π or the height. A common mistake is to square the whole product. First r², then multiply by π and by h. And if the problem gives the diameter, divide it by 2 first to get the radius.

4Solving for the height or the radius

When you know the volume and one measure is missing, solve for it as in Topic 5. For the height of a prism, divide the volume by the area of the base.

Example 4: solving for the height. A prism has a volume of 60 cubic inches and a base with an area of 12 square inches. What is its height?

1Start from V = area of the base × height. 60 = 12 × h.

2Divide by 12. h = 60 ÷ 12 = 5.

✓Answer. The height is 5 inches.

The radius of a cylinder

To find the radius when you know the volume and the height, divide the volume by πh to get r², then take the square root: the same steps as the Pythagorean theorem in Lesson 6.2.

5Uses: capacity and storage

Volume answers real questions about building and storage: how many gallons fit in a tank, how much concrete a column needs, how much fits in a box.

Example 5: a cylindrical tank has a radius of 2 feet and a height of 5 feet. How many cubic feet of water fit? About how many gallons is that? (π ≈ 3.14)

1Area of the base. 3.14 × 2² = 3.14 × 4 = 12.56.

2Times the height. 12.56 × 5 = 62.8 ft³.

3Convert to gallons. 62.8 × 7.48 ≈ 470 gallons.

✓Answer. About 62.8 ft³, or about 470 gallons.

Use the same unit for every measure

Before you calculate, make sure every measure is in the same unit (all in feet, or all in inches). If the radius is in inches and the height is in feet, convert first to a single unit.

6Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. Solve four exercises on the volume of prisms (rectangular and triangular), four on the volume of cylinders (with π ≈ 3.14), and two that solve for the height or the radius. Then check your answers with the calculator's π key: the difference shows how much you rounded.
  • In your daily life. Find a box and a can at home. Measure the box in inches and find its volume. Measure the diameter and the height of the can, find its volume with V = πr²h, and divide by 231 to get gallons (1 gallon = 231 cubic inches). Compare it with the amount printed on the label.

📘Key words

volume
The amount of space a solid takes up. It is measured in cubic units.
prism
A solid with two equal, parallel bases joined by flat faces, like a box.
cylinder
A solid with two equal, parallel circular bases, like a can.
radius
The distance from the center of a circle to its edge: half the diameter.
cubic unit
A cube that measures 1 unit on each side, like 1 in³ or 1 ft³.
