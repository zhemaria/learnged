From a decimal to scientific notation: large numbers

›

A number in scientific notation has two parts: a coefficient that is at least 1 and less than 10, and a power of 10.

a × 10n with 1 ≤ a < 10

For a large number, the decimal point moves to the left until one digit, not zero, is left before the point. The places it moved are the exponent, and it is positive.

The point's trip

Move the point one place at a time and watch the coefficient and the exponent change. The value never changes: what the coefficient loses, the power of 10 gains.

a place the point has already jumped
The coefficient rules: 45 × 105 has the same value as 4,500,000, but it is not valid scientific notation, because 45 is not between 1 and 10. The right form is 4.5 × 106.

From a decimal to scientific notation: small numbers

›

For a number less than 1, the point moves to the right until it passes the first digit that is not zero. The places it moved are the exponent, and now it is negative.

The point's trip to the right

The zeros the point leaves behind are exactly the ones the negative exponent “keeps.”

a place the point has already jumped
The direction rule: a number 10 or larger → positive exponent; a number less than 1 → negative exponent. The sign of the exponent tells you right away whether the amount is huge or tiny.

Practice: small numbers

Write it yourself: large and small numbers mixed

× 10

Use a decimal point (4.5). For a negative exponent, type the minus sign: −3 or -3.

Order of magnitude and comparing

›

The exponent of 10 is the order of magnitude: it tells the “size” of an amount. To compare two numbers in scientific notation, look at the exponent first; only if the exponents are equal do you compare the coefficients.

Put them in order from least to greatest

These are real data from the universe, all in the same unit. Use the ↑ and ↓ buttons to put them in order, then check.

    Do not be fooled by the coefficient: 9 × 107 is less than 3 × 108, even though 9 is larger than 3. The exponent 8 beats the 7, and that decides.

    How many times larger?

    Each step up in the exponent multiplies the amount by 10. If two numbers have the same coefficient, the difference of the exponents tells how many times one fits in the other: 108 is 108 − 5 = 103 = 1,000 times 105.
