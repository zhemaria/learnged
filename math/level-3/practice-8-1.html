The counting principle

›

If one decision can be made in m ways and another in n ways, the two together can be made in m × n ways. With more decisions, keep multiplying the choices for each one.

The growing tree

Put together a menu. Each branch of the tree is a choice; the leaves at the end are all the possible meals.

Practice: how many possibilities are there?

The factorial

›

The factorial of a number, n!, is the product of all the whole numbers from n down to 1. By agreement, 0! = 1. It grows very fast, so simplify quotients before you multiply.

n! = n × (n − 1) × … × 2 × 1

Permutations: order matters

›

A permutation counts how many ways you can put in order r items chosen from n. Use it when the order changes the result: a podium, a code, an order of arrival.

P(n, r) = n!(n − r)! = n × (n − 1) × … (r factors)

Filling the places

There are n people and r places. Anyone can go in the first place; in the second, one person fewer; and so on.

Combinations: order does not matter

›

A combination counts how many ways you can choose a group when order does not matter: a committee, a team, a hand of cards. It is the permutation divided by r!, because each group shows up r! times among the permutations, once for each order.

C(n, r) = n!r! (n − r)! = P(n, r)r!

From order to group

With small numbers you can see them all: each row is the different orders of one group.
