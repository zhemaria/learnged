1Adding: match the exponents first

In Lesson 2.2 you multiplied and divided by working the coefficients and the powers of 10 separately: the exponents were added or subtracted. Adding and subtracting work differently. Here the exponents do not change when you calculate; what matters is that they are equal before you start. Making the exponents equal is the step that organizes this whole lesson.

You can only add amounts directly when they have the same power of 10, just as you only add things of the same kind: 4 hundreds plus 5 hundreds are 9 hundreds. Add the coefficients and keep the power of 10.

(a × 10n) + (b × 10n) = (a + b) × 10n

Example 1: find 3 × 104 + 2 × 104

1Same exponent. Both have 104.

2Add the coefficients. 3 + 2 = 5.

3Keep the power. 104.

✓Answer. 5 × 104

The power does not change

When you add, the exponents of 10 are not added. 3 × 104 + 2 × 104 is 5 × 104, never 5 × 108. And if the sum of the coefficients is 10 or more, adjust it as in the last lesson: 14 × 105 = 1.4 × 106.

2When the exponents are different

If the powers of 10 do not match, first rewrite one of the amounts so both have the same exponent. It is best to match the larger one. Then add the coefficients.

Example 2: find 4 × 105 + 3 × 104

1Match. The exponents are 5 and 4. Raise the smaller one: 3 × 104 = 0.3 × 105.

2Same power. 4 × 105 + 0.3 × 105.

3Add. 4 + 0.3 = 4.3.

✓Answer. 4.3 × 105

Why match the larger exponent

When you raise the smaller exponent to the larger one, the term you rewrite gets a coefficient less than 1 (like 0.3), but the sum almost always lands right in valid form, with no extra adjustment.

3Subtracting in scientific notation

Subtraction follows exactly the same idea: if the exponents are equal, subtract the coefficients; if they are different, match them first.

(a × 10n) − (b × 10n) = (a − b) × 10n

Example 3: find 9 × 106 − 4 × 106

1Same exponent. 106.

2Subtract the coefficients. 9 − 4 = 5.

✓Answer. 5 × 106

Example 4: find 7 × 104 − 2 × 103

1Match. 2 × 103 = 0.2 × 104.

2Same power. 7 × 104 − 0.2 × 104.

3Subtract. 7 − 0.2 = 6.8.

✓Answer. 6.8 × 104

When the amounts are close

If the two amounts are close, the difference can come out less than 1 and needs a new adjustment: 1.2 × 105 − 9 × 104 = 1.2 × 105 − 0.9 × 105 = 0.3 × 105 = 3 × 104. And two equal amounts give 0.

4Science and technology applications

Scientific notation is the natural language of science. These examples use the four operations in real settings: space, cells, and technology.

Hints for choosing the operation from the words of the problem
If the question says…It is usually
“in all,” “are added,” “together”an addition
“is left,” “how much more,” “difference”a subtraction
“each hour for…,” “for each one, how many in…”a multiplication
“how many fit,” “per,” “share”a division

Example 5 · Space: a probe travels 4 × 104 km each hour. How far does it go in 6 × 102 hours?

1Operation. A multiplication: (4 × 104)(6 × 102).

2Calculate. 4 × 6 = 24; 4 + 2 = 6. Partial answer: 24 × 106.

3Adjust. 24 × 106 = 2.4 × 107.

✓Answer. 2.4 × 107 km

Example 6 · Cells: a blood sample has 5 × 106 red blood cells, and 3 × 105 more are added. How many are there in all?

1Operation and match. An addition with different exponents: 3 × 105 = 0.3 × 106.

2Add. 5 + 0.3 = 5.3.

✓Answer. 5.3 × 106 red blood cells

Example 7 · Technology: a drive holds 8 × 1012 bytes, and each file takes up 2 × 106 bytes. How many files fit?

1Operation. A division: (8 × 1012) ÷ (2 × 106).

2Calculate. 8 ÷ 2 = 4; 12 − 6 = 6.

✓Answer. 4 × 106 files

🧮 Check on the calculator

Type the amounts with the ×10n key and keep the order of operations: 5 ×10n 6 + 3 ×10n 5 enter shows 5300000, that is, 5.3 × 106. The calculator matches the exponents on its own, but you still have to read the answer in valid scientific notation. Use (−) for a negative exponent, not the subtraction key.

5Homework

This is independent practice. Plan for about one hour, with your notebook open.

  • The practice page. Open the practice page for this lesson and do each activity. The exercises change every time. Do at least 10 in each activity, and keep going until you get 8 out of 10 right on the first try. The score under each activity keeps count for you.
  • Paper and pencil. Do three additions and three subtractions in scientific notation (at least two of each with different exponents), and four mixed problems (space, cells, or technology) that use the four operations. Once the exponents are equal, adding in scientific notation is just adding decimals, and that is where careless points are lost.
  • In your daily life. Look up two large amounts of the same kind in the news (for example, the populations of two states) and write them in scientific notation. Find how many more people one has than the other, and the total, in scientific notation.

📘Key words

make the exponents equal
Rewrite one amount so both have the same power of 10 before you add or subtract.
common power
The power of 10 that two amounts share. It stays the same in the answer when you add or subtract.
difference
The answer to a subtraction.
unit
What an amount is measured in: kilometers, bytes, cells. It goes with the answer.
